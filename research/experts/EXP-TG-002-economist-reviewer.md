## What they shared

Written answers to our review dossier, 21 November 2026. He declined to be cited, so do not
name him or his thesis anywhere. Refer to him as "our economist reviewer". His figures are
expert opinion (tier B); the sources behind them are not given.

- **Horizon and discounting (G1):** a 10-year horizon. Discount future costs and benefits at
  about 9-10%, the long-term lending rate. He suggested that over this horizon new power
  plants are built to close the energy deficit, together with awareness campaigns for
  farmers to ration pumping. A fall in investment (such as the 87% fall at STEG) is not an
  environmental gain; environmental quality improves with clean energy such as renewables.
- **Rebound (G2):** about 35% for air conditioning and about 50% for solar pumping. Direct
  rebound shows as more hours of use. Backfire (total use rising) is plausible for solar
  pumping because the energy cost is low.
- **Who pays (G3):** with electricity sold below cost, a tariff rise lowers consumption.
  Well-off households would benefit most from rewards for reports and peak reduction.
- **Do not move the problem (G4):** compare effects with a multi-criteria analysis. Use a
  penalty, not exclusion, for a solution that degrades a critical resource.
- **Cause and effect (G5):** use time series to test a cause when data are limited. The
  heatwave versus under-investment debate contains a contradiction.
- **Data (G6):** require a source. Use INS for economic data and ANME for environmental data.
- **Humans (G7):** strategic investment decisions should never be given to an AI. Always
  verify what the AI presents.
- **For the jury demo:** keep guardrails 1, 5 and 6.
- He left blank: indicators of dynamic environmental effect, distribution indicators, the
  value of non-renewable groundwater, whether to add or merge guardrails, and where our
  reasoning is weakest.
