## What it is

The PNEEI, launched in 1995, pays part of the cost of on-farm water-saving equipment (drip, sprinkler, improved gravity). It is run by the Ministry of Agriculture and financed through the agricultural investment incentives (APIA). Reported equipped share rose from 37% in 1995 to 94.5% in 2021 (SRC-TG-403, p. 86) and 96.9% (428,500 of 442,500 ha) in 2024 (SRC-TG-530, p. 58). In the 2022 report, localised (drip) irrigation covered 211,000 ha, sprinkler 114,800 ha and improved gravity 95,200 ha, 421,000 ha or 95.2% in total (SRC-TG-528, p. 80); the 2023 report gives 241,000, 114,700 and 95,800 ha (SRC-TG-529). Investment from 1995 to 2021 was 1,672 MTND including 759 MTND of subsidies, and 1,692 MTND with 768 MTND by 2023 (SRC-TG-528, SRC-TG-529).

The brief's "up to 60%" subsidy rate is not confirmed. FND-TG-525 only documents 40% to 50% and 50% to 55% under the 2016 Investment Code. The 40-60% range appears in a search result I did not open.

## What it claims, and what the evidence shows

- **Coverage is real but is not a water indicator.** The programme indicator is the share of area equipped (SRC-TG-529, p. 53; FND-TG-525). The ministry's own PNEEI evaluation, cited in SRC-TG-402, was not found online, so no net saving is measured.
- **Plot-level savings, regional rebound.** In Central Tunisia, simulated drip adoption "showed some savings in water", which "would consequently enable farmers to extend the area of drip-irrigated land", and then "no savings in water at the regional scale"; the abstract says 2005 data confirmed this (SRC-TG-531). This is one region (Central Tunisia; demand estimated for 1999, abstract only).
- **Comparable countries.** In Morocco, researchers link drip subsidies to denser planting, more water-intensive crops and expansion with "saved" water, with higher consumption as the result (SRC-TG-940, abstract only). A Science policy forum concludes that efficiency gains rarely reduce consumption unless paired with accounting, measurement and a cap on extractions (SRC-TG-941). In Spain, a 2019 review of 2002-2015 modernisation says certain unfavourable effects need discussion (SRC-TG-942, abstract). Its detail on a heterogeneous rebound, area limits and energy use is unconfirmed: it rests on a search summary that was not an original.
- **Basin-level result in Tunisia.** No source reviewed shows withdrawals falling after PNEEI. The 2018 irrigation figure (2.4 billion m3 for 406,540 ha, 84% groundwater, SRC-TG-504 via FND-TG-525) cannot be compared with older series. Deep-aquifer exploitation rose from 82% to 134% over 1996-2020 (SRC-TG-403); that is correlation, not proof of rebound.

## Audit

**Root-cause coverage: 1.** The overdraft root causes in FND-TG-410 and FND-TG-525 are uncapped, unmetered groundwater use and illicit drilling. PNEEI reduces water per hectare but leaves access untouched, so it addresses a symptom. It earns 1 because per-hectare efficiency is real on the plot.

**Evidence quality: 1.** Coverage figures are tier A ministry reports, but they measure equipment. The rebound evidence is one regional model abstract (SRC-TG-531) and a 20-farm survey (SRC-TG-532). Foreign studies were read in abstract only. No net-saving evaluation was found.

**Adoption: 3.** Equipment on 96.9% of irrigated area (SRC-TG-530). Adoption is nearly complete.

**Side effects: 1.** The likely side effect is more groundwater use through area expansion and intensification. Evidence is local (SRC-TG-531, SRC-TG-532) and foreign (SRC-TG-940), not national. Kept at 1 rather than 0 because the national effect is not shown.

**Cost and accessibility: 1.** 768 MTND of subsidies to 2023 (SRC-TG-529). A farmer must pre-finance to claim a subsidy; no source opened shows how take-up or amounts differ between small and large farms, so access cannot be scored higher. This is a judgement, flagged as such.

## Limits and blockers

See the frontmatter lists. The legal text of the incentives (Investment Code 2016 subsidy classes, the 1995 decision) was not opened. The caps and conditions of the ANME solar scheme were reformed in 2017; the effect was not measured (SRC-TG-532, notes).

Weak metering and illicit drilling make any volume-based condition hard to enforce. In SRC-TG-402, CRDA reports "n'aboutissent pas toujours" because of form defects, and boreholes covered by reports were not plugged.

## Criticisms

Listed in the frontmatter. All four are rated partly-supported: each rests on an abstract, a small sample or a different country. Molle co-authored SRC-TG-940 and SRC-TG-941, so these are not two independent confirmations. The Tunisian rebound studies, SRC-TG-531 and SRC-TG-532, cover groundwater-irrigated private farms in the centre, the segment where area growth is hardest to monitor.

A defence is also possible. Per-plot savings and higher yields from fertigation are shown in the same Tunisian study (SRC-TG-531), and the 2007 Plan Bleu national report shows value added of irrigated production per m3 of irrigation water demand up 29% over 1990-2003 (SRC-TG-430), an economic indicator that predates most of the equipment drive. The programme may have improved farm income even if it did not save basin water.

## Trade-offs and consequences

- **Water:** possible aquifer pressure if area grew; not measured nationally. The Eau 2050 scenario table shows an overdraft of 865 Mm3 and 139% exploitation in 2020 is why this matters (SRC-TG-503).
- **Energy:** solar pumping lowers energy cost for farms that used diesel (the main motive in SRC-TG-532); an energy effect of drip equipment is not shown. The 20-farm survey found almost 90% of farmers pumping more water after solar installation (SRC-TG-532). Not nationally representative.
- **Agriculture:** better yields per plot, and possibly a shift to higher-value, more water-demanding crops (argued in Morocco, SRC-TG-940, not shown for Tunisia).
- **`creates` is empty:** the closest existing findings are FND-TG-400 and FND-TG-401 (groundwater overdraft and drilling), but the rebound link is not demonstrated nationally. Lane owner should consider a phase 1 finding on "irrigated-area expansion on groundwater after subsidised drip", if a measurement can be found.

## Verdict and gap

**Superficial.** It reached near-full coverage but acts on efficiency per plot and not on the access and allocation causes of the overdraft. The programme is not a failure on adoption; the problem is that nobody has measured net withdrawals, and local evidence points to rebound. "Unverifiable" would also be defensible at national level; superficial is chosen because the Tunisian causes in FND-TG-525 are unaddressed whatever the net effect is.

For phase 3: pair efficiency subsidies with a withdrawal or area cap, metering that works, and enforcement of borehole licences (recommended by SRC-TG-941 and consistent with the GIZ recommendations in FND-TG-525); subsidy conditioned on a fixed irrigated area; a before/after basin water audit.
