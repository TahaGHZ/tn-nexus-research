## What it is

Tunisia treats about 293 million m3 of sewage a year in 127 ONAS plants and aims to turn it into irrigation water (SRC-TG-530). The solution has four parts:
- Tertiary upgrades at selected plants: sand filter, UV and similar (SRC-TG-650).
- Irrigated perimeters supplied with treated water through the CRDA and GDA user groups (SRC-TG-652).
- Legal rules: Decree 89-1047 of 1989 as amended in 1993, standard NT 106.03, and a crop list (SRC-TG-650, SRC-TG-557).
- Strategy: Eau 2050 and "Water REUSE 2050", with a stated 80% use target for 2050 (SRC-TG-530).

Phase 1 of the AfDB-financed upgrade programme (PAQEE, 2012) rehabilitated about thirty plants. Phase 2 (PAQEE-RCC, 2024) funds 18 to 19 plants with a EUR 81.9 million loan (SRC-TG-650, SRC-TG-559). The Northern Tunis project in Borj Touil is a flagship case (SRC-TG-556).

## What it claims, and what the evidence shows

**Volumes do not match the target.** Three official series disagree, which is itself a finding:
- 22 of 292 Mm3 reused in 2017 (105 plants analysed), a direct rate of 8% (SRC-TG-650).
- 57 of 293 Mm3 per year at end-2023 (about 19%), stated orally by ONAS to a parliamentary committee (SRC-TG-559).
- 10 Mm3 (2023) and 11.3 Mm3 (2024) used for irrigation out of 293 and 295.7 Mm3 treated, about 3.4% and 3.8% (SRC-TG-530).

The ministry report itself says the use rate "stagne" at 10% at most (SRC-TG-530). The 57 Mm3 probably includes other uses, but this is not stated. Area actually irrigated was 2,425 ha (2021), 2,725 (2022), 2,577 (2023) and 2,778 ha (2024) (SRC-TG-530). Against that, PAQEE alone was said to give potential for 12,500 ha (SRC-TG-650).

**Potential versus actual is an old gap.** In 2000-2009 the area actually irrigated averaged 47% of equipped area (SRC-TG-651). Demand for treated water plateaued at 25 to 30% of treated volume even though it was priced 3 to 4 times below freshwater (SRC-TG-652). FAO's 2002 paper already said the water was available but "not suitable for crops that are economically profitable", is still echoed in the crop restrictions (SRC-TG-653, SRC-TG-557).

**Upgrades act on a real bottleneck.** The AfDB reports that stakeholders in the REUSE 2050 survey called quality "sometimes the main hindrance" (SRC-TG-650), and the ministry says treatment ignores user needs (SRC-TG-530). JICA, citing ONAS 2021, reports 28 of 125 plants with tertiary treatment (SRC-TG-558); the ministry reports 31 of 127 in 2024 (SRC-TG-530). That is a rise of three plants in three years, on slightly different plant counts. Outcomes of the 2012 phase (how many hectares were actually irrigated after upgrades) were not found.

## Audit

**root_cause_coverage: 1.** FND-TG-550 names two root causes: effluent quality and conveyance. The programme acts on the first. It funds no transfer lines. For FND-TG-501, the root causes are climate decline, demand growth, and subsidised tariffs that encouraged over-use. Reuse adds supply and leaves all three in place. Supply of 290 to 300 Mm3 a year, if fully used, covers about a quarter of the 2050 deficit (SRC-TG-504 via FND-TG-501).

**evidence_quality: 1.** Evidence on effects is thin. The sources opened are lenders' appraisals (targets, not outcomes), a ministry sector report with inconsistent series, and oral statements recorded in a parliamentary report. No ex-post evaluation of PAQEE 1 was found. The soil studies are peer-reviewed but were seen as abstracts only.

**adoption: 1.** Widely deployed (127 plants, hundreds of hectares to a few thousand under irrigation), but actual use is 3 to 4% on the ministry's series, and the 80% target is far away.

**side_effects: 2.** Salinity and boron build-up are documented at perimeter scale but not as yield losses. Health risk is flagged by the lender but not measured. Offsetting benefits exist (less discharge, fertiliser savings). Energy effects are unquantified. Hence moderate, not none known.

**cost_accessibility: 1.** The price to farmers (TND 0.02/m3, unchanged since 1997) is a fraction of the TND 0.3 to 0.7/m3 treatment cost (SRC-TG-652), so the state and foreign lenders carry the cost. As an indicative figure only, EUR 81.9 million for about 3,357 ha is roughly EUR 24,000 per hectare, but the loan also pays for equipment renewal and solar PV, so this overstates the irrigation cost. ONAS's own financing depends on state aid and tariff rises.

## Limits and blockers

See the front matter for each blocker with its type. Legal text: Decree 89-1047 (1989), amended by Decree 93-2447 (1993), allows agricultural use only on authorisation by the Agriculture Minister after agreement of the Environment and Health Ministers, and only after treatment that meets current standards (SRC-TG-650). It also sets, via NT 106.03, a prohibition on irrigating vegetables and crops eaten raw and on direct grazing (SRC-TG-557, a consultant's second-hand report; the JORT text and the crop-list order were not opened). A revised Water Code was before Parliament at the time of the AfDB report and would allow use-specific standards based on risk analysis (SRC-TG-650); its adoption status was not checked.

## Criticisms

Listed in front matter. Two points stand out. First, the sources disagree on the cause of low uptake: the lenders and ministry stress quality, while the economic literature stresses cheap alternatives, distance and crop restrictions. The evidence we hold supports both, with quality plausible for some plants (72.42% compliance in 2024, SRC-TG-530) but no source opened shows how much of the 25 to 30% plateau quality explains, since plant-level compliance and use were not compared. Second, nothing opened shows that upgraded plants led to proportionally higher use.

## Trade-offs and consequences

- **Agriculture (long-term, observed):** soil salinity and boron accumulation under long-term irrigation (SRC-TG-654, SRC-TG-655). Whether this lowers yields was not shown. No existing finding covers this. It should go to the agriculture lane owner for phase 1 as a candidate: "Salt and boron accumulation in soils and aquifers under treated-wastewater irrigation".
- **Water (observed locally):** boron reaching groundwater at Cebala Borj-Touil (SRC-TG-655).
- **Energy (not quantified):** energy is about 60% of ONAS operating cost (SRC-TG-652); tertiary treatment and pumping add load. The only offset figure is the AfDB's own preliminary estimate (1,887 tCO2eq/year, mixing solar and lower consumption, to be confirmed; SRC-TG-650). Any energy figure here needs a kWh per m3 source.
- **Health (risk flagged, not measured):** AfDB names illness among farmers and consumers as a project risk (SRC-TG-650).
- **Public finance:** ONAS operates at a deficit covered by state balancing subsidies; new loans add guaranteed debt (SRC-TG-559, SRC-TG-650).
- **Projection, not observation:** the AfDB statement that treated water "will possibly more than double by 2050" is a projection (SRC-TG-650).

## Verdict and gap

Partially solves. It acts on a real technical cause for part of the problem and delivers a modest, bounded supply, but does not address pricing, crop rules, distance or seasonality, and it cannot close the 2050 deficit. It works at small scale (the Ouardanine model, SRC-TG-652) and is not widely adopted, which is a delivery and design problem rather than a proof that reuse fails.

Phase 3 could examine: pairing upgrades with funded transfer lines and storage; reviewing crop lists and standards on a WHO-style risk basis; salinity management and drainage in reuse perimeters; reuse targeted at coastal aquifers where groundwater is over-pumped (SRC-TG-653); and consistent metering of reuse. Regional comparators (Jordan, Morocco, Egypt) were not audited in this record and are not claimed.
