## Problem

The brief framed pesticides as a problem of use, residues and regulation. On volume, the data do not support a problem. FAOSTAT estimates Tunisian use at 0.65 kg of active ingredient per hectare of cropland in 2023, against 2.38 kg/ha worldwide, 0.82 in Northern Africa and 1.55 in Europe (SRC-TG-831). Total use rose from 909 t in 1990 to a peak of 3,511 t in 2019, then eased to about 3,195 t in 2023, of which about 2,000 t were fungicides (SRC-TG-831). The evidence of harm is concentrated in intensive greenhouse vegetables. In Sousse in 2017, 20 harvest samples of tomato and chili pepper showed 7 exceedances of EU maximum residue limits, up to 26 times the limit. Worst-case chlorpyrifos intake from tomatoes reached 312% of the acute reference dose for children (SRC-TG-832). Workers rarely wear protection, and growers lack training (SRC-TG-832, SRC-TG-833).

## Root causes

- **Practice in protected horticulture.** Tomato and chili pepper are sprayed many times up to harvest. Growers say some products fail and that they then raise doses or spray more often (SRC-TG-832). Only 8.3% of the workers surveyed wear gloves (SRC-TG-832). This is the proximate cause of residues and worker exposure.
- **Training and waste.** In an FAO survey of 1,174 farmers in Ben Arous, Nabeul and Monastir, relayed by Böll, 91% had no training on pesticide best practice and only 33% used protective equipment; 63% burned empty packaging outdoors and 30% left waste in nature (SRC-TG-833).
- **Regulation (past decision).** Law 92-72 of 3 August 1992 recast plant protection law and organised the pesticide sector. It bans the import, sale or distribution of any pesticide without registration or a provisional sales authorisation from the Minister of Agriculture (SRC-TG-834). The framework covers market entry, but monitoring is weak. The Toumi et al. study links the problems mainly to "regulation weaknesses, the lack of awareness and monitoring" (SRC-TG-832). Böll reports that no statistics on use by crop or on health and environmental effects are collected, and that pesticides banned in Europe remain on the Tunisian market (SRC-TG-833).

## Evidence

- National volumes and intensity, with comparisons for 2023: world 2.38 kg/ha, Africa 0.81, Northern Africa 0.82 and Europe 1.55 (SRC-TG-831).
- Training: in the FAO 2019 survey relayed by Böll (1,174 farmers in Ben Arous, Nabeul and Monastir), 91% had no training on best practice (SRC-TG-833). All Tunisian values carry FAOSTAT flags E, I or X, not A.
- **Sources disagree:** Böll gives 4,161.2 t of pesticide imports in 2022 and an average use of 0.714 kg/ha (ANGeD, 2013) (SRC-TG-833). FAOSTAT gives 3,247 t of use and 0.66 kg/ha for 2022 (SRC-TG-831). Imports and use are different measures, and the Böll figure may be formulated product, so both are recorded. They agree that intensity is below 1 kg/ha.
- Residues, consumer intake and worker exposure: SRC-TG-832 (peer-reviewed; 20 fruit samples, 73 growers, Sousse, April 2017; EU MRLs as reference). Böll quotes the chlorpyrifos adult figure as 80%; the paper says 82% (SRC-TG-833, SRC-TG-832).
- Legal framework: SRC-TG-834.

## Why confidence is what it is

Low overall. The volume comparison is moderately solid, since FAOSTAT is consistent with the ANGeD estimate relayed by Böll, but none of these values is official. The misuse and residue part rests on one small peer-reviewed study (20 samples, one governorate, one week, EU limits) and on a tier B policy paper relaying surveys that were not opened. Low use per hectare across all cropland can hide high use on a small area of greenhouses; the data needed to show how use is distributed do not exist in the sources opened. The finding refutes "Tunisia uses a lot of pesticides" as a national claim. The concentrated-misuse claim is plausible but thinly sourced.

## Links to other sectors

Water: the 2024 national plant genetic resources report lists irrational pesticide use among threats to biodiversity (SRC-TG-828), but no water contamination data were opened. None found for energy.
