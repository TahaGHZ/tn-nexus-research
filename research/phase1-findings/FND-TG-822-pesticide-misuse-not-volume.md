## Problem

The brief framed pesticides as a problem of use, residues and regulation. Per-hectare use remains below the world average: FAOSTAT estimates Tunisian use at 0.83 kg of active ingredient per hectare of cropland in 2024, against 2.49 kg/ha worldwide, 0.87 in Northern Africa and 1.65 in Europe (SRC-TG-831). Total use was estimated at 4,047 t in 2024, the highest value in the series, including 2,533 t of fungicides (SRC-TG-831). Tunisia's intensity is close to the Northern Africa average and about one third of the world average. The evidence of harm is concentrated in intensive greenhouse vegetables. In Sousse in 2017, 20 harvest samples of tomato and chili pepper showed 7 exceedances of EU maximum residue limits, up to 26 times the limit. Worst-case chlorpyrifos intake from tomatoes reached 312% of the acute reference dose for children (SRC-TG-832). Workers rarely wear protection, and growers lack training (SRC-TG-832, SRC-TG-833).

## Root causes

- **Practice in protected horticulture.** Tomato and chili pepper are sprayed many times up to harvest. The study links some product inefficacy to growers raising doses or spraying more often; it also reports that labels and pre-harvest intervals are not followed (SRC-TG-832). Only 8.3% of surveyed re-entry farm workers wore gloves (SRC-TG-832). These practices contribute to residue and worker-exposure risks.
- **Training and waste.** In an FAO survey of 1,174 farmers in Ben Arous, Nabeul and Monastir, relayed by Böll, 91% had no training on pesticide best practice and only 33% used protective equipment; 63% burned empty packaging outdoors and 30% left waste in nature (SRC-TG-833). The same report gives high PPE cost (no subsidy) and market unavailability as reasons for non-use.
- **Safe-use barriers (reported, tier B).** The same survey summary gives high PPE cost (no subsidy) and market unavailability as reasons for non-use, alongside training gaps and unsafe disposal (SRC-TG-833). The original survey was not opened, so these barriers are reported here, not presented as independently established root causes.
- **Regulation (past decision).** Law 92-72 of 3 August 1992 recast plant protection law and organised the pesticide sector. It bans the import, sale or distribution of any pesticide without registration or a provisional sales authorisation from the Minister of Agriculture (SRC-TG-834). The framework covers market entry, but monitoring is weak. The Toumi et al. study links the problems mainly to "regulation weaknesses, the lack of awareness and monitoring" (SRC-TG-832). Böll reports that no statistics on use by crop or on health and environmental effects are collected, and that pesticides banned in Europe remain on the Tunisian market (SRC-TG-833).

## Evidence

- National volumes and intensity, with comparisons for 2024: Tunisia 0.83 kg/ha; world 2.49, Africa 0.83, Northern Africa 0.87 and Europe 1.65 (SRC-TG-831).
- Training: in the FAO 2019 survey relayed by Böll (1,174 farmers in Ben Arous, Nabeul and Monastir), 91% had no training on best practice (SRC-TG-833). For Tunisia's pesticide-use total, 1997 is flagged A; from 1998 onward values are E, I or X (SRC-TG-831).
- **Sources disagree:** Böll gives 4,161.2 t of pesticide imports in 2022 and an average use of 0.714 kg/ha (ANGeD, 2013) (SRC-TG-833). FAOSTAT gives 3,247 t of use and 0.66 kg/ha for 2022 (SRC-TG-831). Imports and use are different measures, and the Böll figure may be formulated product, so both are recorded. They agree that intensity is below 1 kg/ha.
- Residues, consumer intake and worker exposure: SRC-TG-832 (peer-reviewed; 20 fruit samples, 73 growers, Sousse, April 2017; EU MRLs as reference). Böll quotes the chlorpyrifos adult figure as 80%; the paper says 82% (SRC-TG-833, SRC-TG-832).
- Legal framework: SRC-TG-834.

## Why confidence is what it is

Low overall. The volume comparison relies on FAOSTAT estimates from 1998 onward, not official Tunisian values. The misuse and residue part rests on one small peer-reviewed study (20 samples, one governorate, one week, EU limits) and on a tier B policy paper relaying surveys that were not opened. Low use per hectare across all cropland can hide high use on a small area of greenhouses; the data needed to show how use is distributed do not exist in the sources opened. FAOSTAT's 2024 estimate is the highest in its series, though flags and estimation limit certainty about the level. The concentrated-misuse claim is plausible but thinly sourced.

## Links to other sectors

Water: the 2024 national plant genetic resources report lists irrational pesticide use among threats to biodiversity (SRC-TG-828), but no water contamination data were opened. None found for energy.

## Leads for phase 2

- Pesticide registration (homologation) under law 92-72 and decree 92-2246 (SRC-TG-834, SRC-TG-833): https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/tun11737.pdf
- Mandatory import inspection of agricultural pesticides, decree 94-1744 (SRC-TG-833).
- Distributing protective equipment with pesticide purchases, proposed by workers (SRC-TG-832).
- Biological control studies cited by Böll: mass trapping of the carob moth, entomopathogenic nematodes (SRC-TG-833).
- ILO Convention C184 on Safety and Health in Agriculture, not ratified by Tunisia according to Böll (SRC-TG-833).
