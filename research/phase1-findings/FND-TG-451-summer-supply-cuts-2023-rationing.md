## Problem

In 2009 the World Bank described SONEDE supply as running "24 heures sur 24", with cuts "très rares" and made only for operations (SRC-TG-455). By 2016-2017, summer disruptions were routine: 224 SONEDE localities in summer 2016 and 185 in summer 2017, plus 202 and 159 rural Génie Rural/GDA systems disrupted or stopped (SRC-TG-402).

On 29 March 2023 the Ministry of Agriculture issued a decision imposing a temporary quota system on all SONEDE users. It banned the use of SONEDE water for agriculture, green spaces, street cleaning and car washing until end-September 2023 (decision announced 31 March; SRC-TG-459, SRC-TG-460). SONEDE announced night cuts (21:00-04:00, varying by region), and its CEO said available water covered only 16 hours of consumption a day (SRC-TG-460, SRC-TG-461). On 28 September 2023 the decision was extended "until further notice" (SRC-TG-460), and the measures ran into 2024 (SRC-TG-463).

The Observatoire Tunisien de l'Eau logged 1,592 citizen alerts on unannounced cuts in 2023 (SRC-TG-462). Night cuts returned in Sousse, Monastir and Mahdia in July 2026. National dams were then reported 60% full, though the central Nebhana dam, which feeds the Sahel, was still in deficit (SRC-TG-463).

## Root causes

Low dams are the symptom and the trigger, not the whole cause. The evidence points to three structural causes beneath the drought.

1. **Regional supply capacity, foreseen and still constrained.** The coastal demand centres rely on transfers from northern and central resources. In 2017 the ministry warned that Grand Tunis, Cap Bon, the Sahel and Sfax faced a peak-day deficit of 1.6 m3/s from 2020. It called for faster delivery of reservoirs (Saïda, Kalaa), transfer reinforcement and desalination plants (SRC-TG-402). It named "les longs délais de réalisation des projets" as a problem in its own right (SRC-TG-402). In 2026 WMC reported that the north-to-centre transfer linked to Kalaa Kebira was delayed by land expropriation and that the Sousse desalination plant remained in testing (SRC-TG-464).
2. **Network efficiency limits delivered supply.** In SONEDE's 2023 figures, 773.3 Mm3 were produced and 516.0 Mm3 consumed; the difference is about one third of production, calculated from the published totals, and is not a direct measure of physical leakage or billed consumption (SRC-TG-453). This gap means more production is needed to deliver a given volume of recorded consumption.
3. **Pressure asymmetry within networks.** In the Sahel, high neighbourhoods (Hay Riadh, Fgaiya, Ouled Ahmed) ran dry first because consumption in low zones emptied the pipes. SONEDE presented the 2026 night cuts as a way to rebalance pressure between high and low zones (SRC-TG-463, SRC-TG-464).

The drought itself is the trigger cited by the decision (SRC-TG-459, SRC-TG-460) and by SONEDE (SRC-TG-461). Dam fill levels are covered by FND-TG-425.

## Evidence

- **Baseline.** Continuous supply was the norm in 2009 (SRC-TG-455).
- **Chronic summer disruptions before the 2023 drought peak.** See the 2016-2017 counts above (SRC-TG-402).
- **The 2023 measures.** Quotas for all SONEDE users; bans on agricultural, green-space, street-cleaning and car-washing uses; penalties under Water Code Article 158 (fine of 60-1,000 TND and/or 6 days to 9 months in prison) and Decree 2017-157 Article 44 (SRC-TG-459, SRC-TG-460). Night cuts ran 21:00-04:00 by region (SRC-TG-460). The CEO said supply covered only 16 hours a day (SRC-TG-461).
- **Dates disagree slightly.** The decision is dated 29 March 2023 and the ban was announced on 31 March 2023 (SRC-TG-460). It was extended on 28 September 2023 (SRC-TG-460) and continued into 2024 (SRC-TG-463).
- **Felt impact.** In 2023, 1,893 citizen alerts were logged, 1,592 of them unannounced cuts. Ben Arous (187), Nabeul (158) and Gafsa (131) led (SRC-TG-462).
- **2026 Sahel cuts with refilled dams.** National dams were 60% full at end-June 2026, but central dams such as Nebhana were still short (SRC-TG-463). The two press framings differ:
  - SRC-TG-463 presents the cuts as pressure balancing during desalination-plant tests;
  - SRC-TG-464 presents them as chronic regional water stress worsened by Nebhana.

  Both point to regional supply and infrastructure rather than national stock.
- **Demand outlook.** The Eau 2050 strategy scenario has drinking-water demand rising from 780 Mm3 (2020) to 1,100 Mm3 (2050). This is a normative scenario that assumes the strategy is carried out (SRC-TG-503). The national supply-demand outlook is covered in FND-TG-501.

## Why confidence is what it is

The pre-2023 chronic disruption and the forecast regional deficit come from the ministry's tier A national water report (SRC-TG-402). Everything about the 2023 decision, the 16-hour statement, the citizen alerts and the 2026 cuts rests on tier C press reports (SRC-TG-459 to SRC-TG-464). The ministerial decision texts and the OTE report could not be found or opened, hence medium confidence.

The interpretation that cuts are driven by regional capacity and network problems as well as drought is supported by two sources: the 2017 ministry forecast (tier A) and the 2026 episode (tier C). Their framings differ, as noted above.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture.** The 2023 decision banned agricultural use of SONEDE drinking water (SRC-TG-459). In rural areas, irrigation drawn from drinking-water networks weakens those systems (see FND-TG-452). The same drought cut surface-water irrigation allocations; that is outside this finding.
- **Energy.** Relief options cited (seawater desalination at Sousse, long-distance transfers) are energy-intensive (SRC-TG-402, SRC-TG-464). The cost and energy side belongs to the desalination lane (575-599).

## Independent source verification (2026-11-21)

- **Passed for in-review.** SRC-TG-402 was reopened from the archived original PDF after matching its title and edition; its report confirms the SONEDE disruption counts (224 localities in summer 2016; 185 in summer 2017), rural Génie Rural counts (202 in 2016; 159 in 2017), and the forecast peak-day deficit of 1.6 m3/s from 2020 for Grand Tunis, Cap Bon, the Sahel and Sfax. SRC-TG-455 confirms continuous SONEDE subscriber supply in 2009 and says cuts were very rare and operational. SRC-TG-503's Eau 2050 scenario table gives national drinking-water demand of 780 Mm3 in 2020 and 1,100 Mm3 in 2050.
- The 2023 restrictions, 16-hour statement, citizen alerts, and 2026 Sahel interruptions are supported by the cited press originals and remain explicitly attributed to their reported ministry, SONEDE, or Observatoire Tunisien de l'Eau sources. They are tier C evidence and do not establish subscriber counts affected, actual hours supplied by region, or an official lifting date for the 2023 quotas. The 2026 press accounts differ in emphasis: Business News reports SONEDE's stated pressure-balancing, summer-demand and Sousse desalination-test reasons; WMC adds Nebhana's supply deficit and reports high-zone shortages. The 2017 ministry report independently documents the earlier regional supply-demand imbalance and forecast capacity deficit.
- The original indicator values and claim context were checked with units, dates and geography. The 780-to-1,100 Mm3 values are the strategy's scenario, not measured consumption. The roughly one-third production-to-consumption difference is a calculation from SONEDE's published 2023 totals, not a direct leakage indicator. The 2023 16-hour figure is a CEO statement reported by Kapitalis; the 1,592 figure is citizen alerts for unannounced cuts, not a count of people or affected subscribers. No post-2017 locality count is claimed.
- Remaining data gaps are listed above: the ministerial decision texts, official quota end date, regional supply hours and affected subscribers, post-2017 disrupted-locality counts, OTE's own annual report, and confirmation whether the 2050 demand value remained in the final strategy scenario.
