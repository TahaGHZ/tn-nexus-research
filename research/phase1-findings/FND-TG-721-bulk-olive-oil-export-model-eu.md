## Problem

Tunisia exports most of its olive oil, and mostly as an unbranded bulk commodity. In 2024/25 it exported 275 kt, 81% of production, while only 45 kt was consumed at home (SRC-TG-724; IOC figures in SRC-TG-721). 53% of export value went to the EU in 2025, with Spain (25%) and Italy (22%) the top clients. 85% of exports leave in bulk to be blended with other oils, and only 15% are bottled (ONAGRI data in SRC-TG-724). The bulk share "a peu évolué dans le temps" (has changed little over time) despite promotion of bottled oil (SRC-TG-724).

As a result, Tunisian oil serves mainly as a cheap input and stabiliser for European bottlers (SRC-TG-724). Tunisian growers and exporters take the price swings of a commodity market (see FND-TG-720) without the margin of a brand. Bulk oil sold into the EU can carry a generic origin label: "blend of EU and non-EU olive oils", with no shares stated (SRC-TG-724).

## Root causes

**Symptom vs cause.** Low export value per kg and dependence on EU buyers are symptoms. The causes are a decades-old policy choice to treat olive oil as an export cash crop, and an EU trade regime that rewards bulk.

1. **Colonial and post-independence export model (legacy).** The Sfax rainfed groves were developed under the protectorate partly to guarantee oil for France, through dedicated customs policies (SRC-TG-725). After independence, ONH (created 1962) held the monopoly on collection and export and set prices (SRC-TG-724). The 1972 law created a special regime for export-oriented investment (SRC-TG-725, citing Elloumi 2013; Trésor also cites the 1972 law, SRC-TG-724).
2. **Reserve olive oil for export; subsidise seed oil at home (legacy, still active).** Public policy "a privilégié historiquement" (has historically favoured) exporting most production while steering consumers to other oils. A basic seed oil (soy, palm) is sold at 0.9 TND/L under the subsidy system managed by ONH, at a cost of about TND 120 million/yr (SRC-TG-724). Peer-reviewed work describes domestic olive oil consumption as declining in favour of cheaper vegetable oils, following "policies that formerly reserved olive oil for export" (SRC-TG-725).
3. **1994 liberalisation: from public to private monopoly.** In 1994 the state ended ONH's monopoly on prices and marketing (SRC-TG-724, SRC-TG-725). Lavie et al. report, from interviews, that marketing then moved to "half a dozen" dominant private groups (SRC-TG-725). Trésor counts about 70 private exporters and over 200 traders (SRC-TG-724), so the two describe concentration and number differently. Most exporters remain bound to bulk by limited packaging capacity (SRC-TG-725, SRC-TG-724). The wider 1986-1995 adjustment programme left ONH's role in olive oil exports ambiguous (see FND-TG-920). Price support continues to favour cereals, milk and beef over Mediterranean products such as olive oil (see FND-TG-921).
4. **The EU trade regime.** The Association Agreement (OJ L 97, 30.3.1998) and its 2005 Protocol open a zero-duty quota for Tunisian virgin olive oil. It was set at 50,000 t, raised by 1,500 t a year from 2002 and reached 56,700 t from 1 January 2005 (SRC-TG-728). The 56,700 t volume is set in Regulation 1918/2006 (SRC-TG-727) and is still current (SRC-TG-724). **Source disagreement:** Trésor states 56.7 kt "depuis 1998" (since 1998, SRC-TG-724), but the 2005 Protocol shows the 56,700 t level only from 2005 (SRC-TG-728). Above the quota, a EUR 1,245/t duty applies, except for oil imported under inward processing for refining, blending and re-export, about 30% of EU-bound Tunisian exports in 2018 (SRC-TG-724). FAO (2015) expected EU-bound exports to stay in bulk because EU oils "jouissent d'une forte protection" (enjoy strong protection, SRC-TG-726).

**What the evidence does not support.** A common belief is that the small EU quota caps Tunisian exports. The evidence is mixed:
- Exports to the EU averaged 128,000 t/yr over the last decade, more than twice the quota, because oil also enters under inward processing or pays duty (SRC-TG-724).
- Exports track domestic production more than quota size. When the quota was raised 62% to 91.7 kt in 2016, exports to the EU fell 62%, and when it went back to 56.7 kt in 2018 they more than doubled (SRC-TG-724).
- Around 2012, in data analysed by FAO in 2015, the quota was under-used for administrative reasons (SRC-TG-726). Since 2020 it is saturated, with allocation coefficients of 1-2% (SRC-TG-724).

So the quota is less a volume cap than a **value-capture problem**. Because quota certificates can be resold, speculation adds an estimated EUR 280-520/t, a rent for certificate traders. The security deposit required with applications favours large, well-financed operators (SRC-TG-724).

## Evidence

- Bulk/bottled split: 85/15 (ONAGRI via Trésor, ca. 2025) (SRC-TG-724).
- **Source disagreement on the bottled share.** 11% bottled (FAO, ca. 2013) (SRC-TG-726); 7.4% of export volume in 2020 (ONH bulletin via SRC-TG-725); 15% (ca. 2025) (SRC-TG-724). Definitions and years differ (volume vs value is not always stated). The figures do not show a clear rise in bottling.
- Exports and domestic consumption: IOC balances give consumption of 30 kt (2023/24), 45 kt (2024/25) and 50 kt (2025/26 estimate). Exports were 199.5, 275 and 300 kt (SRC-TG-721).
- **Source disagreement on 2024/25 exports.** The IOC estimate of November 2024 was 310 kt (SRC-TG-723); the provisional figure of November 2025 is 275 kt (SRC-TG-721).
- Export destinations diversified between 2015 and 2025: the US took 20% and Canada 8% of export value in 2025. The share of olive oil exports going to the EU nonetheless stays above half (53%, 2025) (SRC-TG-724).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The structural facts (bulk share, EU quota, liberalisation in 1994, seed-oil subsidy) are consistent across a tier A legal text, a peer-reviewed paper, a 2015 FAO/EBRD study and a 2026 Trésor note. But the key current numbers (85/15 split, certificate speculation cost, 30% inward processing, 128 kt average to the EU) come from a single tier B secondary source (Trésor) quoting ONAGRI, Eurostat and the European Commission. The underlying ONAGRI and Eurostat tables were not opened. The value forgone by exporting in bulk is not quantified by any opened source.

## Links to other sectors

None found directly in the opened sources. Indirectly, the export-first model rewards volume, which supports the move to irrigated intensive groves on groundwater described in FND-TG-720 (SRC-TG-725). This is a nexus link that belongs to the water lane.
