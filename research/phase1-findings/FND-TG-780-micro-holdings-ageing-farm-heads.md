## Problem

Tunisia's farm count rose from 326,000 in 1961-62 to 516,000 in 2004-05 while the farmed area stayed near 5.2-5.3 million ha, so the average farm shrank from 16 ha to 10.2 ha (SRC-TG-780). The growth is concentrated at the bottom: farms under 5 ha went from 133,000 (41% of farms) to 281,000 (54%), yet they hold only 11% of the farmed area; three farms in four are under 10 ha (SRC-TG-780). At the same time farm heads aged: those aged 60 and over rose from 21% to 43% of heads and work 46% of farmland, while heads under 40 fell from 33% to 13% (SRC-TG-780). Small farms rarely invest or borrow: 22.8% of farm heads under 5 ha invested in 2000-2004, against 61% of those over 100 ha, and 67.6% of their investment-credit applications were refused (SRC-TG-780). These are the latest national data we found: studies published in 2017 and about 2020 still rely on the 2004-05 survey, and the first general agricultural census is planned for 2026-2027 (reference season 2025-26; launch date was not yet announced in the source) (SRC-TG-781, SRC-TG-782, SRC-TG-793). The present size of the problem is therefore unknown.

## Root causes

The symptom is the growing number of very small, ageing holdings. The causes the sources give are:

- **Inheritance partition.** The ministry attributes the drop in average size mainly to division among heirs, strongest on farms under 5 ha (SRC-TG-780). Inheritance is the main source of ownership of owner-operated parcels (SRC-TG-780). The FAO/CIHEAM study calls attachment to land as a psychological and economic asset, together with inheritance sharing, the decisive factor (SRC-TG-781).
- **Past land policy (legacy decisions).** The ministry also names the settlement of collective lands and the restructuring of state farms as drivers of the rise in farm numbers (SRC-TG-780). The FAO/CIHEAM study links fragmentation to a policy of generalising private property: dismantling habous land (from 1 million ha after independence to about 80,000 ha), settling half of the 3 million ha of collective land in the centre and south, restructuring state land and consolidating plots in public irrigated schemes (SRC-TG-781). FTDES adds that more than half of restructured state land went to private management after farms were cut into smaller units (SRC-TG-782).
- **Tenure context (not a separately established root cause).** Owner operation rose from about 82% of parcels in the early 1960s to about 95% in 2004, and renting fell from 7.5% to 2.2% of parcels (SRC-TG-780). The survey does not establish that this thin rental market causes farm fragmentation.

The absence of data since 2005 is not a cause of fragmentation, but it means farm, tenure and credit policy has had no current structural data to work from (SRC-TG-793; see `data_gaps`).

**Where the evidence departs from the common framing.** Two common beliefs are not supported by the survey data:

- *"Fragmentation" as plots scattered across many parcels.* The number of parcels per farm fell from 2 (1994) to 1.8 (2004); 55% of farms, and 69% of farms under 5 ha, are a single parcel (SRC-TG-780). The problem is that holdings are multiplying and shrinking, not that each farm is split into many separate plots.
- *Land concentration.* The FAO/CIHEAM study says land concentration is "increasingly visible" (SRC-TG-781). The size tables show the opposite trend for 1961-2005: farms of 100 ha and more fell from 29% to 22% of the farmed area, and land held by legal persons (SMVDA, cooperatives, agro-combinats) fell from 482,000 ha (9%) in 1994 to 364,500 ha (6.8%) in 2004 (SRC-TG-780). The two may describe different things: FAO/CIHEAM points to a rush of outside investors onto land and water in high-potential localities since the 1990s, citing Sidi Bouzid, and says national data on its scale are lacking (SRC-TG-781), whereas the survey gives national averages up to 2005. We record both and cannot settle the question without post-2005 data.

## Evidence

- Farm numbers, area and average size, 1961-62 / 1994-95 / 2004-05: 326,000 / 471,000 / 516,000 farms; 5.21 / 5.30 / 5.27 million ha; 16 / 11.2 / 10.2 ha average; farmland per inhabitant 1.2 / 0.6 / 0.5 ha (SRC-TG-780).
- Size classes in 2004-05: under 5 ha, 54% of farms and 11% of area; under 10 ha, 75% and 25%; over 50 ha, 3% and 34% (SRC-TG-780).
- Ageing: heads aged 60+ were 21% / 37% / 43% of farm heads and held 24% / 40% / 46% of farmland; 84% of heads had at most primary education (SRC-TG-780).
- Investment, 2000-2004: 26% of all heads invested; 22.8% of those under 5 ha and 61% of those over 100 ha. Help from relatives was a more common source of finance than bank credit or leasing (SRC-TG-780).
- Credit, campaign 2003-04: 87.5% of heads did not apply, 6.3% applied and obtained credit, 6.2% applied and were refused. Investment-credit applications came from 5.2% of heads under 5 ha and 18.4% of those over 100 ha. Refusal rates for small farms were 41.6% (seasonal) and 67.6% (investment); large farms had 93.8% and 80.6% of applications satisfied. Non-applicants cited access difficulty (28%) and fear of debt (27%) (SRC-TG-780).
- The FTDES study gives about 470,000 ha for farms under 5 ha in 2005 (SRC-TG-782). That is the survey's 1994-95 value; the 2004-05 value is 556,000 ha (SRC-TG-780). We use the survey.

## Why confidence is what it is

The figures come from one tier A official sample survey (47,700 farms, with large farms fully enumerated), consistent across three survey rounds (SRC-TG-780). The trend up to 2005 is solid. Confidence is medium, not high, because:

- the data stop in 2003-04 and the trend since is unknown, which is why `trend` is `unknown`;
- the causal weight of inheritance against past land policy rests on the ministry's and FAO/CIHEAM's statements, not on a decomposition;
- the survey breaks credit down by farm size, not by tenure status, so it cannot show whether a missing title is what blocks credit (see FND-TG-781).

Related records: FND-TG-720 names fragmented olive smallholdings with little capital as a cause of low yields; FND-TG-742 names land fragmentation among the causes of declining traditional oases in Kebili; FND-TG-921 finds that output- and area-based support mostly benefits large farms.

## Links to other sectors

Water: in 2004-05 farms under 5 ha irrigated 16% of their cultivated area, compared with 6.4% for farms of 100 ha and more, and FTDES puts their irrigated area (about 83,000 ha) at about a quarter of the national irrigated area (SRC-TG-780, SRC-TG-782). Small irrigated holdings are therefore a large share of groundwater and public-scheme users, which matters for the water lane's irrigation and groundwater findings. Energy: none found.
