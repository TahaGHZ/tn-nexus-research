## Problem

In Kébili governorate (Nefzaoua), the deep aquifers of the Saharan system (Complexe Terminal and Continental Intercalaire) were exploited at 171% of their estimated resource in 2016, second only to Nabeul (195%) among the governorates the ministry singled out that year (SRC-TG-402). DGRE data put controlled withdrawals at 406.82 Mm3 against a resource of 238 Mm3. On top of that, 4,549 unauthorised boreholes drew an estimated 210.3 Mm3 (SRC-TG-630). The pumping feeds private date-palm "extensions" built on collective land outside the old oases. They grew from about 7,000 ha in 1996 to 32,700 ha in 2020, while the old public palm groves cover at most 10,500 ha (SRC-TG-631). Kébili's oasis area rose from 7,600 ha in 1992 to 36,560 ha in 2018, and Tunisian surveys attribute almost all growth after 2001 to illicit extension (SRC-TG-634). A DGRE figure for 2021, cited by researchers, gives over 765 Mm3 pumped in the region, 47% of it illicit (SRC-TG-631). The extensions now provide about 80% of the region's Deglet Nour dates (SRC-TG-632).

## Root causes

The symptom is a deep-aquifer exploitation rate far above 100% and falling water levels. The causes:

1. **Private extensions on unauthorised boreholes.** In the extensions, access to water is private and farmers irrigate from individual or collective illicit boreholes. The old oases are served by public CRDA boreholes through GDA networks (SRC-TG-631). New farmland in Kébili taps the CT through artisanal boreholes about 100 m deep (SRC-TG-630). Some farmers connect peripheral extensions illicitly to the collective networks of public deep boreholes drilled for the old oases (SRC-TG-631).
2. **Enforcement that gave way to tolerance.** Almost all of Kébili lies in safeguard perimeters decreed in 1985 for the CI and the CT (SRC-TG-632). Coercive measures (rig seizures, prosecutions) were short-lived and had very little impact, "conduisant en pratique à une situation de laisser-faire" (SRC-TG-632). Researchers describe an "unacknowledged tolerance" by the regional administration. As early as 1991, officials refused to map the extensions because they eased social tension (SRC-TG-631). Land tenure adds a structural incentive: on collective land, sinking a well, even an illicit one, opens a route to individual appropriation (SRC-TG-632). This traces to past decisions, recorded in FND-TG-626.
3. **Economic pull and push.** Liberalisation of the date market raised Deglet Nour prices. The old oases became less profitable through fragmentation, falling fertility and water deficit, and the region offers few non-farm alternatives (SRC-TG-631, SRC-TG-632). The 2019 water-balance study says export prices drove investment in the 2000s and that fraud multiplied because the aquifer is shallow enough to reach (SRC-TG-630).
4. **Solar pumping removed the last cost brake.** Farmers in the extensions moved from diesel and grid pumps to solar from about 2014. Solar is used especially far from towns, where no STEG connection is possible (SRC-TG-631, SRC-TG-632). The researchers detected 2,358 photovoltaic stations, and found that installers tend to oversize systems, which encourages over-irrigation (SRC-TG-631). The FAO-commissioned report and the World Bank-filed study say solar enabled the explosion of illicit extensions after 2011 (SRC-TG-634, SRC-TG-630).

## Evidence

- **Kébili, DGRE 2016 (SRC-TG-630, Tableaux 3-4).** Resource 238 Mm3; controlled exploitation 406.82 Mm3 from 3,575 water points (2,848 pumped, 714 artesian, 13 springs yielding 0.96 Mm3). Unauthorised boreholes: 4,549, drawing 210.3 Mm3. The ministry gives 171% for Kébili in 2016 (SRC-TG-402), which equals 406.82/238 and so may exclude the illicit volume. It also estimates 4,559 illicit boreholes in Kébili (SRC-TG-402). The two counts differ by 10.
- **Kébili, AGRIDATA 2019, as cited (SRC-TG-634).** 418.3 Mm3 withdrawn against 236.7 Mm3 available, 177%.
- **Kébili region, DGRE 2021, as cited (SRC-TG-631).** Over 765 Mm3 pumped, 47% illicit, said to be 40% of the volume pumped in Tunisia. Against a national deep-aquifer total of 2,084 Mm3 in 2021 (same source), 765 Mm3 would be about 37%, so the 40% may include shallow wells or a different perimeter.
- **Kébili, UNECE GIS model (SRC-TG-633).** Irrigation consumption is modelled at 307 Mm3/yr. This is far below DGRE figures, probably because the model's cropped-area mapping misses the extensions; it is not used as an indicator.
- **Extension area (SRC-TG-631).** About 7,000 ha (1996), 26,020 ha (2016 official census, nearly double 2010) and 32,735 ha mapped in 2021. Earlier, illicit extensions in Kébili were 4,085 ha in 1995 (Hajji 1996, as cited in SRC-TG-634).
- **Oasis surveys (SRC-TG-634).** Kébili 7,600 ha (1992), 15,960 ha (2001), 36,560 ha (2018); 64.5% of the national oasis area. "Oasis nouvelles privées" make up 37,387 of 56,654 ha nationally. Local sources quoted in the report estimate 9,000-10,000 illicit wells in Kébili after 2011, about twice the DGRE count.
- **Water use.** A 2012 survey found 16,813 m3/ha in the Kébili-Tozeur oases against 3,633 m3/ha in the Jeffara. Private-access farms used about 7,000 m3/ha and collective schemes about 12,500 m3/ha (SRC-TG-629). Private extensions are therefore not necessarily the heaviest users per hectare; their impact comes from the area added.
- **Consequences reported.** Farmers in the extensions report waterlogging and soil salinisation with no state drainage, and dig informal drains themselves (SRC-TG-631). Water quality is degrading near the chotts and pumping costs are rising as levels fall (SRC-TG-630).
- **Ministry statements.** The 2017 national water report names the Kébili region among the places where private irrigation spreads through boreholes drilled without authorisation (RNE 2017, PDF p. 88). It reports growing water waste in Kébili oases after solar pumps were installed on illicit boreholes "qui ne peuvent avoir accès à un raccordement STEG" (RNE 2017, PDF p. 111). Both extracts belong to the document in SRC-TG-402 and are not yet in that record (see data_gaps). In 2021 the minister told parliament the ministry was grouping farmers with unauthorised wells in Kébili into water groups to control consumption (SRC-TG-484).

**How representative is the entry issue?** For Kébili it is the core of the problem: Kébili drew 52% of the water mobilised across the four oasis governorates in 2016 (SRC-TG-630), and the unauthorised volume is almost entirely there. Tozeur (Djerid) is different. DGRE recorded 20 unauthorised boreholes there in 2016, and its controlled deep-aquifer use was 85% of resource (SRC-TG-630). The study attributes this to the greater depth and cost of reaching the aquifer (SRC-TG-630). Local sources claim about 1,000 illicit wells in Tozeur (SRC-TG-634), so the DGRE count may be low, but on the available data the "Kébili and Tozeur" framing overstates Tozeur. Nationally, FND-TG-400 shows the same pattern of illicit drilling in the centre; Kébili's distinguishing feature is that the water is largely non-renewable.

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The direction is consistent across a ministry report (tier A), DGRE tables reproduced in a World Bank-filed study, a peer-reviewed article and an FAO-commissioned report. Every source shows Kébili withdrawals well above the resource, driven by illicit extensions. The magnitudes disagree:
- Exploitation rates: 171% (2016), 177% (AGRIDATA, about 2019), and about 259% if the 2016 illicit volume is additional to the controlled volume. None of the sources states how the illicit volume enters the rate.
- Volumes: 406.82 Mm3 controlled plus 210.3 Mm3 illicit (2016), against 765 Mm3 (2021, perimeter undefined, cited second-hand).
- Illicit boreholes: 4,549-4,559 (official, 2016-2017) against 9,000-10,000 (local estimates, after 2011).
- Extension areas come from satellite mapping by one research team and an official census, which roughly agree (26,020 ha in 2016; 32,735 ha in 2021).

The study filed by the World Bank is of draft quality (see SRC-TG-630 notes), so only its DGRE tables and piezometric statements are used. The 80% share of date production is unreferenced in its source.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture (dates-oases):** the extensions produce about 80% of Kébili's Deglet Nour dates (SRC-TG-632). Kébili produced about 250,000 t of dates, as cited in SRC-TG-634. Soil salinisation and waterlogging in undrained extensions are already cutting palm productivity in places such as Om Somâa Sud (SRC-TG-631). Old oases lose labour and water as farmers concentrate on the extensions (SRC-TG-632).
- **Energy:** most extension pumping is off-grid. STEG representatives "had no idea" of the scale of solar pumping and ignored the existence of the illicit extensions (SRC-TG-631). Energy planning and the ANME installer scheme therefore operate without regard to groundwater limits (SRC-TG-631). The UNECE model gives Tunisia about 67 GWh/yr of irrigation pumping energy in the SASS area, highest in Kébili and Tozeur (SRC-TG-633, model output).
- **Water quality:** the same pumping is associated with salinisation of soils and the CT near the chotts (SRC-TG-630). Tap-water salinity is a separate topic (records 600-624).

## Leads for phase 2

- Massire project (2019-2024) multi-stakeholder process and proposed "win-win contract": farmers stop extending and accept meters in exchange for legalisation of plots; caps on solar pumping or resale of surplus electricity to STEG (SRC-TG-632, SRC-TG-631). https://agritrop.cirad.fr/611699/
- Grouping farmers with unauthorised wells in Kébili into water groups (GDA), announced to parliament in 2021 (SRC-TG-484).
- New Water Code (bill 66/2019): regional water committees, an inspectors' corps, a national information system and heavier penalties (SRC-TG-631, SRC-TG-484).
- ANME list of eligible solar installers and control of installers (SRC-TG-631, SRC-TG-632). https://www.anme.tn/sites/default/files/liste_des_installateurs_eligibles_01-12-2023.pdf
- Collective borehole management examples visited by Kébili farmers: GDA Bsissi (Gabès), GDA Abène (Cap Bon), energy transition in the Zarat oases (SRC-TG-631).
- India's solar-pump buy-back model (Shah et al. 2016), cited in SRC-TG-632.
- Management "par les forages" or "par les superficies" as alternatives to metering (Bouzidi et al. 2023, cited in SRC-TG-632).
- APIOS project (Amélioration des Périmètres Irrigués des Oasis du Sud), named in RNE 2017.
