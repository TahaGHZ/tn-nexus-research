## Problem

A 2013 World Bank working paper estimated informal fuel imports from Algeria at about 25% of
Tunisian consumption and separately extrapolated 921,600 m³/year from assumed border-van activity
(SRC-TG-310). The paper's estimate is historical and method-sensitive: the 25% calculation uses
an import-consumption gap, while the later ONEM series shows domestic refining also supplied a
material share. These figures cannot describe current smuggling volumes.

## Root causes

The paper attributes informal trade incentives to fuel-price and tax differences between the
countries. Its quantified estimates depend on assumptions and dated inputs.

## Evidence

Method and assumptions are reproduced in SRC-TG-310; its notes identify the limitations.

## Why confidence is what it is

Low: tier-B estimate, dated around 2012-2013, with a gap-based method and assumed van activity.

## Links to other sectors

Informal fuel trade links border commerce, transport fuel supply and public revenue.

## Leads for phase 2

- None identified in this historical estimate.

## Verification status

Draft retained. Outstanding exact issue: the estimate concerns circa 2012-2013 and depends on assumptions about fuel imports, refining and border-van trips; the paper itself does not establish a current informal-import share.
