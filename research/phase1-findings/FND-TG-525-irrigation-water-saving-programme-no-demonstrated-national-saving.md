## Problem

Since 1995 Tunisia's main irrigation demand-management policy has been the national irrigation water-saving programme (PNEEI): subsidies for farmers to install drip, sprinkler and improved-gravity equipment on their plots. On its own indicator it is a success. The equipped share of irrigated perimeters went from 37% in 1995 to 75% in 2005 and 94.5% in 2021 (SRC-TG-403), and reached 96.9% (428,500 of 442,500 ha) in 2024 (SRC-TG-530). Drip went from 8% of the equipped area in 1995 to 48% in 2017 (SRC-TG-402, p. 68). The programme cost 1,692 MTND from 1995 to 2023, 768 MTND of it subsidies (SRC-TG-529). Over the same period, though, irrigated area and irrigation water use did not fall. Irrigable area rose from 328,000 ha in 1993 to 397,000 ha in 2003 (SRC-TG-430), 435,000 ha in 2017 (SRC-TG-402) and 442,500 ha in 2024 (SRC-TG-530). The ministry's estimate of agricultural water demand rose from 1,386 Mm3 in 1990 to 1,918 Mm3 in 2003 (SRC-TG-430). Later figures, on different methods, are 2.4 billion m3 in 2018 (SRC-TG-504) and about 2.5 billion m3 in dry years (SRC-TG-528). No source we found shows a national water saving from the programme. The only study that tested the question, in one central Tunisian plain, found that drip let farmers extend irrigated land, with "no savings in water at the regional scale", and that this was confirmed in 2005 (SRC-TG-531). The entry-point hypothesis (saved water reused to expand irrigation) is therefore **supported locally and consistent with the national figures, but not proven nationally**.

## Root causes

The symptom is rising irrigation use despite near-universal water-saving equipment. The causes:

1. **A programme that subsidised hectares, not water (policy, 1995).** The PNEEI was "favorisé par la décision relative à l'augmentation de la subvention d'investissement accordée aux équipements d'irrigation modernes" (SRC-TG-402, p. 13). Subsidy rates were 40% (category A) and 50% (category B) until the 2016 Investment Code raised them to 50% and 55% (SRC-TG-402, p. 97). Its national indicator is the share of area equipped (GBO indicator 3.1.1, SRC-TG-529, p. 53). None of the sources read ties the subsidy to a volume cap, a meter reading or a fixed irrigated area. Per-hectare efficiency was the target, while basin-level withdrawal was nobody's indicator.
2. **Water saved on the plot stays with the farmer (institutional, legacy).** Most irrigation water is groundwater: 84.5% in 2003 (SRC-TG-430, p. 11), 84% in 2018 (SRC-TG-504) and 75% in the 2022 report (SRC-TG-528). Access to groundwater is in practice open. The ministry says private perimeters tend to grow through boreholes "réalisés sans autorisation préalable" in Kebili, Central Tunisia and Nabeul, and expects them soon to exceed the public perimeters (SRC-TG-402, p. 66). In 2022 it wrote that the inventory of irrigated perimeters "n'est pas rigoureusement établi", with some perimeters "agrandis sur les initiatives des agriculteurs" (SRC-TG-528, p. 84). With free access to the aquifer, Poussin et al. argue that overexploitation can be prevented only by managing farmers' choices, not by technology alone (SRC-TG-531).
3. **The efficiency gain was taken as income, as intended (economic).** Already in 2007 the ministry reported that agricultural water demand "ne cesse de s'accroître" through intensification on existing perimeters and "l'extension concomitante des superficies nouvellement aménagées". In the same period value added per m3 rose by 29% (1990-2003) (SRC-TG-430, p. 12). The programme raised water productivity; it did not lower water use.
4. **A second subsidy removed the energy cost of pumping (policy, 2010s).** Solar pumping subsidies (FTE via ANME, FTI via APIA) spread from about 2010 (SRC-TG-532). The ministry says they push towards waste by making pumped water "totalement gratuite" (SRC-TG-402, already extracted). It reports more waste in Kebili oases after solar was installed, especially on illicit boreholes that cannot get a grid connection (SRC-TG-402, p. 89). A GIZ survey of 20 subsidised farms found that almost 90% pump more water than before. A third used the systems to bring arid or unused land into irrigation, and most removed or stopped using the meters required for the subsidy after the inspection visit (SRC-TG-532). All surveyed farms already used drip, so solar pumping compounds the drip rebound rather than replacing it. The overdraft effect itself is FND-TG-400's subject; this finding adds the expansion mechanism.

## Evidence

- **Equipment rate:** 37% (1995), 67% (2001), 75% (2005), 92% (2017), 94.5% (2021) (SRC-TG-403, p. 85; extract to add). 421,000 ha or 95.2% in 2022 (SRC-TG-528, p. 80), 424,700 of 442,000 ha in 2023 (SRC-TG-529, p. 53), 428,500 of 442,500 ha in 2024 (SRC-TG-530, p. 58). Investment rose from 1,258 MTND (616 MTND subsidy) by 2017 (SRC-TG-402, p. 97) to 1,672 MTND (759 MTND subsidy) by 2021 (SRC-TG-528, p. 80) and 1,692 MTND (768 MTND) by 2023 (SRC-TG-529). Note that SRC-TG-403 gives 1,665 MTND to 2021, close to but not the same as SRC-TG-528's 1,672 MTND.
- **Area:** irrigable area under full control 328,000 ha (1993), 361,000 (1995), 376,000 (2000), 397,000 (2003). Actually irrigated: 279,000, 315,000, 345,000, 348,000 ha (SRC-TG-430, Tableau 7, p. 11; extract to add). 435,000 ha in 2017 (SRC-TG-402, p. 65); 442,000 ha in 2022 (SRC-TG-528); 442,500 ha in 2024 (SRC-TG-530). Growth is +35% from 1993 to 2024 (derived). It was fast before 2003 and slow after 2017, when "tous les grands périmètres ont été faits" (SRC-TG-402, p. 65). The official area totals may miss illicit private extensions (SRC-TG-528, p. 84), so the slowdown after 2017 may be partly an artefact of the inventory.
- **Water use (sources disagree or measure different things; all kept):**
  - Ministry demand estimates: 1,386 Mm3 (1990) and 1,918 Mm3 (2003), +38% (SRC-TG-430, Tableau 8).
  - 2.4 billion m3 for 406,540 ha in 2018 (SRC-TG-504).
  - About 2.5 billion m3, consumption "over a set of dry years" (SRC-TG-528, p. 76).
  - 3,382 Mm3, the Eau 2050 "stabilised" irrigation demand for 2020 (SRC-TG-503), a modelled demand, not a measurement.
  - WDI/AQUASTAT: total withdrawals × agriculture share gives about 2.72 (1990), 2.13 (2000), 2.62 (2017) and 2.92 (2020) billion m3 (derived from SRC-TG-533). The series is largely interpolated, and its 1990 value is twice the ministry's.
  - Per hectare: 5,500 m3/ha in 2003 (SRC-TG-430). The 2018 figure (2.4 billion m3 / 406,540 ha) gives about 5,900 m3/ha (derived). So average use per irrigated hectare did not visibly fall after two decades of equipment, though the two figures use different methods.
- **Deep-aquifer pressure grew in parallel:** exploitation rose from 82% (1996) to 134% (2020) (SRC-TG-403, in FND-TG-400).
- **Local mechanism:** Central Tunisia, simulation on 1999 farm typologies confirmed with 2005 typologies: drip saved water per plot, enabled extension of drip-irrigated land, and gave "no savings in water at the regional scale" (SRC-TG-531).
- **Representativeness:** the national figures show co-movement, not causation. Part of the area growth came from new public perimeters on dams and boreholes (e.g. 4 new PPIs on boreholes in 2022, SRC-TG-528). The rebound mechanism is shown directly only for groundwater-irrigated private farms in the centre (SRC-TG-531, SRC-TG-532). That is the segment where the ministry reports unauthorised expansion (SRC-TG-402).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The equipment and area series come from the ministry's own reports (tier A) and agree with each other. The claim that saved water funded expansion rests on one peer-reviewed regional study, read as abstract only (SRC-TG-531). It is backed by a 20-farm survey on solar pumping (SRC-TG-532, tier B) and by the ministry's qualitative statements about unauthorised expansion. The volume figures are not one series and cannot show a trend on a single method. No source claims that the PNEEI saved water nationally, and the ministry's own PNEEI evaluation was not found. The finding is therefore framed as "no demonstrated national saving; rebound shown locally". The trend is `worsening` because irrigation demand is still above what Eau 2050 targets (3,382 to 2,502 Mm3, SRC-TG-503), and both subsidies are still running.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture:** the programme is an agricultural investment subsidy (APIA). It raised water productivity (+29% value added per m3, 1990-2003, SRC-TG-430) and income, which is what drove the extension (SRC-TG-531). The ministry links overexploitation to high-value export crops (dates, early vegetables) (SRC-TG-402, extracted in that record).
- **Energy:** drip under pressure needs pumping, and the solar-pumping subsidy removes its marginal cost (SRC-TG-402, SRC-TG-532). Grid-connected solar pumps are not viable because the electricity tariff for agricultural pumping is itself highly subsidised (SRC-TG-532). Irrigation by CRDAs, GDAs and farmers uses about 100 GWh/yr of electricity (SRC-TG-528, p. 89); private diesel and off-grid pumping are not counted in that figure.
- **Groundwater:** the overdraft the extension feeds is FND-TG-400 and FND-TG-401.

- Eau 2050 demand axis: rehabilitation, efficiency, deficit irrigation (ICD) on 140,000 ha (SRC-TG-503 document).
- Smart irrigation, irrigation scheduling and the INGC IREY app for cereals (SRC-TG-402).
- GIZ recommendations: size solar systems to the authorised flow, mandatory storage and water-management plans, real-time pump monitoring, mapping of all operating wells, collective solar pumping (SRC-TG-532).
- "Etudes de Caractérisation des Exploitations Irriguées" recommended by Eau 2050 (SRC-TG-528, p. 84).
- Updating the agricultural map (carte agricole) and targeting subsidies to low-water crops (SRC-TG-530, SRC-TG-402).
- AGIRE (GIZ) short training for farmers on water saving with CFPA Kairouan (SRC-TG-402).
