## Problem

Since 1995, Tunisia's PNEEI has supported on-farm water-saving equipment. The reported equipped share rose from 37% in 1995 to 75% in 2005 and 94.5% in 2021 (SRC-TG-403), then reached 96.9% (428,500 of 442,500 ha) in 2024 (SRC-TG-530). Drip irrigation rose from 8% of equipped area in 1995 to 48% in 2017 (SRC-TG-402, p. 68). Reported investment totalled 1,692 MTND from 1995 to 2023, including 768 MTND of subsidies (SRC-TG-529). Irrigable area rose from 328,000 ha in 1993 to 397,000 ha in 2003 (SRC-TG-430), 435,000 ha in 2017 (SRC-TG-402) and 442,500 ha in 2024 (SRC-TG-530). Ministry water-demand estimates and later consumption or withdrawal values use different methods and cannot establish a continuous national trend (SRC-TG-430, SRC-TG-504, SRC-TG-528, SRC-TG-533). No source reviewed here measures net national water savings attributable to PNEEI. In one Central Tunisia case study, a model found that drip-related expansion in irrigated area offset per-hectare savings at regional scale; the abstract says this was confirmed with 2005 farm typologies (SRC-TG-531). This rebound mechanism is **supported locally, but not demonstrated nationally**.

## Root causes

The evidence shows broad equipment coverage alongside irrigable-area growth, but does not establish a national causal relationship between the two or quantify net water savings attributable to PNEEI. Evidence-supported mechanisms are narrower:

1. **Investment support (policy).** The 2017 report says PNEEI was promoted by a decision increasing investment subsidies for modern irrigation equipment (SRC-TG-402, p. 13). The 2016 Investment Code rates reported for categories A and B rose from 40% and 50% to 50% and 55% (SRC-TG-402, p. 97). The programme indicator is equipped area (SRC-TG-529, p. 53). The reviewed sources do not establish whether the subsidy required a withdrawal cap or fixed area.
2. **Local rebound mechanism (behavioural).** A Central Tunisia model study found that drip-related area expansion offset per-hectare savings at regional scale; its abstract reports confirmation using 2005 farm typologies. This is one regional result, not a national estimate (SRC-TG-531).
3. **Solar-powered irrigation (water-energy link).** In a survey of 20 subsidised systems, almost 90% of surveyed farmers reported pumping more after installation and one third reported cultivating new or unused land. Grid-connected systems were not economically viable under the subsidised electricity tariff, while off-grid systems compared favourably with diesel. The small survey does not establish a national causal effect (SRC-TG-532).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The equipment and area series come from ministry reports (tier A). The local rebound mechanism rests on one peer-reviewed study accessed here through its abstract (SRC-TG-531), and a small self-reported survey (SRC-TG-532, tier B). Water-use figures do not form a comparable national trend, and no national net-savings evaluation was located. The trend is `stable`: available evidence does not establish a consistent direction attributable to PNEEI.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture:** the programme is an agricultural investment subsidy (APIA). A 1990-2003 ministry estimate reports a 29% rise in water productivity (value added per m3; SRC-TG-430). The sources reviewed do not establish that this drove area expansion. The ministry links overexploitation to high-value export crops (dates, early vegetables) (SRC-TG-402, extracted in that record).
- **Energy:** Drip systems require pressurised delivery, and solar-powered systems can alter pumping economics; the 20-farm survey findings are not nationally representative (SRC-TG-532). Grid-connected solar pumps are not viable because the electricity tariff for agricultural pumping is itself highly subsidised (SRC-TG-532). Irrigation by CRDAs, GDAs and farmers uses about 100 GWh/yr of electricity (SRC-TG-528, p. 89); private diesel and off-grid pumping are not counted in that figure.
- **Groundwater:** the overdraft the extension feeds is FND-TG-400 and FND-TG-401.
