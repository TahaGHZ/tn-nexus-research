## Problem

Olive oil and dates dominate Tunisia's agricultural exports: together they were 45% of agricultural export value in 2010 (SRC-TG-860). Of the two, dates depend most on blue water:
- they are fully irrigated (SRC-TG-860);
- they have the highest blue water footprint per tonne among Tunisia's main crops in two separate estimates, 5,000 m3/t for 1981-2010 (SRC-TG-860) and 3,270 m3/t for 1996-2005 (SRC-TG-868), against 180 m3/t blue for tomatoes for 1981-2010 (SRC-TG-860);
- they are grown only in the south, the most water-scarce part of the country (SRC-TG-860);
- date palms need 15,000 to 20,000 m3/ha per year, against an average of 4,500 m3 per irrigated hectare (SRC-TG-864).

Exports rose from 22,411 t in 2000 to 130,307 t in 2022 (SRC-TG-863), and production from 105,000 t to 369,000 t (SRC-TG-862). That puts about 35% of production on export markets in 2022 (our calculation). The official ONAGRI export effort is similar: 36.1% in 2021, down from 50% in 2011 (SRC-TG-743).

Tunisia is a net virtual water importer overall because of cereal imports (SRC-TG-861). SRC-TG-860 likewise shows net imports for staple crops. Both peer-reviewed studies agree that dates carry large volumes of blue water. They disagree on the rest of the export basket. One finds exported virtual water over 80% blue (SRC-TG-861). The other finds the dominant export, olive oil, mostly green water (SRC-TG-860).

## Root causes

The symptom is a growing volume of irrigation water leaving the country embedded in dates. The causes are mainly choices in irrigation policy, not trade policy alone.

- **Past decision: supply-side irrigation policy.** From 1956 to 2015 the state:
  - raised water mobilisation from 8% to 90% of resources;
  - multiplied the number of boreholes by 11;
  - extended irrigable land from about 65,000 ha to over 496,000 ha;
  - backed this with "very generous" on-farm incentives and supported irrigation water prices (SRC-TG-864).

  The national agricultural studies centre calls exports from irrigated agriculture in general "one of the major justifications" for irrigated agriculture (SRC-TG-864).
- **Past decision: groundwater infrastructure and tolerated extensions in the south.** The government developed groundwater infrastructure to revive and create oases. Private extensions grew from the 2000s, even though individual drilling outside traditional oases was not allowed (SRC-TG-740). The sibling records FND-TG-741 (unauthorised extensions) and FND-TG-740 (Deglet Nour monoculture) cover these in detail; this record does not repeat them.
- **Return per hectare, not per m3, drives the choice.** The return per m3 of blue water on irrigated dates is low, about US$0.11/m3 against US$1.13 for tomatoes and US$0.97 for potatoes (1996-2005). Dates nonetheless rank among the highest returns per hectare (US$3,080/ha), behind oranges and tomatoes (SRC-TG-868). The same authors note that return per hectare explains Tunisian cropping patterns better than return per m3 (SRC-TG-868). With irrigation water prices supported by the state (SRC-TG-864), a farmer gains from the crop that pays most per hectare, even where it pays little per m3. The national studies centre likewise reports weak economic water value in the southern oases because of the trend toward date palm monoculture (SRC-TG-864).

## Evidence

- **Water footprints, 1981-2010 average (AquaCrop):** dates 650 m3/t green, 5,000 blue, 5,600 total; olives 7,100 green, 420 blue, 7,500 total; tomatoes 140 green, 180 blue, 320 total (SRC-TG-860, Table 5).
- **Water footprints, 1996-2005 (Mekonnen and Hoekstra model):** dates 1,030 green, 3,270 blue, 80 grey, 4,390 total m3/t. Dates and olives together make up 47% of the crop blue water footprint (SRC-TG-868). This source comes from the same research group as SRC-TG-860 but uses a different model and period, so it is only partly independent.
- **Trade is not explained by water scarcity.** Over 1981-2010, blue water scarcity did not explain net virtual water trade for any of the six main crops. For dates, each extra 1,000 ha of irrigated land raised net virtual water export by 5.6 Mm3 (SRC-TG-860). The authors note that the reason lies outside their variables, such as agricultural policies, the value of water and international prices (SRC-TG-860).
- **Share of virtual water exports.** Olive oil, dates, wheat and tomatoes made up 72% of crop-related virtual water export over 1996-2005, 68% for olive oil alone (SRC-TG-860).
- **2013 export flows.** Exports of 145,900 t of olive oil and 97,800 t of dates carried 1,079.6 and 283.6 million m3 of virtual water, and more than 80% of exported virtual water was blue (SRC-TG-861). FAOSTAT records 105,803 t of date exports for 2013, so the implied water per tonne is approximate.
- **Production and area.** Dates rose from 105,000 t (2000) to 174,000 t (2010) and 369,000 t (2022), all official FAOSTAT figures (SRC-TG-862). Harvested area was 31,610 ha in 2000 (official). FAOSTAT area flags are official for most years up to 2016 (61,240 ha), except 2010 and 2012. From 2017 they are imputed or estimated (SRC-TG-862).
- **Area sources disagree.** The ministry figure relayed by ONAGRI is 57,385 ha in 2022, of which 38,000 ha in Kebili (SRC-TG-743). FAOSTAT imputes 77,404 ha for 2022, about 35% higher (SRC-TG-862). The ministry figure is preferred for the level; both are recorded.
- **Export share sources disagree:**
  - 35% in 2022, from our FAOSTAT calculation (SRC-TG-862, SRC-TG-863);
  - 36.1% in 2021 and a 2011-2021 average of 44.4%, from ONAGRI (SRC-TG-743);
  - 60%, from an NGO report (SRC-TG-744), checked against the Wayback Machine copy because the live URL was unreachable; no method is given.
- **Exports in tonnes and value.** 22,411 t (2000), 84,282 t (2010), 130,307 t (2022), 129,929 t (2023). Export value was US$38.6 million in 2000 and US$256.7 million in 2023 (SRC-TG-863).
- **Sources disagree on the water content of dates and of the export basket.** For dates, the estimates are:
  - 5,600 m3/t total, 5,000 blue (SRC-TG-860);
  - 4,390 m3/t total, 3,270 blue (SRC-TG-868);
  - about 2,900 m3 per exported tonne, implied by the 2013 figure (SRC-TG-861).

  For olive oil, SRC-TG-860 finds olives mostly green water (7,100 green against 420 blue m3/t). SRC-TG-861 calls olive oil the most water-demanding commodity at 7 m3/kg "due to the need of blue water for its processing". The studies use different methods (AquaCrop, a global model, farm surveys) and periods. All values are recorded; none is preferred.
- **Projection.** A sector-level CGE model (conference paper, not peer-reviewed) projects:
  - agricultural output 7.39% to 19.87% lower by 2050 under 5-14% less water availability;
  - lower agricultural exports.

  It names dates, olive oil, tomatoes and citrus as the main export products, and cites yield declines of 2% per year for tomatoes and dates over 2021-2050 (SRC-TG-866). It does not project the crop mix, so this record stays `current`.

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The direction of the finding is solid:
- three estimates from two peer-reviewed studies and one research report agree that dates have the largest blue water footprint per tonne among the main crops (SRC-TG-860, SRC-TG-861, SRC-TG-868);
- official FAOSTAT and ONAGRI figures show the export growth (SRC-TG-862, SRC-TG-863, SRC-TG-743).

Confidence is not high because:
- the studies disagree on whether the export basket as a whole is mostly blue water (olive oil is the swing item);
- the per-tonne water content of dates ranges from about 2,900 to 5,600 m3/t across estimates;
- the footprint series end in 2010 (model) and 2013 (survey);
- FAOSTAT has imputed or estimated the date area since 2017, and its 2022 figure is about 35% above the ministry's;
- the export share is our own calculation from FAOSTAT, and one source's figure (60%, NGO) gives no method.

The policy causes rest on a tier B national report (SRC-TG-864), a tier B research report (SRC-TG-868) and a peer-reviewed study of Kebili (SRC-TG-740). No official text on export incentives for dates was opened.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water:** dates draw on the southern aquifers. SRC-TG-864 describes intensive use of "near-fossil" water and illegal extensions in Kebili. Aquifer overdraft itself belongs to the water lane; this record covers only the crop and export choice.
- **Energy:** borehole pumping in the oases uses energy, but no figure was opened for this topic (none sourced).

## Leads for phase 2

- Crop water allocation by virtual water content and bioclimatic zone, as proposed in SRC-TG-861 and SRC-TG-864 ("import water-intensive products, export low-water high-value ones").
- Programme national d'économie d'eau en irrigation (PNEEI), evaluated by SCET Tunisie 2016 (cited in SRC-TG-864).
- Water use efficiency scenarios (network rehabilitation, irrigation modernisation, less water-intensive varieties) modelled in SRC-TG-866.
- FOPRODEX export transport support (Fonds de promotion des exportations), seen in press only.
- Higher charges for blue water in export value chains, suggested in SRC-TG-861.
- Expanding irrigation only for crops with high water productivity and a large irrigated-versus-rainfed land productivity gap, suggested in SRC-TG-868.
