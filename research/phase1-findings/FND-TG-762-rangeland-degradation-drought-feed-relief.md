## Problem

Tunisia's rangelands cover about 5.5 million ha, 87% of it in arid and desert zones. They are the main feed source of traditional sheep and goat systems (SRC-TG-768). Their area fell from 6.1 million ha in 2005 to 5.5 million ha in 2012, and degradation is estimated to cost 224 million TND a year, 21.1% of the economic value they generate (SRC-TG-767). ICARDA states that rangelands supplied about 80% of national livestock feed in 1980 and about 20% by 2022 (SRC-TG-769; tier C, low confidence). Tunisia's fourth national communication to the UNFCCC puts the current share lower, at about 16% of herd needs in 2020. It projects that climate change will cut it to 8-10% by 2050 (SRC-TG-885). The two sources disagree on the current level (20% vs 16%) but agree on a small and falling share. The national flock is estimated at 5.18 million breeding ewes and 1.25 million breeding goats in 2024 (SRC-TG-762). As the range supplies less, herders turn to barley. USDA notes barley is used "especially when and where rangelands are stressed" (SRC-TG-704). In drought years that barley is largely imported: production was 90 thousand t against 965 thousand t of use in MY 2023/24 (SRC-TG-763; see FND-TG-760).

## Root causes

Lower biomass and bare ground are symptoms. The record points to these causes:

- **Past decision: drought feed relief.** Since at least the late 1980s, governments in the region, Tunisia included, have answered droughts with subsidised barley and concentrates, usually distributed by parastatals (SRC-TG-770). In Tunisia this prevented heavy losses. In the 1988-89 drought the World Bank estimated that a 74 million TND programme avoided producer losses of 119.7 million TND, and sheep numbers "rose consistently" through the droughts of the early 1990s (SRC-TG-770). IFPRI argues that such relief "probably accelerated rangeland degradation in the long term". It broke the traditional adjustment of flock size to rainfall, and animals stayed on the range through the dry season with feed and water trucked in (SRC-TG-770; MENA-wide, hedged). The 2017 ICARDA/OEP workshop lists "inappropriate policies to safeguard the herd during drought years" among the direct causes of degradation in Tunisia (SRC-TG-767). Current public money for rangelands still goes to livestock subsidies "mostly oriented towards inputs" rather than payment for range services (SRC-TG-767).
- **Past decision: barley prices and cropping.** High procurement prices for barley encouraged mechanised cultivation of rangeland where it cannot last (SRC-TG-770; regional). Clearing for cropping is listed as a direct driver in Tunisia (SRC-TG-767).
- **Institutions and tenure.** Underlying causes include complex land tenure, unsuitable laws, top-down administration and deep changes in pastoral societies (SRC-TG-767). The traditional Bedouin-led grazing system has been dismantled (SRC-TG-769). Only 33% of pastoral land is covered by the forest code (SRC-TG-767). See FND-TG-781 on collective land.
- **Climate and stocking.** Successive droughts and climate change (SRC-TG-767), and larger livestock numbers (SRC-TG-769).

## Evidence

- Area 6.1 million ha (2005) to 5.5 million ha (2012) (SRC-TG-767). The ICARDA brief gives the same loss of "some 600,000 ha" over 2005-2012 (SRC-TG-768). Both trace to Croitoru and Daly (2015) for the DGF and the World Bank, so they are one underlying source. The brief's "34,000 ha per year" does not match 600,000 ha over seven years (about 86,000 ha a year) and is not used.
- Degradation cost 49.8 TND/ha/yr, or 224 million TND/yr (SRC-TG-767).
- Forage now makes up only about 18% of the economic value of rangelands, a share of value, not of feed (SRC-TG-767).
- Feed share of rangelands: about 80% in 1980 and about 20% in 2022 (SRC-TG-769). About 16% in 2020, projected at 8-10% by 2050 (SRC-TG-885; footnote cites the World Bank CCDR, then forthcoming). See FND-TG-880 for the climate projections behind it.
- Drought relief scale. The 1988-89 programme cost 74 million TND, 6.9% of agricultural value added (SRC-TG-770). Animal feed subsidies peaked at 49 million TND in the 1989 drought and fell to 4 million TND in 1992-93 (SRC-TG-921).
- **Sources disagree on 1994.** IFPRI says "some 500,000 tons of heavily subsidized concentrate were fed to livestock under the program in Tunisia" in the 1994 drought (SRC-TG-770). The World Bank's ASAL II completion report says there was "no backtracking" on input subsidy reductions during the 1994 and 1995 droughts (SRC-TG-921). These could both hold if the 1994 relief was an ad hoc drought operation outside the regular input subsidy line, but neither source says so. Both are recorded.
- Restoration by resting: the 1990 National Strategy for Rangeland Management bans grazing on degraded areas for three years and compensates herders. In Tataouine about 41,850 ha were restored, but herders say the compensation does not cover lost feed access and pushes them to "costly feed alternatives" (SRC-TG-768).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The area loss and degradation cost come from a DGF and World Bank study cited by two ICARDA documents (tier B), effectively one source. The feed-share collapse from 80% to 20% is the most striking number but rests on a single communications page without method (tier C), so that indicator is low confidence. The current level of about 16-20% is supported by two sources, one of them official (SRC-TG-885). The causal role of drought relief is plausible and named by the Tunisian workshop. The mechanism itself comes from an IFPRI discussion paper that is regional and hedged ("probably"), not a Tunisian measurement. The 1994 relief volume is contested between IFPRI and the World Bank. No post-2012 area or condition data were found. The only quantified projection (8-10% feed share by 2050) has no method stated in the extract.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water:** IFPRI notes that degradation concentrates near water and feed supply points, where trucked-in water keeps animals on the range in the dry season (SRC-TG-770). Water development is part of the drought relief package (SRC-TG-770). Degraded range also feeds wind erosion (SRC-TG-770), linking to soil (FND-TG-800). No Tunisian source quantifying water points and grazing pressure was found.
- **Energy:** none found.

## Leads for phase 2

- National Strategy for Rangeland Management (1990) and resting with compensation ("gdel"/"gdal" practice), OEP (SRC-TG-768)
- Controlled rapid grazing and flexible opening rules (60-80 mm rain, 40% cover, 30% palatable species) piloted by ICARDA and OEP (SRC-TG-769)
- Stratégie nationale de développement et de gestion durable des forêts et parcours 2015-2024 and its successor SNDDFP 2026-2050 (seen in search results, not opened)
- Draft pastoral code (code pastoral) demanded at the 2017 workshop (SRC-TG-767)
- Payment for ecosystem services and cactus or shrub plantations listed as investment options (SRC-TG-767)
- PRODESUD programme and socio-territorial units for collective rangeland management (SRC-TG-767)
- Area-based rainfall insurance proposed by IFPRI as an alternative to feed subsidies (SRC-TG-770, ageconsearch record 16069)
