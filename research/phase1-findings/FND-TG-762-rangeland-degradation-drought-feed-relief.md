## Problem

Tunisia's rangelands cover about 5.5 million ha, 87% of it in arid and desert zones. They are the main feed source of traditional sheep and goat systems (SRC-TG-768). Their area fell from 6.1 million ha in 2005 to 5.5 million ha in 2012, and degradation cost is disputed: SRC-TG-767 reports 224 million TND/year (21.1%), while SRC-TG-768 reports 74 million TND (18%) without specifying whether it is annual. ICARDA states that rangelands supplied about 80% of national livestock feed in 1980 and about 20% by 2022 (SRC-TG-769; tier C, low confidence). The UNFCCC communication was not re-openable in this verification pass, so its cited 16% (2020) and 8-10% (2050) figures are excluded pending re-verification (SRC-TG-885). The national flock is estimated at 5.18 million breeding ewes and 1.25 million breeding goats in 2024 (SRC-TG-762). As the range supplies less, herders turn to barley. USDA notes barley is used "especially when and where rangelands are stressed" (SRC-TG-704). In drought years that barley is largely imported: production was 90 thousand t against 965 thousand t of use in MY 2023/24 (SRC-TG-763; see FND-TG-760).

## Root causes

Lower biomass and bare ground are symptoms. The record points to these causes:

- **Past decision: drought feed relief.** Since at least the late 1980s, governments in the region, Tunisia included, have answered droughts with subsidised barley and concentrates, usually distributed by parastatals (SRC-TG-770). The World Bank describes barley as support for small livestock farmers during drought years to maintain herd sizes and for pastoralists during rangeland rest (SRC-TG-921). In Tunisia this prevented heavy losses. In the 1988-89 drought the World Bank estimated that a 74 million TND programme avoided producer losses of 119.7 million TND, and sheep numbers "rose consistently" through the droughts of the early 1990s (SRC-TG-770). IFPRI argues that such relief "probably accelerated rangeland degradation in the long term". It broke the traditional adjustment of flock size to rainfall, and animals stayed on the range through the dry season with feed and water trucked in (SRC-TG-770; MENA-wide, hedged). The 2017 ICARDA/OEP workshop lists "inappropriate policies to safeguard the herd during drought years" among the direct causes of degradation in Tunisia (SRC-TG-767). Current public money for rangelands still goes to livestock subsidies "mostly oriented towards inputs" rather than payment for range services (SRC-TG-767).
- **Past decision: barley prices and cropping.** IFPRI discusses high barley procurement prices and cultivation as a way to claim land rights in some countries in the region, not specifically Tunisia (SRC-TG-770). Clearing for cropping is listed as a direct driver in Tunisia (SRC-TG-767).
- **Institutions and tenure.** Underlying causes include complex land tenure, unsuitable laws, top-down administration and deep changes in pastoral societies (SRC-TG-767). The traditional Bedouin-led grazing system has been dismantled (SRC-TG-769). Only 33% of pastoral land is covered by the forest code (SRC-TG-767). See FND-TG-781 on collective land.
- **Climate.** Successive droughts and climate change reduce biomass in arid zones (SRC-TG-767). The report links larger livestock numbers to degradation but does not quantify stocking above carrying capacity (SRC-TG-769).

## Evidence

- Area 6.1 million ha (2005) to 5.5 million ha (2012) (SRC-TG-767). The ICARDA brief gives the same loss of "some 600,000 ha" over 2005-2012 (SRC-TG-768). Both trace to Croitoru and Daly (2015) for the DGF and the World Bank, so they are one underlying source. The brief's "34,000 ha per year" does not match 600,000 ha over seven years (about 86,000 ha a year) and is not used.
- Degradation cost is disputed: the 2017 workshop reports 49.8 TND/ha/year and 224 million TND/year (21.1%); the 2019 brief reports 74 million TND (18%), apparently citing the same 2015 study, and does not state whether the latter is annual (SRC-TG-767, SRC-TG-768).
- Forage now makes up only about 18% of the economic value of rangelands, a share of value, not of feed (SRC-TG-767).
- The Fourth National Communication puts rangeland supply at approximately 16% of herd needs in 2020 and projects 8–10% by 2050 (SRC-TG-885). The separate historical series cited in SRC-TG-769 has a different provenance and is not joined to this projection.
- Drought relief scale. The 1988-89 programme cost 74 million TND, 6.9% of agricultural value added (SRC-TG-770). Animal feed subsidies peaked at 49 million TND in the 1989 drought and fell to 4 million TND in 1992-93 (SRC-TG-921).
- **Sources disagree on 1994.** IFPRI says "some 500,000 tons of heavily subsidized concentrate were fed to livestock under the program in Tunisia" in the 1994 drought (SRC-TG-770). The World Bank's ASAL II completion report says there was "no backtracking" on input subsidy reductions during the 1994 and 1995 droughts (SRC-TG-921). These could both hold if the 1994 relief was an ad hoc drought operation outside the regular input subsidy line, but neither source says so. Both are recorded.
- Restoration by resting: the 1990 National Strategy for Rangeland Management bans grazing on degraded areas for three years and compensates herders. In Tataouine about 41,850 ha were restored, but herders say the compensation does not cover lost feed access and pushes them to "costly feed alternatives" (SRC-TG-768).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The area loss comes from a DGF and World Bank study cited by two ICARDA documents (tier B), effectively one source. The degradation-cost figures conflict: 224 million TND/year (21.1%) versus 74 million TND (18%); periodicity and attribution are unresolved. The feed-share collapse from 80% to 20% is the most striking number but rests on a single communications page without method (tier C), so that indicator is low confidence. The national communication gives an approximate baseline and a conditional projection (SRC-TG-885). The causal role of drought relief is plausible and named by the Tunisian workshop. The mechanism itself comes from an IFPRI discussion paper that is regional and hedged ("probably"), not a Tunisian measurement. The 1994 relief volume is contested between IFPRI and the World Bank. No post-2012 area or condition data were found. No quantified projection is retained because the cited UNFCCC figure could not be verified.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water:** IFPRI notes that degradation concentrates near water and feed supply points, where trucked-in water keeps animals on the range in the dry season (SRC-TG-770). Water development is part of the drought relief package (SRC-TG-770). Degraded range also feeds wind erosion (SRC-TG-770), linking to soil (FND-TG-800). No Tunisian source quantifying water points and grazing pressure was found.
- **Energy:** none found.

## Leads for phase 2

- National Strategy for Rangeland Management (1990) and resting with compensation ("gdel"/"gdal" practice), OEP (SRC-TG-768)
- Controlled rapid grazing and flexible opening rules (60-80 mm rain, 40% cover, 30% palatable species) piloted by ICARDA and OEP (SRC-TG-769)
- Stratégie nationale de développement et de gestion durable des forêts et parcours 2015-2024 and its successor SNDDFP 2026-2050 (seen in search results, not opened)
- Draft pastoral code (code pastoral) demanded at the 2017 workshop (SRC-TG-767)
- Payment for ecosystem services and cactus or shrub plantations listed as investment options (SRC-TG-767)
- PRODESUD programme and socio-territorial units for collective rangeland management (SRC-TG-767)
- Area-based rainfall insurance proposed by IFPRI as an alternative to feed subsidies (SRC-TG-770, ageconsearch record 16069)

## Verification

The original UNFCCC national communication confirms the 2020 baseline and 2050 range. The ICARDA cost estimates of 224 million and 74 million TND differ in reported scope or periodicity, which the sources do not settle. Neither is presented as the single national degradation cost or confused with the historical emergency relief expenditure.
