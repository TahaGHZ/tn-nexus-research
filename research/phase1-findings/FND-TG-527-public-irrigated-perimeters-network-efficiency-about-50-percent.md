## Problem

Water delivered in bulk to Tunisia's public irrigated perimeters (PPI, about 250,000 ha in 2022, SRC-TG-528) does not reach the farm hydrants. In the perimeters diagnosed by the ministry's tariff study, efficiency between the bulk delivery point and irrigators' hydrants ranged from 38% to 100% and averaged 59% over 2010-2016. The norm for pressurised networks is about 10% losses (SRC-TG-402, p. 71). In 2022 the ministry put network efficiency in the PPIs it is rehabilitating at "près de 50 %", with a target of 80% (SRC-TG-528, p. 78). The ministry says most of the gap is not physical leakage. It is "pertes administratives": water not billed because meters fail or have been tampered with, or because of theft. Only about 40% of irrigators have meters, after waves of vandalism, especially after 2011 (SRC-TG-402, p. 71). On the farm, a 2007 ministry report put efficiency often at 50-60% (SRC-TG-430, p. 12). The problem matters more in drought years. When dam quotas are cut (FND-TG-526), losses between quota and hydrant decide whether orchards survive. The ministry notes that downstream CRDAs get significantly less than their quota because of the old infrastructure (SRC-TG-528, p. 76).

## Root causes

- **Symptom:** unbilled and undelivered water, debts, perimeters that cannot be run on quotas. The causes are the following.
- **Metering collapse and theft (institutional).** Vandalism and water theft "accroissent notablement la surexploitation des eaux" and unbalance the irrigation service's accounts. Meters were destroyed in post-2011 vandalism waves. The CRDAs now penalise offenders, but they lack the means for systematic re-metering campaigns, and users "se sont accoutumés à bénéficier d'une eau quasi gratuite" (SRC-TG-402, p. 71). Where meters are missing, water is sold at flat rates, which encourages waste (SRC-TG-402, extracted in that record; see FND-TG-477 for tariffs).
- **Under-funded maintenance of an ageing network (economic, legacy).** The PPIs contain 1,358 small and medium pumping stations, 90 large stations and 21,000 km of drainage (SRC-TG-528, p. 78). In 2022, 22.2 MTND went to maintenance and 18.9 MTND to operating improvements (boreholes, meters, tracks), but about half of the improvement actions were postponed for lack of credit (SRC-TG-528, p. 78). The 2017 report warned that rehabilitated networks have a "durée de vie modeste" (SRC-TG-402, p. 70).
- **Rehabilitation that runs late (institutional).** In 2022, 58,000 ha of PPI were under rehabilitation or new development, costing 909 MTND. The ministry says these projects "accusent généralement des retards considérables". The reasons it gives are late studies and procurement, land expropriation disputes, contract terminations, costly materials, beneficiaries refusing to co-finance, and the late creation of a public management company for PIAIT. The 9,000 ha central PPI project (Kairouan, Kasserine, Sidi Bouzid), planned for 2018-2022, was only 40% complete. PIAIT was being restructured to fund cost overruns on works at the expense of its crop-valorisation component (SRC-TG-528, pp. 77-78).
- **On-farm management (technical).** The 2017 report lists pressure variations, leaks at connections, non-compliant equipment, and fertigation rules that farmers do not know. It also cites no record of volumes and not knowing crop water needs, which leads to over-irrigation (SRC-TG-402, p. 71). Farmers often buy low-quality equipment on the informal market (SRC-TG-402, p. 72, citing the PNEEI evaluation). So high equipment rates (FND-TG-525) do not translate into high efficiency.

## Evidence

- Efficiency in diagnosed PPIs: 38-100%, mean 59%, 2010-2016 (SRC-TG-402, p. 71). About 50%, with a target of 80%, in PPIs under rehabilitation in 2022 (SRC-TG-528, p. 78). These are different samples and definitions and are not a time series.
- Large northern PPIs: in 2007 the ministry reported the "situation paradoxale" that irrigation demand "dépasse rarement 60% des allocations" (SRC-TG-430, p. 12). In 2021-22, 84% of the dam quota was consumed (SRC-TG-528). By then quotas were far below needs, so under-use of allocations had turned into shortage (FND-TG-526).
- Utilisation and intensification of the perimeters: the 2017 report calls water use in PPIs "insatisfaisante", with under-use of equipped areas and low intensification (SRC-TG-402, p. 72). The 2024 report gives an average intensification rate of about 90%, "très variable d'un périmètre à un autre" (SRC-TG-530, p. 7). **These two statements disagree**, and the definitions are not given. Both are recorded.
- Coverage: rehabilitation projects concern public perimeters only. Private perimeters, "close to 50%" of irrigated area, are not covered (SRC-TG-529, p. 83).

**Representativeness.** The 59% figure comes from a sample of perimeters diagnosed in a tariff study. The 50% figure applies to perimeters selected for rehabilitation, which are probably the worst. Neither is a census of the roughly 1,350 PPIs (SRC-TG-402, p. 65). The direction is consistent across three ministry reports from 2007, 2017 and 2022.

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. All figures are the ministry's own (tier A), but the efficiency values rest on samples and a project statement, not on routine measurement. The ministry itself asks for a PPI monitoring system that would record equipped area, actually irrigated area and consumption (SRC-TG-402, p. 73). The split into physical and administrative losses is qualitative. The trend is `stable`: rehabilitation adds about 1,000 ha a year (1,072 ha completed in 2022, SRC-TG-528), while networks keep ageing and half the improvement budget is deferred. There is no evidence of improvement or deterioration at national scale.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture:** losses before the hydrant reduce the water farmers can use under quotas. This adds to the production losses in FND-TG-526.
- **Governance and finance:** unbilled water lowers GDA and CRDA revenue and feeds the debt chain in FND-TG-477. The 2022 report links lack of payment partly to poor service, with breakdowns and interruptions causing crop losses (SRC-TG-528, p. 79).
- **Energy:** pumped PPIs lose energy along with water. CRDAs that run large pumping stations owed STEG 49 MTND in 2022 (SRC-TG-528, p. 79); the debt itself belongs to FND-TG-477.

## Leads for phase 2

- PIAIT (World Bank, northern PPIs, 23,000 ha), with a public management company and two-part tariff (SRC-TG-528, SRC-TG-402).
- Central PPI valorisation project (AfDB, Kairouan, Kasserine, Sidi Bouzid) (SRC-TG-528).
- Lower Medjerda PPI modernisation (Manouba, Ariana) and Mornag integrated water management project (SRC-TG-528).
- Metering campaigns, CRDA penalties for meter tampering, the circular of 22 March 2021 on cutting water for non-payment (SRC-TG-402, SRC-TG-528).
- Institutional options study (public company, mixed company, user group) for PPI management (SRC-TG-402).
- Computerised regional PPI monitoring system (SRC-TG-402).
