## Problem

Tunisian households throw away a large share of the bread they buy. The Institut National de la Consommation (INC) estimated in 2014 that about 900,000 loaves were thrown away each day, equal to 102,000 tonnes and 100 million TND a year (SRC-TG-906). Its May-June 2016 household surveys put bread waste at 42 kg per household a year, 113,000 tonnes in total, or 15.7% of the bread households buy (SRC-TG-906, SRC-TG-907). An INC study published in 2019, cited by the World Bank, gives 16% of bread bought and 10% of cereals; its survey year is not stated (SRC-TG-903). These figures are still quoted unchanged in 2026 (SRC-TG-910), so the current level is not measured. Bread is the most wasted food in every INC figure we found. Total household food waste was valued at 570-572 million TND a year in 2018 (SRC-TG-909, SRC-TG-907). Since over 80% of soft wheat is imported (SRC-TG-901, SRC-TG-907; see FND-TG-700), much of the wasted bread was bought abroad with foreign currency.

## Root causes

**Symptom vs cause.** The 900,000 loaves is the symptom. The evidence names three layers of cause.

1. **Past policy: universal cereal price subsidy (legacy decision).** Consumer price compensation dates from 1945 and the Caisse Générale de Compensation was set up in 1970 (SRC-TG-903; the institutional history is in FND-TG-702). The World Bank's 2022 monitor says the retail bread price has not been raised since 2008, and that consumer prices of subsidised goods were cut symbolically on the eve of 14 January 2011 and not adjusted since (SRC-TG-903). The two statements are compatible. The World Bank concludes that low prices gave Tunisia a world record for wheat consumption per head, more than double the world average, that some bread is fed to livestock because it is cheaper than feed, and that the subsidy structure produces "overconsumption at the household level" and "significant leakages and waste" (SRC-TG-903). The World Bank's 2024 project paper names wastage of 900,000 baguettes daily among the system's distortions (SRC-TG-705). The FAO/ministry chain study says products sold well below cost "incite au gaspillage" (SRC-TG-901).
2. **Supply-side incentives.** Subsidies are paid on volumes, so processors have an incentive to produce more to receive more subsidy (SRC-TG-903). Bakeries buy flour from mills at a negative price and are also subsidised for water, electricity and labour (SRC-TG-903).
3. **Household behaviour and bread quality.** INC respondents name over-buying (87.9%), cooking too much (87.3%) and unplanned purchases (84%) as the main causes (SRC-TG-906). The FAO/ministry study suggests flour quality and bakery processes may also explain bread waste (SRC-TG-901); in 2016, 56% of INC respondents rated subsidised bread quality as average or poor (SRC-TG-906).

**Evidence that complicates the subsidy explanation.** In the same INC survey, 73% of respondents said the current bread price does not encourage them to buy more than they need, and only 1-2% knew the real size of the subsidy on the baguette or "gros pain" (SRC-TG-906). The price link therefore rests on the World Bank's cross-country reasoning and the FAO/ministry SWOT statement, not on household evidence. Low price may work by making waste feel costless rather than by conscious over-buying, but no Tunisian study tests this.

## Evidence

- Bread waste volume, two INC estimates: 102,000 t/year (2014 estimate from 900,000 loaves a day) and 113,000 t/year (2016 surveys, 42 kg per household, 2.7 million households) (SRC-TG-906, SRC-TG-907).
- Share of bread wasted: 15.7% (2016 surveys, SRC-TG-906; SRC-TG-907); 16% (INC study cited for 2019, SRC-TG-903; also in 2026 press, SRC-TG-910).
- Value: 100 million TND a year for bread (SRC-TG-906). Total household food waste: "over 570 million TND" (Minister of Trade, December 2018, SRC-TG-909), 572 million TND (INC via TAP, November 2018, SRC-TG-907), 570 million TND (2026 press, SRC-TG-910).
- **Share of food spending wasted, sources disagree:** 5% of household food spending, or 17 TND per household per month (INC 2016 surveys, SRC-TG-906; repeated in SRC-TG-907, SRC-TG-910) vs 18% of food spending (INC study published in 2019, as cited in a World Bank footnote, SRC-TG-903). We could not see either INC report to reconcile them.
- **UNEP Food Waste Index:** household food waste of 88 kg per capita in 2019 (very low confidence) and 172 kg per capita, 2.12 million tonnes, in 2022 (medium confidence) (SRC-TG-900). The jump is a change of method and confidence class between the 2021 and 2024 editions, not a trend. The UNEP figures include inedible parts and cover all foods, so they are not comparable with INC bread figures. They do suggest household waste in Tunisia is not small.
- **Cost of the subsidy that is wasted:** food subsidy spending rose from 730 million TND in 2010 to 2,569 million TND in 2020, and cereals took over three quarters of direct food subsidies in 2020 (SRC-TG-903). The cereal subsidy line in the FAO/ministry study was 887 million TND in 2011 and 1,286 million TND in 2016 (SRC-TG-901). The bread or bread-flour subsidy was reported at 400 million TND and 450 million TND for 2017 in two INC statements (SRC-TG-906, SRC-TG-907). If 15.7% of bread is wasted, a comparable share of the bread subsidy is spent on bread that is thrown away; this is our inference, not a sourced figure.

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The direction is solid: two independent institutions (World Bank, FAO/ministry) identify subsidised cereal pricing as a driver of waste, and every INC figure ranks bread first. The magnitudes are weaker. Several figures rest only on tier C press reports of INC statements: 102,000 and 113,000 t a year, 42 kg per household, 15.7% of bread, 5% of food spending, 570-572 million TND, and the 400 and 450 million TND bread subsidy figures. Tier B reports (World Bank, FAO/ministry) repeat only the 900,000 loaves a day, 100 million TND and 16% figures. All INC figures reach us second-hand (press, or World Bank and FAO reports citing them), come from surveys of 2014-2016 (2019 at the latest) with unknown samples, and disagree on the share of food spending (5% vs 18%) and on volume (102,000 vs 113,000 t). The UNEP estimate is modelled and its 2022 value rests on a study we could not identify. The subsidy-causes-waste link is contradicted by household self-reports (SRC-TG-906).

## Links to other sectors

- Energy: bakeries' electricity is subsidised as part of the cereal subsidy chain (SRC-TG-903), so subsidised energy goes into bread that is thrown away. No source quantifies this.
- Water: none found. No Tunisian source quantifies the water embedded in wasted bread.
- Waste management: the INC says 68% of household waste is organic and only 5% is recycled (SRC-TG-907); wasted bread adds to this stream.
