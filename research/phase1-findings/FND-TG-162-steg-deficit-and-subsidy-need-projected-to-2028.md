## Verification status

Draft retained. Outstanding exact issue: the draft-budget values rely on tier-C press summaries
without the underlying official PLF 2027 document. The press-only claims lack an independent
primary-source cross-check.

## Problem

The World Bank's appraisal of the 2025-2028 STEG reform programme (April 2025) projects that STEG
will keep losing money before subsidy every year to 2028. For 2026-2028 the projected loss is 3.7
to 3.4 billion dinars a year without the programme and 3.1 to 2.3 billion with the programme fully
delivered; over 2025-2028 it sums to 15.8 and 12.9 billion respectively (SRC-TG-105). The 2025
values (5.1 and 4.7 billion) were forecasts and have not been checked against STEG's 2025 outturn. The analysis assumes no tariff increase. Even in the better case, STEG's liabilities
to suppliers plus debt reach 18.8 billion dinars at end-2028, and tariffs cover 80% of cost
instead of 60% (SRC-TG-105). Near term, the draft 2027 budget plans about 7,090 MDT of
hydrocarbon and electricity subsidies, 3,676 MDT of it for STEG, against 3,138 MDT planned for STEG
in the 2026 draft budget, according to press reports (SRC-TG-166, SRC-TG-167).

## Root causes

- **No tariff path.** The World Bank projection assumes unchanged tariffs while including efficiency and other programme measures (SRC-TG-105). The resulting financing and subsidy needs are not fully specified in the cited passage (see FND-TG-161).
- **Fuel cost exposure and an optimistic gas price path.** The projection's gas price falls from
  10.3 to 7.8 US$/MMBtu over 2025-2028 (SRC-TG-105), and imported gas is partly indexed to Brent
  (SRC-TG-105). In 2026 Brent averaged 91.21 dollars to end-August against a 63.3-dollar budget
  assumption, according to the PLF 2027 as reported (SRC-TG-170). If that persists, the projected
  losses are a lower bound; this is an inference, not a modelled result.
- **Legacy arrears.** The draft 2027 budget includes settling about 500 MDT of STEG claims for
  2017-2023 (SRC-TG-167), and the World Bank programme targets cutting end-2023 subsidy arrears by
  900 MDT by 2028 (SRC-TG-105). These are separate budget and programme targets; the sources do not establish their effect on projected STEG liabilities.
- In the "with programme" case, the World Bank attributes the cost recovery gain to "fuel cost
  reduction, higher share of RE in the power generation mix, and measures supported under the
  Program" (SRC-TG-105, para. 47); see also FND-TG-102.

## Evidence

- PAD Table 5 and paras. 46-47 (SRC-TG-105, p. 18): net results with and without the programme,
  subsidy need reduction of 2,045 MDT over 2025-2028, liabilities of 18.8 billion TND at end-2028,
  cost recovery from 60% to 80%, gas price path, no tariff increase.
- PLF 2026 (press): 3,138 MDT to STEG and 1,855 MDT to STIR, 4,993 MDT total, Brent at 63.3
  dollars (SRC-TG-166).
- PLF 2027 (press): 7,090 MDT (3,676 STEG, 3,414 STIR), Brent at 80 dollars, total subsidies 11,846
  MDT against 9,772 MDT in the LF 2026; financing need of the hydrocarbons, electricity and gas
  system 6,870 MDT; each extra dollar per barrel adds about 172 MDT (SRC-TG-167, SRC-TG-170). An
  expert quoted in press gives 140-150 MDT per dollar; this is not used as a finding claim.

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium for the STEG trajectory: it is a single World Bank projection (tier B) built on STEG's own
model and qualified accounts, with one gas price path and no stress case, and it was made before the
2026 oil price rise. Low for the 2026-2027 budget numbers: they rest only on press summaries (tier
C) of the draft budget. The direction (persistent large losses before subsidy, subsidy need in the
billions of dinars each year) is consistent across all sources; the levels are uncertain. The PLF
2026 figure for STEG (3,138 MDT) was a draft made at a 63.3-dollar Brent assumption and was
overtaken by 2026 prices (SRC-TG-170).
