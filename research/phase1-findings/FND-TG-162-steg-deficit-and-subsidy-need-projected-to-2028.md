## Verification status

The modelled losses in PAD Table 5 and the parliamentary proposal were checked independently as separate measures. No budget figure is presented as an outturn or as STEG’s realised loss. The 2026 comparison and 2027 company split are explicitly secondary.

## Problem

The World Bank's April 2025 appraisal of Tunisia's 2025-2028 TEREG programme models STEG's net result before subsidy and corporate tax under two scenarios. Without TEREG, it forecasts a result of TND -3,720m in 2026 and -3,395m in 2028; with TEREG, TND -3,086m and -2,288m respectively. These correspond to annual projected losses of about TND 3.4-3.7bn without the programme and TND 2.3-3.1bn with it during 2026-2028 (SRC-TG-105). The PAD's 2025 values (-5,103m and -4,691m) are forecasts; this finding does not assert a 2025 outturn. For 2025-2028, the PAD gives undiscounted sums of TND -15,803m without TEREG and -12,852m with it, and estimates subsidy need is TND 2,045m lower with the programme over the same period (SRC-TG-105). The model assumes no tariff increase and a declining gas-price path. Under the with-programme scenario, supplier liabilities plus short- and long-term debt are projected at TND 18.8bn at end-2028, while tariff cost recovery is projected to rise from 60% to 80% (SRC-TG-105).

The parliamentary budget report proposes TND 7,090m for hydrocarbon support in 2027 and TND 11,846m in total subsidies (SRC-TG-174, PDF pp. 51–52). Press sources attribute TND 3,676m of the proposed grant to STEG and TND 3,414m to STIR (SRC-TG-167). The committee confirms the two beneficiaries but does not itemise their amounts in that passage. Appropriations and the PAD’s projected utility losses measure different things.

## Root causes

- **Tariff and cost recovery assumptions.** The World Bank PAD reports that the Government was not considering tariff increases at that stage, citing social vulnerability and economic competitiveness concerns. Its modeled scenarios assume no increase (SRC-TG-105; see FND-TG-161). This is dated policy context and a model assumption, not evidence about subsequent decisions.
- **Fuel cost exposure and uncertain gas-price path.** The PAD forecasts gas prices of US$10.3/MMBtu in 2025, US$8.6 in 2026, US$8.2 in 2027 and US$7.8 in 2028, and says imported gas is partly indexed to Brent (SRC-TG-105). A separate press summary of draft-budget documents reports Brent at US$91.21/bbl through August 2026 against a US$63.3/bbl assumption in the 2026 finance law (SRC-TG-170). These are different commodities and periods; they do not establish an updated STEG gas-cost projection or a lower bound for PAD losses. That sensitivity remains unmodeled in the cited PAD.
- **Legacy arrears and liabilities.** The PAD states that clearing part of past arrears and improving collection would help STEG repay overdue supplier obligations. It projects TND 18.8bn in supplier liabilities and short- and long-term debt at end-2028 with TEREG (SRC-TG-105). La Presse separately reports that the draft 2027 budget proposes settling about TND 500m in STEG arrears for 2017-2023 (SRC-TG-167). These facts document a legacy balance-sheet burden but do not isolate arrears as the cause of modeled annual pre-subsidy losses.
- In the with-programme case, the World Bank attributes improved tariff cost recovery to fuel-cost reduction, a higher renewable share in power generation, and programme measures (SRC-TG-105, para. 47); see also FND-TG-102.

## Evidence

- PAD Table 5 and paragraphs 46-47 (SRC-TG-105, p. 18): annual scenario results, undiscounted 2025-2028 totals, subsidy-need reduction, liabilities, cost recovery, gas-price path and the no-tariff-increase assumption.
- PLF 2026 press report: TND 3,138m proposed for STEG and TND 1,855m for STIR, for TND 4,993m combined; Brent assumption US$63.3/bbl (SRC-TG-166).
- Parliamentary committee report on the 2027 draft budget (SRC-TG-174, PDF pp. 49, 51–52): Brent assumption USD 80/bbl, proposed performance measures TND 600m, and estimated TND 172m extra subsidy for a USD 1/bbl increase. The company split and 2026 comparison remain attributed to the press (SRC-TG-166, SRC-TG-167, SRC-TG-170).

## Why confidence is what it is

The World Bank supplies a scenario projection from a single financial model (SRC-TG-105). The parliamentary report corroborates the aggregate proposed subsidy and oil-price assumption (SRC-TG-174). Confidence is lower for the company split and prior-year draft figures available through press summaries.

## Links to other sectors

The finding concerns STEG and energy-sector public finances; no distinct cross-sector effect is established in the sources reviewed.
