## Problem

Tunisia's main tool for protecting farmland is law 83-87 of 11 November 1983. It sorts agricultural land into prohibition zones, safeguard zones and a residual zone where any change of use needs authorisation. It also orders that new settlements go on the least fertile land (SRC-TG-840, SRC-TG-842). In practice the protection gives way. Safeguard zones are fixed by decree for each governorate and redrawn by decree each time a plot is converted. In 2008, for example, a single decree turned 107 ha of safeguard land in Ben Arous into an industrial zone (SRC-TG-844). A ministry-commissioned diagnosis found that thousands of hectares were declassified one plot at a time, outside urban plans, and that penalties deter little (SRC-TG-845). Since 2019, investment reforms have moved the other way from protection: the minister's authorisation was replaced by a commission opinion with a deadline, then by automatic approval, and industrial projects already built illegally on farmland can now be regularised (SRC-TG-853).

## Root causes

- **Design of the 1983 law (legacy decision).** Only prohibition zones are hard-protected: public irrigated perimeters and state forest, which can change only under their own special laws (SRC-TG-840). Safeguard zones cover oases, irrigated land, olive plantations, fruit-tree areas and fertile or groundwater-rich land. They are defined by decree per governorate, and the law lets them be modified by decree through the same procedure (SRC-TG-840, art. 6). All remaining farmland can change use with the agriculture minister's authorisation by arrêté (SRC-TG-840, art. 8). The 1983 penalties were fines only: 1,200-10,000 DT in prohibition and safeguard zones, 120-1,200 DT elsewhere (SRC-TG-840, art. 14).
- **Amendments tightened enforcement on paper.** Law 90-45 (1990) empowered governorate and municipal agents to record offences and made demolition of unauthorised construction mandatory ("doivent") rather than optional (SRC-TG-841). Law 96-104 (1996) did three things (SRC-TG-842):
  - raised fines to 2,000-20,000 DT for changing the use of farmland and 1,500-15,000 DT for building without a permit;
  - added prison terms (up to 1 year), mandatory in prohibition and safeguard zones;
  - banned non-farm industrial, commercial and service installations on farms.
- **Derogatory practice.** According to the 2014 diagnosis, public operators' sector logic, such as the AFH's need to develop land reserves, overrode planning. Thousands of hectares were declassified case by case, and before 2011 some declassifications served private actors close to power against the administration's opinion. The last two points are the consultant's assessment (SRC-TG-845). The same report says the declassification procedure is long and cannot keep up with informal housing (SRC-TG-845).
- **Recent loosening for investment.** Gazette visas confirm that law 83-87 was amended by law 2019-47 on the investment climate (SRC-TG-843) and by decree-law 2022-68 on speeding up public and private projects (SRC-TG-844). A legal-database summary describes what changed (SRC-TG-853):
  - since 2019 (new article 8 ter), a change of use for a direct-investment project needs only the regional commission's opinion within three months, no longer the minister's authorisation;
  - since 21 October 2022, a request not answered in three months goes to the Investment Authority's commission and is granted automatically;
  - under article 28 of decree-law 2022-68 and an arrêté of 3 May 2023, industrial projects built on farmland before 21 October 2022 without authorisation can be regularised after the fact if they have at least 50 jobs and 1,500 m2 of functional area.
  The underlying texts were not opened.

## Evidence

- **Zoning categories and the "least fertile land" rule:** 1983 original arts. 3-9 (SRC-TG-840); 1996 new arts. 3, 8, 9 (SRC-TG-842).
- **Penalties over time:** 1983, fines only, maximum 10,000 DT (SRC-TG-840). 1996, maximum 20,000 DT and 3 months to 1 year in prison, or either penalty alone; prison is mandatory in prohibition and safeguard zones (SRC-TG-842). **The sources disagree here.** The 2014 ministry diagnosis says prison applies only in prohibition and safeguard zones, and that elsewhere the penalty is financial and capped at 15,000 DT (SRC-TG-845). The gazette text allows prison in every zone and caps the fine at 15,000 DT only for building without a permit (20,000 DT for changing the use of land) (SRC-TG-842). The gazette text prevails, but both readings agree that the amounts are small next to the speculative gain (SRC-TG-845).
- **Enforcement duties:** in 1990 demolition became an obligation (SRC-TG-841). No data on actual demolitions was found.
- **Safeguard zones redrawn case by case:**
  - 2008: decree 2008-157 converts 107.22 ha of safeguard land in El Mhamdia (Ben Arous) for an industrial zone and amends the Ben Arous safeguard limits fixed in 2005 (SRC-TG-844).
  - 2022: decree 2022-57 converts a 1.40 ha safeguard plot in Sakiet Ezzit (Sfax) for an electrical substation and amends the 2007 Sfax safeguard limits (SRC-TG-843).
  - 2024: decree 2024-59 repeals a 2018 decree that had converted two Nabeul plots for a retail store whose permit had expired (SRC-TG-844). Conversion is reversible only on paper if nothing has been built.
- **Procedure duration:** for a 1.33 ha conversion in Mhamdia (Ben Arous), the regional commission gave its opinion on 24 September 2019 and the joint order came on 24 March 2022 (SRC-TG-843). Slow formal procedures are consistent with the 2014 report's statement that informal building gets ahead of them (SRC-TG-845).
- **Planning loopholes:** public land perimeters (PIF) could be created outside urban plans on farmland, e.g. Ennasr II, Ariana (1987, declassified only in 1993) (SRC-TG-845).

## Why confidence is what it is

The legal history from 1983 to 1996 rests on gazette texts (tier A): SRC-TG-840, SRC-TG-841, SRC-TG-842. The case-by-case redrawing of safeguard zones is documented in gazette decrees (SRC-TG-843, SRC-TG-844). The diagnosis of derogatory practice and weak deterrence comes from one ministry-commissioned consultant report (tier B, SRC-TG-845), and its reading of the penalty article differs from the gazette. The content of the 2019-2023 loosening rests on a tier C legal-database summary (SRC-TG-853), although the gazette confirms the amending texts exist. There are no enforcement or conversion statistics, so the effect of the law on the rate of farmland loss cannot be measured. Confidence is medium overall: high for the legal design, low for how it works in practice.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water.** Prohibition zones are defined by public irrigation perimeters, and safeguard zones by irrigation works and exploitable groundwater (SRC-TG-840). Loosening the procedure therefore also exposes land served by public water infrastructure.
- **Energy.** Grid infrastructure is one of the uses for which safeguard land is converted, e.g. a high/medium-voltage substation in Sfax in 2022 (SRC-TG-843). No figure on the share of conversions for energy (including solar plants) was found.
