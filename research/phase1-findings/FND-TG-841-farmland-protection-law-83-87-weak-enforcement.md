## Problem

Tunisia's main tool for protecting farmland is law 83-87 of 11 November 1983. It sorts agricultural land into prohibition zones, safeguard zones and a residual zone where any change of use needs authorisation. It also orders that new settlements go on the least fertile land (SRC-TG-840, SRC-TG-842). In practice the protection gives way:

- **Zones are redrawn case by case.** Safeguard zones are fixed by decree for each governorate and redrawn by decree whenever a plot is converted. In 2008, a single decree turned 107 ha of safeguard land in Ben Arous into an industrial zone (SRC-TG-844).
- **Derogations and weak penalties.** A ministry-commissioned diagnosis found that thousands of hectares were declassified one plot at a time, outside urban plans, and that penalties deter little (SRC-TG-845).
- **Loosening for investment since 2019.** For direct-investment projects, the minister's authorisation was replaced by a regional commission opinion with a three-month deadline. From October 2022, approval is granted "d'office" (automatically) after the deadline, once the investment authority's commission has checked conditions and deadlines. Industrial projects already built illegally on farmland can now be regularised (SRC-TG-853).

## Root causes

**Design of the 1983 law (legacy decision).**
- Only prohibition zones are hard-protected: public irrigated perimeters and state forest, whose use can change only under their own special laws (SRC-TG-840).
- Safeguard zones cover oases, irrigated land, olive plantations, fruit-tree areas, and land that is fertile or rich in groundwater. They are fixed by decree per governorate, and the same decree procedure can modify them (SRC-TG-840, art. 6).
- In 1983, the remaining farmland could change use with the agriculture minister's authorisation by arrêté (SRC-TG-840, art. 8). Law 96-104 replaced article 8: a change of use in the "other agricultural zones" now follows the art. 6 para. 2 procedure, i.e. a decree after the regional commission's opinion (SRC-TG-842). An example is decree 2008-158 for a plot in Gafsa's other agricultural zones (SRC-TG-844).
- By 2022, changes of use in other agricultural zones were again made by joint arrêté of the agriculture and equipment ministers (SRC-TG-843). The amendment that allowed this was not identified.
- The 1983 penalties were fines only: 1,200-10,000 DT in prohibition and safeguard zones, 120-1,200 DT elsewhere (SRC-TG-840, art. 14).

**Amendments on enforcement.**
- Law 90-45 (1990) empowered governorate and municipal agents to record offences. It also made demolition of unauthorised construction mandatory ("doivent") rather than optional (SRC-TG-841).
- Law 96-104 (1996) replaced article 13 again. Building offences are now handled under articles 80-84 of the spatial planning and urbanism code (CATU), with the agriculture minister given the urbanism minister's powers, so the 1990 "doivent" wording is no longer in force (SRC-TG-842).
- The same law raised fines to 2,000-20,000 DT for changing the use of farmland and 1,500-15,000 DT for building without a permit. It added prison terms of up to one year, mandatory in prohibition and safeguard zones. It also banned non-farm industrial, commercial and service installations on farms (SRC-TG-842).

**Derogatory practice.** According to the 2014 diagnosis, public operators' sector logic overrode planning. It cites the AFH's pressure to develop land reserves bought very cheaply in the 1970s. Thousands of hectares were declassified case by case. Before 2011, some declassifications served private actors close to power, against the administration's opinion. These are the consultant's assessments (SRC-TG-845). The same report says the declassification procedure is long and cannot keep up with informal housing (SRC-TG-845).

**Recent loosening for investment.** Gazette visas confirm that law 83-87 was amended by law 2019-47 on the investment climate (SRC-TG-843) and by decree-law 2022-68 on speeding up public and private projects (SRC-TG-844). According to a legal-database summary (SRC-TG-853), the changes apply to direct-investment projects as defined by Investment Law 2016-71:
- Since 2019, a new article 8 ter requires only the regional commission's opinion, due within three months, instead of the minister's authorisation.
- Since 21 October 2022, an unanswered request passes to the Investment Authority's approvals commission. That commission grants the change "d'office" after first checking that the conditions and deadlines were respected.
- Under article 28 of decree-law 2022-68 and an industry minister's arrêté of 3 May 2023, industrial projects built on farmland before 21 October 2022 without authorisation can be regularised after the fact. Conditions include at least 50 jobs and 1,500 m2 of functional area.

The underlying texts were not opened.

## Evidence

- **Zoning and the "least fertile land" rule:** 1983 original arts. 3-9 (SRC-TG-840); 1996 new arts. 3, 8, 9 (SRC-TG-842).
- **Penalties over time:**
  - 1983: fines only, maximum 10,000 DT (SRC-TG-840).
  - 1996: maximum fine of 20,000 DT and/or 3 months to 1 year in prison for changing the use of farmland; prison is mandatory in prohibition and safeguard zones (SRC-TG-842).
  - **The sources disagree.** The 2014 ministry diagnosis says prison applies only in prohibition and safeguard zones and that elsewhere the penalty is a fine capped at 15,000 DT (SRC-TG-845). The gazette text allows prison in every zone, and its 15,000 DT cap applies only to building without a permit; changing the use of land carries up to 20,000 DT (SRC-TG-842). The gazette text prevails. Both readings agree the amounts are small next to the speculative gain (SRC-TG-845).
- **Enforcement:** building offences fall under CATU arts. 80-84 since 1996 (SRC-TG-842). No data on offence reports or demolitions was found.
- **Safeguard and other zones redrawn case by case:**
  - 2008: decree 2008-157 converts 107.22 ha of safeguard land in El Mhamdia (Ben Arous) to an industrial zone and amends the Ben Arous safeguard limits fixed in 2005. Decree 2008-158 converts a plot in Gafsa's other agricultural zones (SRC-TG-844).
  - 2022: decree 2022-57 converts a 1.40 ha safeguard plot in Sakiet Ezzit (Sfax) for an electrical substation and amends the 2007 Sfax safeguard limits (SRC-TG-843).
  - 2024: decree 2024-59 repeals a 2018 decree that had converted two Nabeul plots for a retail store whose permit had expired (SRC-TG-844).
- **Procedure duration, and a conflict with the 2019 reform:** for a 1.33 ha conversion in Mhamdia (Ben Arous), the regional commission gave its opinion on 24 September 2019, after law 2019-47 of 29 May 2019. A joint ministerial arrêté was still issued on 24 March 2022, about 30 months later (SRC-TG-843). This does not fit SRC-TG-853's summary that, since 2019, a change of use for investment projects needs only the commission's opinion. Possible explanations: the project fell outside "direct investment", transitional rules applied, or the summary is incomplete. The texts needed to decide were not opened. Slow formal procedures fit the 2014 report's statement that informal building gets ahead of them (SRC-TG-845).
- **Planning loopholes:** public land perimeters (PIF) could be created outside urban plans on farmland. Example: Ennasr II in Ariana, started in 1987 although the land was declassified only in 1993 (SRC-TG-845).

## Why confidence is what it is

The legal history from 1983 to 1996 rests on gazette texts (tier A: SRC-TG-840, SRC-TG-841, SRC-TG-842). The case-by-case redrawing of zones is documented in gazette decrees (SRC-TG-843, SRC-TG-844). The diagnosis of derogatory practice and weak deterrence comes from a single ministry-commissioned consultant report (tier B, SRC-TG-845), and its reading of the penalty article differs from the gazette. The content of the 2019-2023 loosening rests on a tier C legal-database summary (SRC-TG-853). The gazette confirms that the amending texts exist, and one 2022 case does not fit that summary. Without enforcement or conversion statistics, the law's effect on the rate of farmland loss cannot be measured. Confidence is medium overall: high for the legal design, low for how it works in practice.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water.** Prohibition zones are defined by public irrigation perimeters, and safeguard zones by irrigation works and exploitable groundwater (SRC-TG-840). Loosening the procedure therefore also exposes land served by public water infrastructure.
- **Energy.** Grid infrastructure is one use for which safeguard land is converted, e.g. a high/medium-voltage substation in Sfax in 2022 (SRC-TG-843). No figure on the share of conversions for energy, including solar plants, was found.
