## Problem

Tunisia's state owns about 500,000 ha of agricultural land according to the minister of State Domains in 2026, of which only 38% is operated under lease contracts (SRC-TG-789). A 2024 press breakdown gives 310,000 ha "structured" (leased or allocated) and 190,000 ha "unstructured", mostly run directly by the Office des Terres Domaniales (SRC-TG-788). In 2022 the then minister said more than 50,000 people farm state land without regularised status (SRC-TG-790). In 2026 bill sponsors said the commissions created in 2015 to regularise them had settled no more than 15% of files in ten years (SRC-TG-791). This land, much of it among the most fertile in the north (SRC-TG-786), has been reallocated with every political turn since 1964, and its use and productivity are not tracked in any published inventory we found. The problem is a large, high-quality land reserve whose operators' rights keep changing and, for many, remain unregularised. We found no data on how this affects investment or output on the land.

## Root causes

The symptom is contested, partly idle and partly unregularised state land. The causes are a sequence of past decisions:

1. **Nationalisation (1964).** Law 64-5 of 12 May 1964 nationalised foreign-owned land (SRC-TG-781). Together with earlier purchases and the recovery of public habous, it gave the state about 800,000 ha, roughly 10% of farmland, mostly in the fertile north (SRC-TG-785, SRC-TG-786).
2. **Forced cooperatives (1964-1969).** The state grouped small farmers with under 40 ha around former colonial farms in production cooperatives. Small farmers resisted from the start; the 1969 plan to extend cooperatives to all agriculture met fierce opposition from large farmers and the experiment was stopped in September 1969 (SRC-TG-781, SRC-TG-786). Small farmers took back their land, but cooperatives remained on state land alongside OTD farms (SRC-TG-786). FTDES notes the word "cooperative" is still tainted by this episode (SRC-TG-782).
3. **Privatised management under structural adjustment (1980s-2011).** From the mid-1980s, management of state farms passed mainly to agronomist technicians and SMVDA companies (SRC-TG-786). Between 1981 and 1995, 143,000 ha were transferred to private management (SRC-TG-785). Law 95-21 kept ownership with the state. It made the land inalienable except for regularisation or exchange, and set lease terms of 25 years extendable to 40 for SMVDA and up to 40 years for technicians and young farmers (SRC-TG-784). By 2001 SMVDA held 145,000 ha, while young farmers had only 15,000 ha in 8-ha lots (SRC-TG-785). Because ownership stayed with the state, allocation became a patronage tool: more than 12,000 ha went to relatives of the deposed president (SRC-TG-785). This sits within the wider structural adjustment covered by FND-TG-920.
4. **Unmet lessee commitments and the 2011 occupations.** Lessees often did not keep their commitments to hire locally and invest. After 14 January 2011, more than a hundred SMVDA and technician farms covering several tens of thousands of hectares were occupied or looted (SRC-TG-786).
5. **Changing post-2011 policy.** Governments forfeited the rights of 178 SMVDA (73,446 ha) by 2016 and returned their farms to OTD (SRC-TG-786). They counted 20,330 ha of occupied land in 2016 (SRC-TG-786). They rescheduled lessees' debts by law 2016-08 (SRC-TG-786) and re-awarded 43 SMVDA (32,154 ha), according to the authors through clientelist networks (SRC-TG-786). Awards to young farmers stopped in 2011-2017 and then covered only 406.55 ha (SRC-TG-786). In 2025-2026 a new allocation channel to sociétés communautaires opened, with 24 state farms of more than 11,000 ha made available (SRC-TG-789).

**Jemna as a case.** In January 2011 residents of Jemna (Kebili) occupied a state palm grove that had been run as an SMVDA by promoters close to the old regime, and ran it through a local association (SRC-TG-786, SRC-TG-787). In 2016 the Ministry of State Domains ordered the association to stop and had its bank accounts frozen. It offered a 49/51 state-association SMVDA, which the association refused (SRC-TG-787). An agreement was reached in September 2017 under the Ministry of Agriculture; the author notes it does not question state ownership of the land (SRC-TG-787). The sources disagree on when the association was founded: right after 14 January 2011 according to Vico (SRC-TG-787), March 2012 according to Gana and Taleb (SRC-TG-786).

## Evidence

- Origins: 800,000 ha (SRC-TG-785, SRC-TG-786); 600,000 ha of colonial land and 160,000 ha of public habous (SRC-TG-785); about 300,000 ha nationalised by the 1964 law itself (SRC-TG-785).
- Privatised management: 143,000 ha in 1981-1995. End-2001 allocation: 145,000 ha SMVDA (226 companies), 50,000 ha technician lots (600 lots), 15,000 ha young-farmer lots (1,850 lots), with 210,000 ha still to restructure (SRC-TG-785).
- 2004 structure survey: legal-person farms (SMVDA, companies, UCPA, agro-combinats) held about 364,500 ha (6.8% of farmland), down from 482,000 ha (9%) in 1994 (SRC-TG-780).
- Post-2011: Elloumi reports more than 100 farms attacked, damage over 30 million TND and about 30,000 ha recovered (SRC-TG-785). Gana and Taleb report 33 SMVDA (22,377 ha) forfeited in 2012, 178 (73,446 ha) by 2016, and 20,330 ha occupied in 2016 (SRC-TG-786).
- Today: 500,000 ha total, 38% leased (SRC-TG-789). Breakdown: SMVDA 80,000 ha, OTD 76,000 ha structured plus 157,000 ha unstructured, technician lots 54,000 ha, young promoters and workers 30,000 ha, private leases 32,000 ha, cooperatives 18,000 ha (SRC-TG-788). More than 50,000 unregularised operators in 2022 (SRC-TG-790); at most 15% of regularisation files settled in ten years, according to bill sponsors (SRC-TG-791).
- Legal frame: law 95-21 articles 2, 3, 10, 11, 15, 17 and 18 (SRC-TG-784). The 2016 investment law frees acquisition of non-agricultural real estate only (SRC-TG-794).

**Disagreements recorded, not reconciled:** SMVDA area was 145,000 ha in 2001 (SRC-TG-785) and 80,000 ha in 2024 (SRC-TG-788), which is consistent with forfeitures after 2011 but not confirmed by any source. Elloumi puts OTD farms at 150,000 ha (SRC-TG-785); the 2024 press gives 76,000 + 157,000 ha (SRC-TG-788). The total fell from about 800,000 ha to about 500,000 ha, and no source we opened documents the transition. Law 95-21 provides for sales to regularise past situations and refers to an earlier law (70-25 of 1970) on the cession of state agricultural land, so sales are one possible channel (SRC-TG-784), but no figures were found.

## Why confidence is what it is

The history (1964 to 2018) rests on two peer-reviewed articles that agree on the main facts (SRC-TG-785, SRC-TG-786), backed by the primary text of law 95-21 (SRC-TG-784). The Jemna case adds a third peer-reviewed source, though one that relies mainly on journalism (SRC-TG-787). The current picture (500,000 ha, 38% leased, 50,000 unregularised operators, 15% regularisation rate) rests only on tier C press reports of ministerial or parliamentary statements (SRC-TG-788 to SRC-TG-791). Several of these were read as fetch-tool fragments, and the 15% figure is a claim by bill sponsors. Elloumi's extracts were obtained as fragments because the site blocked direct download. Overall confidence is medium: the legacy causes are well documented, the present size and use of the land are not.

Related records: FND-TG-920 covers the 1986-1995 agricultural adjustment programme, the period when state-farm management was privatised. FND-TG-720 notes that some new irrigated intensive olive groves are on leased state land.

## Links to other sectors

Water: much state land lies in the irrigated and higher-rainfall north. Jemna is a groundwater-fed oasis in Kebili, where land and water rights are intertwined (SRC-TG-787); see FND-TG-741 on unauthorised extensions. No source in this run quantified irrigated state land. Energy: a 2026 press report (not opened) mentions state land being mobilised for renewable-energy projects; this possible competition between farm and solar use of state land was not verified.
