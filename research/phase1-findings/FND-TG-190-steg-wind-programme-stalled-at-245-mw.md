## Problem

Tunisia's current wind-generation fleet was developed by STEG in two main build-outs: Sidi Daoud began operating in 2001 and reached 55 MW in 2008; Metline and Kchabta in Bizerte entered in 2012 with 190 MW combined (SRC-TG-106). The 2015 Tunisian Solar Plan recorded 245 MW installed wind capacity at its baseline and planned 775 MW by 2020, 1,305 MW by 2025 and 1,755 MW by 2030 (SRC-TG-192). IRENA's energy profile reports 245 MW at end-2024 and zero net wind-capacity change over 2019-2024 (SRC-TG-190). Thus the documented national capacity remained at 245 MW at the 2015 and 2024 endpoints, and it was below the plan's 2020 and 2025 planned totals; these planned values are targets, not forecasts or evidence of actual capacity. The available sources do not establish exactly when the plateau began or why additional wind capacity was not commissioned.

Wind electricity generation also declined over the reported series: IRENA gives 507 GWh in the 2014 peak year and 449 GWh in 2017 (SRC-TG-106); ONEM reports 500 GWh in 2019 and 465 GWh in 2020 (SRC-TG-194), then 337 GWh in 2024 and 259 GWh in 2025 (SRC-TG-193). ONEM labels the 2024-2025 change as -23%; it reports wind as 1.3% of the 2025 electricity-production mix, whose total includes self-produced PV (SRC-TG-193). These are generation data, distinct from installed-capacity values. The evidence establishes the output trend but not its cause.

## Root causes and explanations

The available sources identify historical policy and project-development constraints, but do not prove a single root cause for the capacity plateau or output decline.

- **Historical public-utility-led build-out.** IRENA reports that before the 2015 framework private generation was limited to concessions tendered by the state, unsolicited private power production was not allowed, and only one concession agreement had been granted, for a gas-fired plant. The existing wind farms were developed by STEG (SRC-TG-106). This explains the institutional context of the original fleet; it does not establish that the pre-2015 framework alone caused the later lack of capacity additions. The 2015 Solar Plan still assigned STEG 545 MW of wind capacity by 2020, compared with a 245 MW 2015 baseline, but IRENA's end-2024 data show 245 MW in total (SRC-TG-192, SRC-TG-190).
- **Documented procurement and project-development constraints.** IRENA says most offers in the May 2017 authorisation call were conditional on changes to the standard power-purchase agreement; it reports that no wind project was selected in that call and describes revision of the contract (SRC-TG-106). The May 2017 call's wind proposals were deferred pending PPA revision; under the separate authorisation process, four wind authorisations were granted in January 2019. The 2018 concession prequalification was a different process and covered four wind farms totalling 500 MW (SRC-TG-106). FNF's ministry-assisted 2023 policy paper says a deadline for 300 MW of concession wind projects extended to end-2023 to allow studies and wind-speed measurement; ONEM's 2025 report separately says a state-site wind measurement campaign was due to start in early 2026 and lists one awarded 75 MW private-site wind project under the 2022 tender (SRC-TG-195, SRC-TG-193). Those dated statements document delays and development activity, not the late-2026 status of each project or proof that one law caused the gap. FNF also reports that defence coordination is required for wind projects because of strict siting criteria (SRC-TG-195).
- **Reported turbine outages.** A 2019 WMC/TAP report said 17 of 143 Metline-Kchabta turbines were fully stopped since 2015. The article attributed damage to theft and vandalism and delayed repairs to lack of maintenance, replacement parts, slow purchasing and import procedures; these are press-reported claims, including statements attributed to TAP documents and a STEG official (SRC-TG-197). They support a dated, site-specific report of outages, not a verified explanation for national generation changes through 2025.
- **El Haouaria rehabilitation report.** La Presse reported in September 2026 that the Sidi Daoud/Borj Essalhi plant was being rehabilitated, with 32 old turbines to be replaced by seven new turbines; it reported a 102 million dinar loan-funded project and completion expected in 2027 (SRC-TG-198). The same article gives current production capacity as 10.5 MW and a future range of 29-33 MW, but does not define the measurement basis for either. These press figures are retained as reported project details and are not treated as official installed capacity or as a confirmed cause of the national generation decline.

## Evidence and interpretation

- **Observed capacity:** 245 MW at the 2015 baseline in the Solar Plan; 245 MW at end-2024 in IRENA; IRENA reports zero wind-capacity change over 2019-2024 (SRC-TG-192, SRC-TG-190). This supports endpoint continuity and the 2019-2024 flat series, not an exact start date for stagnation.
- **Plan targets:** the 2015 plan's planned totals were 245 / 775 / 1,305 / 1,755 MW for 2015 / 2020 / 2025 / 2030; planned STEG wind capacity was 245 / 545 / 645 / 735 MW at those horizons (SRC-TG-192, p.15). The 2020 and 2025 values are planned capacity, not achieved outcomes. The same plan distinguishes a 35% renewable share of installed capacity from a 30% renewables share of electricity production by 2030; these are different measures (SRC-TG-192).
- **Generation:** reported STEG wind generation was 507 GWh (2014), 449 GWh (2017), 500 GWh (2019), 465 GWh (2020), 337 GWh (2024) and 259 GWh (2025), from IRENA and ONEM (SRC-TG-106, SRC-TG-194, SRC-TG-193). IRENA separately lists 338 GWh for 2023 (SRC-TG-190). The series is not a continuous annual series in this record, and the cited reports use different publication years and presentations. ONEM's 2025 table identifies its production values as GWh and labels the year-over-year wind change -23%; the accompanying mix chart says it includes self-produced PV in the total denominator (SRC-TG-193).
- A simple arithmetic comparison of 259 GWh in 2025 with 245 MW nameplate capacity implies about 12% annual energy per nameplate capacity (259,000 MWh / (245 MW x 8,760 hours)). This is an indicative calculation across sources and is not a reported capacity factor or verified availability measure; the 2014 output similarly implies about 24% if divided by 245 MW (SRC-TG-193, SRC-TG-190, SRC-TG-106).

## Confidence and timeframe

Capacity and generation figures are reported by IRENA, ONEM and the official 2015 Solar Plan; the cited 245 MW capacity value recurs across the plan baseline and IRENA's end-2024 series. Medium confidence reflects that the measured shortfall is clear but causal attribution for the plateau and output decline remains unresolved; the outage and rehabilitation detail is press-reported (tier C). `current` is used because the documented national capacity endpoint and generation series run through 2025, while the historical build-out and policy context are retained to explain the issue.

## Links to other sectors

No direct cross-sector mechanism is established in these sources. Lower wind generation could increase reliance on other sources in the electricity mix, but the record does not establish that wind generation was replaced one-for-one by gas. FND-TG-101 discusses the separate gas-dominated electricity system.
