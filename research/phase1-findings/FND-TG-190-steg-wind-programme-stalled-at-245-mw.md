## Problem

Tunisia's wind power was built by STEG in two steps: Sidi Daoud on Cap Bon from 2001 (55 MW by
2008) and the Metline and Kchabta farms near Bizerte in 2012 (190 MW) (SRC-TG-106). Capacity has
stayed at 245 MW since: it was 245 MW at the end of 2015 (SRC-TG-192) and still 245 MW at the end
of 2024, with zero change over 2019-2024 (SRC-TG-190). The 2015 Tunisian Solar Plan had planned
775 MW of wind by 2020 and 1,305 MW by 2025 (SRC-TG-192). Output from the existing fleet has
fallen: from a peak of 507 GWh in 2014 (SRC-TG-106) to 465 GWh in 2020 (SRC-TG-194), 337 GWh in
2024 and 259 GWh in 2025 (SRC-TG-193). Wind was the main renewable source before 2015. Capacity was 245 MW in the 2015 plan baseline and remained 245 MW at end-2024 (SRC-TG-192, SRC-TG-190); the sources reviewed here do not establish the date or cause of the subsequent capacity plateau.

## Root causes

The symptom is a flat and shrinking wind contribution. The causes found are:

- **Past decision: wind as a STEG-only public programme.** Until 2015 private generation was
  allowed only through concessions tendered by the state under Law 96-27 (1996), and unsolicited
  private projects were not allowed; only one concession, a gas plant, was ever granted
  (SRC-TG-106). Wind was therefore built by STEG alone. The 2015 plan still gave STEG the largest
  share of new wind (245 MW to 545 MW by 2020) (SRC-TG-192), but STEG added none (SRC-TG-190).
- **Private wind did not start after Law 2015-12.** In the first authorisation call (May 2017)
  most offers were conditional on changes to the standard power purchase agreement, and no wind
  project was selected (SRC-TG-106). The wind part was moved to the second call after the
  standard contract was revised (SRC-TG-195). Of the 500 MW of wind concessions pre-qualified in
  May 2018 (SRC-TG-106), the deadline for 300 MW was extended to the end of 2023 to complete
  studies and wind measurements (SRC-TG-195); by 2025 the state-site wind programme had been enlarged but its wind
  measurement campaign was only due to start in early 2026, and the 600 MW private-site wind
  tender of 2022 had awarded one 75 MW project (SRC-TG-193). A ministry-assisted policy paper
  adds that the Ministry of National Defence applies strict criteria to wind sites, which need
  prior coordination (SRC-TG-195).
- **Degradation of the existing fleet (tier C only).** In 2019, 17 of the 143 Metline-Kchabta
  turbines had been fully stopped since 2015; the press report blamed theft and vandalism, no
  maintenance and no quick replacement of parts, and a STEG manager blamed slow purchasing and
  spare-part import procedures (SRC-TG-197). In 2026 the Sidi Daoud farm was reported to have a
  current production capacity of 10.5 MW, against a nameplate of about 55 MW, and is being rebuilt
  with 7 new turbines replacing 32 old ones, for completion in 2027 (SRC-TG-198, SRC-TG-106).

## Evidence

- Capacity: 245 MW wind at end 2015, alongside about 30 MW of rooftop PV (SRC-TG-192); 245 MW wind
  at end 2019 within 352 MW of renewables (SRC-TG-106); 245 MW at end 2024, "Wind 0 0.0" change for
  2019-24 (SRC-TG-190).
- Plan: wind 245 / 775 / 1,305 / 1,755 MW in 2015 / 2020 / 2025 / 2030, of which STEG
  245 / 545 / 645 / 735 MW (SRC-TG-192, p. 15).
- Output: 507 GWh in 2014 and 449 GWh in 2017 (SRC-TG-106); 500 GWh in 2019 and 465 GWh in 2020
  (SRC-TG-194); 338 GWh in 2023 (SRC-TG-190); 337 GWh in 2024 and 259 GWh in 2025 (SRC-TG-193).
  The ONEM and IRENA figures agree for 2023-2024 (338 and 337 GWh).
- Our own arithmetic, not a sourced indicator: 259 GWh from 245 MW is a capacity factor of about
  12% (259,000 MWh / (245 MW x 8,760 h)), using SRC-TG-193 and SRC-TG-190. The 2014 output
  implies about 24% on the same capacity.

## Why confidence is what it is

Capacity, plan and output figures come from official ONEM statistics, the official Solar Plan
and IRENA, and they agree with each other: these are solid. Medium overall because the
explanation of the output decline rests on two press articles (tier C, SRC-TG-197 and
SRC-TG-198); no STEG availability data were found. The timeframe is "past" because the decision
(public-only wind) and its build-out played out in 2001-2012; the period runs to 2025 only to
show the continuing effect (flat capacity, falling output).

## Links to other sectors

None found directly. Indirectly, lost wind output is replaced by gas-fired generation in a system
that is over 90% gas (FND-TG-101).
