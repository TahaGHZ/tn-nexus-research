## Problem

Tunisia raised rural drinking-water coverage from 25% in 1984 to 80% in 2000 (SRC-TG-458), and above 90% by 2009 (SRC-TG-455). It did so largely by building systems with State money and handing their operation to user associations. These were first AIC, then GIC, now GDA. They grew from 100 in 1987 to 2,809 in 2005, of which 1,610 managed drinking water (SRC-TG-455).

The model left the groups too small and too poor to maintain what they ran. In 2009, 69% had turnover below 6,000 TND a year, ran mostly on volunteer work, and charged tariffs that were often higher than urban ones yet still did not cover maintenance or renewal (SRC-TG-455). By 2017 GDA-run systems still served about 1.5 million rural people (SRC-TG-402). Only 44% of GDAs had a management contract (SRC-TG-402), and cost recovery had fallen to about 30% (SRC-TG-457). The handover to SONEDE recommended in 2009 was still pending in 2017 (SRC-TG-402, SRC-TG-455).

## Root causes

The symptoms are summer cuts, losses, breakdowns and debts. The cause is a design choice made in the 1980s, plus its financing rule.

1. **Delegation to volunteer associations.** From the 1980s, the State multiplied AIC/GIC/GDA to "ensure the continued operation" of the rural infrastructure it built (SRC-TG-455). The DGGREE created the groups, wrote their statutes and supervised them through CRDA cells (SRC-TG-455). This allowed fast coverage without direct State or SONEDE operation (SRC-TG-455).
2. **Investment by the State, maintenance by the users.** Investment is State-financed. The groups' tariff, often much higher than SONEDE's for the same consumption, covers operation "but not proper maintenance and certainly not renewal" (SRC-TG-455). The World Bank warned that the groups would have no resources for major repairs when breakdowns came, and recommended a State maintenance fund (SRC-TG-455). By about 2017, 40% of GDA systems were dilapidated (SRC-TG-457).
3. **Design and governance limits.** Standpipe-based schemes run by a paid keeper opened only one or two hours a day, with tariffs about 30% higher (SRC-TG-455). After 2011, refusal to pay, vandalism of meters and illegal connections increased (SRC-TG-465). Speakers at a 2020 conference described GDA governance as unchanged for more than 20 years, with opaque finances and boards often controlled by local authorities and influential actors (SRC-TG-466, tier C).
4. **The exit route stalled.** In 2009 the World Bank called for a ten-year strategy to integrate GIC/GDA into SONEDE progressively, while warning that this would raise SONEDE's costs without tariff adjustments (SRC-TG-455). In 2014 AFD found that the rural centres it financed were an extra burden for SONEDE (SRC-TG-458). In 2017 the handover of large rural schemes was "toujours en suspens" (SRC-TG-402). In 2025 RVO described the transfer as "underway" (SRC-TG-456).

## Evidence

- **Counts of drinking-water groups differ by year and source:**

  | Year | Drinking-water groups | Mixed groups | Source |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2005 | 1,610 | 124 | SRC-TG-455 |
  | 2014 | 1,327 | not stated | SRC-TG-465, SONEDE workshop data via the press |
  | 2015 | 1,396 | 121 | SRC-TG-457 |
  | 2017 | 1,441 | 131 | SRC-TG-402 |

  These are recorded separately and not merged.
- **Population served.** More than 2 million beneficiaries in 2005 (SRC-TG-455). About 1.6 million, 46% of the rural population, in 2014 (SRC-TG-465). 1.6 million in 2017 by GDA count, or 1,523,600 people by Génie Rural service figures (SRC-TG-402).
- **Finance.**
  - 2014: drinking-water groups owed 38 MDT, including 20.7 MDT to the CRDAs and 1.8 MDT to SONEDE (SRC-TG-465).
  - About 2017: GDAs owed the CRDAs 50 MDT, and owed STEG as well (SRC-TG-457).
  - Cost recovery fell from 55% (2006) to about 30% (DGGREE estimate, SRC-TG-457).
  - In 2017, 21% of GDAs covered more than half their maintenance costs, and 26% suffered from illegal connections (SRC-TG-402).
- **Service.**
  - SONEDE said in 2014 that weak finances and supervision often cause disrupted supply or outright cuts, "especially in summer", and that the groups lose nearly 32% of their water (SRC-TG-465).
  - In 2017, 159 rural Génie Rural systems were disrupted or stopped in summer (SRC-TG-402).
  - In Kasserine (about 2017), GDA network losses reached 60% on one drinking-water system (SRC-TG-457), and only 50 GDAs had held a statutory general assembly since 2011 (SRC-TG-457).
- **The gap to SONEDE standards persists.** In 2017 the rural individual-connection rate was 67.7% nationally and 48.2% in the Centre-West (SRC-TG-402). In 2023 SONEDE served 54.6% of the rural population, and 51.1% of rural households had a SONEDE connection (SRC-TG-453).

## Why confidence is what it is

The diagnosis is consistent across a World Bank strategy (2009), the ministry's national water report (2017, tier A), an AFD evaluation (2014), an International Alert field study (2017) and RVO (2025). The core figures (coverage history, group counts, contracts, population served) come from tier A and B sources. The debt and loss figures for 2014 (SRC-TG-465) and the 2020 governance statements (SRC-TG-466) are tier C and are used only alongside tier A/B evidence.

The timeframe is `past` because the decision and the build-out (1980s-2005) and the first diagnosis of failure (2009-2014) are complete. The effects are current and unresolved.

## Links to other sectors

- **Energy.** GDAs pump water from boreholes and depend on STEG electricity. STEG cuts power to indebted GDAs, which stops water supply. In Kasserine, 70 GDAs owed STEG between 5,000 and 70,000 TND each (SRC-TG-457). Ministry statistics cited in 2014 say the groups bear more than 90% of energy, water-purchase and labour costs (SRC-TG-465).
- **Agriculture.** The same GDA model runs irrigated perimeters; the irrigation side and its tariff and debt chain are covered in FND-TG-477: 1,182 irrigation GDAs in 2017 (SRC-TG-402). In Kasserine, growing demand for irrigation water drawn from drinking-water networks weakens those systems and threatens drinking-water supply (SRC-TG-457).

## Leads for phase 2

- Planned transfer of GDA drinking-water systems to SONEDE (SRC-TG-456; SONEDE rural supply strategy page: https://www.sonede.com.tn/strategie/strategie-de-desserte-du-milieu-rural/).
- Fonds de Bonne Gestion for GDAs, due to start in 2018 in five Centre-West governorates (SRC-TG-402).
- State maintenance fund for AIC/GIC/GDA, proposed by the World Bank in 2009 (SRC-TG-455).
- Federation of GDAs into local, regional and national structures; SONEDE to take over drinking-water supply for "rural agglomerated habitat" (Eau 2050, SRC-TG-503 document; extract not yet in that record).
- AFD rural drinking-water programmes AEP 1/AEP 2 (SRC-TG-458) and https://www.afd.fr/fr/projets/un-meilleur-acces-leau-potable-en-milieu-rural
- Systematic individual connections in new Génie Rural schemes, and regularisation of illegal connections (SRC-TG-402, p. 80).
