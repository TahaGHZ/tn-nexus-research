## Problem

Tunisia moved from an energy surplus to a deficit around 2000-2001. Official charts show the
primary balance at "Equilibre" in 2000 and in deficit from 2001 (SRC-TG-141). By 2010 Tunisia was
already a net energy importer. From 2010 to 2015, national primary energy production fell from
8,293 to 6,397 ktep, a computed 22.9% decline, while the full-balance energy-deficit share of gross
inland consumption rose from 19.5% to 41.5% (SRC-TG-130). The same official series records
production of 5,077 ktep and a deficit share of 54.9% in 2019; net energy imports rose from 2.1
Mtep in 2010 to 6.5 Mtep in 2019 (SRC-TG-130). Separate sources report that valid exploration and
research permits fell from 56 in 2010 to 23 at end-2020, according to the minister, while ONEM
counted 25 valid permits at end-March 2020; these are differently dated reported counts, not a
continuous series (SRC-TG-139; SRC-TG-131). The available evidence does not establish that
exploration effort collapsed during 2010-2015.

## Root causes

The measured problem is declining domestic production and a widening energy deficit. Evidence about
causes is less complete and does not quantify the contribution of geology, investment conditions,
social conflict or approval procedures.

- **Depletion without replacement (technical; minister-attributed).** The minister attributed the
  hydrocarbon-output decline mainly to natural field decline and inadequate reserve replacement
  through discoveries (SRC-TG-139). This is a ministerial explanation relayed by press, not a
  measured decomposition of ONEM's broader primary-energy series. The ONEM full-balance series
  records falling primary production each year from 2010 to 2015, including 7,616 ktep in 2011 and
  7,304 ktep in 2013 (SRC-TG-130). The decline therefore predates Article 13 and cannot have been
  initiated by it; that timing alone does not quantify later effects.
- **Reported investment conditions and social conflict (institutional).** The minister cites
  "la réticence des entreprises à investir, en raison des protestations sociales" (SRC-TG-139). This
  attribution is not an independently measured post-2011 investment trend. OBG reports three wells
  drilled by international oil companies in 2015; this is a single-year count (SRC-TG-138). Its
  account also mentions work stoppages and permit non-renewals, without quantifying their effects.
- **Past decision: Article 13 and the approval regime (policy, legacy).** The 2014 Constitution
  made natural resources the property of the people. It requires investment agreements to go to
  a parliamentary committee and the related conventions to be approved by the Assembly
  (SRC-TG-136). Under the old code, the executive approved conventions by decree and granted or
  renewed permits by ministerial order (SRC-TG-137). In July 2014 the ANC energy committee
  refused to extend three hydrocarbon permits (Borj El Khadra, Baguel, El Franig), according to a press report (SRC-TG-340, tier C).
  OBG's statement that the committee refused renewal of "all" hydrocarbon permits, until Article 13's implementing laws were in
  place, is not supported by that report (SRC-TG-138, tier C). The stated reasons in SRC-TG-340 are alleged legal and financial
  infractions and, for two permits, the operator's 30-year cap argument; the committee record was not located.
  The Hydrocarbons Code amendment was adopted on 18 April 2017. A legal-expert presentation noted permits were
  expiring and others waiting (SRC-TG-137). These sources suggest possible administrative delay, but
  none measures its effect. Article 13 did not initiate the production decline already under way in
  2010-2013; the evidence does not establish whether the new approval regime materially affected
  later exploration or production, and no causal magnitude is assigned.

## Evidence

- Primary production, full national balance, ktep: 2010 8,293; 2011 7,616; 2012 7,566; 2013
  7,304; 2014 6,749; 2015 6,397; 2019 5,077 (SRC-TG-130, Tableau 7).
- Deficit as share of gross inland consumption: 2010 19.5%; 2012 26.2%; 2014 36.3%; 2015 41.5%;
  2019 54.9% (SRC-TG-130, Tableau 18).
- Crude oil's share of national production fell from 48% to 37% over 2010-2019, and energy
  exports fell 55% (SRC-TG-130).
- On the separate classic monthly-bulletin basis, primary resources were 5,219 ktep in 2015 against
  8,916 ktep of demand (SRC-TG-221). That is an independence rate of about 59% (computed).
- The minister gives 93% energy independence in 2010 and 43% in 2020, with hydrocarbon output of
  7 Mtoe in 2010 and 3.5 Mtoe in 2020 (SRC-TG-139). These are attributed, press-reported statements;
  the source does not specify the independence-index basis. **The sources disagree on the 2010 baseline.**
  ONEM's full balance gives 8.3 Mtep of primary production and a 19.5% deficit (about 80% coverage)
  in 2010 (SRC-TG-130). The minister's 2020 value matches ONEM's classic-approach rate, 43%
  (SRC-TG-133), which counts the Algerian gas royalty as a national resource. His 2010 figure may use a different basis; the article does not say. The two series are not spliced
  here.
- ANME / ONE chart: primary resources of 6,225 ktep against 8,757 ktep of consumption in 2013,
  about 71% coverage (SRC-TG-141, computed). The surplus ended in 2000.
- Permits: 56 in 2010 (SRC-TG-139, tier C) and 25 at end-March 2020 (17 research, 8 prospecting;
  SRC-TG-131).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The production and deficit series are official and internally consistent (SRC-TG-130).
The causes rest on weaker ground. Depletion and investor reluctance come from a minister's
statement relayed by the press (SRC-TG-139). The Article 13 link rests on a business publication
(SRC-TG-138) and an expert slide deck (SRC-TG-137). The 2010 permit count has no tier A source.
The magnitude of each cause is unknown. Strictly, the timeframe is `past` for the 2010-2015 shift,
but the decline did not stop in 2015 (see FND-TG-131).

## Links to other sectors

None found in the sources opened. The production decline feeds the gas shortfall in power
generation described in FND-TG-100 and FND-TG-101. Those findings cover the energy-sector link,
not another sector.

## Verification

The ONEM production series establishes the dated decline. Article 13 was adopted after the decline began, and the sources cannot apportion it between resource maturity, investment, disruption and regulation. Reported permit disputes provide context only.
