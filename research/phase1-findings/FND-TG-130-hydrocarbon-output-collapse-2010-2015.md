## Problem

Tunisia moved from an energy surplus to a deficit around 2000-2001. Official charts show the
primary balance at "Equilibre" in 2000 and in deficit from 2001 (SRC-TG-141). By 2010 it was a
net importer, but a small one. Over 2010-2015 national
primary energy production fell from 8,293 ktep to 6,397 ktep, a 23% drop, and kept falling to
5,077 ktep in 2019 (SRC-TG-130). Demand was roughly flat over the same years, so the energy
deficit more than doubled. It rose from 19.5% of gross inland consumption in 2010 to 41.5% in
2015 and 54.9% in 2019 (SRC-TG-130). Net energy imports went from 2.1 Mtep to 6.5 Mtep between
2010 and 2019 (SRC-TG-130). At the same time the exploration effort that could have replaced the
depleting fields collapsed. The minister reported 56 valid permits in 2010 against 23 at end-2020
(SRC-TG-139). ONEM counted 25 in March 2020 (SRC-TG-131).

## Root causes

The symptom is falling output and rising imports. The causes behind it are partly geological and
partly institutional, and the sources do not allow them to be weighed against each other.

- **Depletion without replacement (technical).** The minister attributes the fall mainly to "déclin
  naturel des champs de production" and to the failure to renew reserves through new discoveries
  (SRC-TG-139). The ONEM series shows production already falling in 2011 (7,616 ktep) and 2012
  (7,566 ktep), before the 2014 Constitution (SRC-TG-130). So the collapse did not start with the
  constitutional change.
- **Post-2011 investment slowdown and social conflict (institutional).** The minister also cites
  "la réticence des entreprises à investir, en raison des protestations sociales" (SRC-TG-139).
  Oxford Business Group reports only three wells drilled by international oil companies in 2015.
  It also says some operators were leaving over "work stoppages" and "non-renewal of drilling
  permits" (SRC-TG-138, tier C).
- **Past decision: Article 13 and the approval regime (policy, legacy).** The 2014 Constitution
  made natural resources the property of the people. It requires investment agreements to go to
  a parliamentary committee and the related conventions to be approved by the Assembly
  (SRC-TG-136). Under the old code, the executive approved conventions by decree and granted or
  renewed permits by ministerial order (SRC-TG-137). According to OBG, in 2014 the energy
  committee refused to renew all hydrocarbon permits until Article 13's implementing laws were in
  place (SRC-TG-138). The code was only amended to comply on 18 April 2017. A legal expert noted
  at the time that permits were expiring and others waiting (SRC-TG-137). This decision plausibly
  added delay, but no opened source measures its effect. The commonly stated belief that Article
  13 "caused" the production collapse is **not supported** by the timeline: output was already
  falling in 2010-2013. At most it is a contributing factor to the exploration standstill.

## Evidence

- Primary production, full national balance, ktep: 2010 8,293; 2011 7,616; 2012 7,566; 2013
  7,304; 2014 6,749; 2015 6,397; 2019 5,077 (SRC-TG-130, Tableau 7).
- Deficit as share of gross inland consumption: 2010 19.5%; 2012 26.2%; 2014 36.3%; 2015 41.5%;
  2019 54.9% (SRC-TG-130, Tableau 18).
- Crude oil's share of national production fell from 48% to 37% over 2010-2019, and energy
  exports fell 55% (SRC-TG-130).
- On the classic monthly-bulletin basis, primary resources were 5,219 ktep in 2015 against
  8,916 ktep of demand (SRC-TG-221). That is an independence rate of about 59% (computed).
- The minister gives 93% energy independence in 2010 and 43% in 2020, with hydrocarbon output of
  7 Mtoe in 2010 and 3.5 Mtoe in 2020 (SRC-TG-139). **The sources disagree on the 2010 baseline.**
  ONEM's full balance gives 8.3 Mtep of primary production and a 19.5% deficit (about 80% coverage)
  in 2010 (SRC-TG-130). The minister's 2020 value matches ONEM's classic-approach rate, 43%
  (SRC-TG-133), which counts the Algerian gas royalty as a national resource. His 2010 figure is
  probably on that basis too, but the article does not say so. The two series are not spliced
  here.
- ANME / ONE chart: primary resources of 6,225 ktep against 8,757 ktep of consumption in 2013,
  about 71% coverage (SRC-TG-141, computed). The surplus ended in 2000.
- Permits: 56 in 2010 (SRC-TG-139, tier C) and 25 in March 2020 (17 research, 8 prospecting;
  SRC-TG-131).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The production and deficit series are official and internally consistent (SRC-TG-130).
The causes rest on weaker ground. Depletion and investor reluctance come from a minister's
statement relayed by the press (SRC-TG-139). The Article 13 link rests on a business publication
(SRC-TG-138) and an expert slide deck (SRC-TG-137). The 2010 permit count has no tier A source.
The magnitude of each cause is unknown. Strictly, the timeframe is `past` for the 2010-2015 shift,
but the decline did not stop in 2015 (see FND-TG-131).

## Links to other sectors

None found in the sources opened. The production decline feeds the gas shortfall in power
generation described in FND-TG-100 and FND-TG-101. Those findings cover the energy-sector link,
not another sector.
