## Problem

Water from large dams delivered to public irrigation schemes, which cover 56% of the irrigable area, has been priced at 0.065 TND/m3 since 2004; in 2018 that covered only 15% of its cost (SRC-TG-482). Further down the chain, a ministry study of sampled public irrigated perimeters found that "whatever the situation", tariffs do not let managers cover their costs: average recovery ranges from 64% (39% with renewal) in autonomous schemes to 38% (23%) in jointly managed pumped schemes, 22-25% in oases and 12% in schemes using treated wastewater (SRC-TG-402, p. 90). Unpaid bills pile up along a chain from farmers to GDAs to CRDAs, STEG and SONEDE: in 2016 irrigation debts in public perimeters totalled 50 million TND, 37 million of it owed by GDAs (SRC-TG-402, p. 91). Rural drinking-water GDAs show the same pattern: their average cost (0.863 TND/m3) exceeds their average tariff (0.730 TND/m3), they owe 10.7 million TND (7.7 million to STEG), and only 20% cover the full maintenance of their systems (SRC-TG-484, p. 16).

## Root causes

- **Symptom:** GDA debts, cuts by STEG, and degrading schemes are symptoms. The causes are below.
- **Prices fixed by the State and frozen (policy, legacy).** Irrigation water supply and pricing in public perimeters are set by decree on the Agriculture minister's proposal (art. 106 bis of the code, added 1988, SRC-TG-406). The dam water price has not moved since 2004 (SRC-TG-482). When the CRDA sells the water itself, the State does not allow it to raise its prices, and CRDAs do not encourage GDAs to raise theirs despite visible annual imbalances (SRC-TG-485, pp. 28-29).
- **Preferential and flat-rate tariffs (policy, 1998).** The preferential tariff introduced in 1998 (50% of the normal price for "strategic" crops, 20 millimes/m3 for treated wastewater) works as a subsidy, and the cheap water is often used on other crops (SRC-TG-402, p. 91). In Bizerte, the farmer price is halved (70 instead of 140 millimes/m3) but the GDA's purchase price only falls from 110 to 55 millimes/m3, so the GDA bears 15 millimes/m3 of the subsidy (SRC-TG-485, p. 30). Flat-rate sales where meters are missing or destroyed remove any incentive to save water (SRC-TG-402, p. 91).
- **Transfer without means (institutional, from 1987).** Rural water systems have been entrusted to user groups since 1987, run by elected volunteer boards that must cover operation and maintenance (SRC-TG-484, p. 16). Members of parliament blamed their debts to SONEDE and STEG, weak supervision and mismanagement on this voluntary basis and on the lack of a legal framework (SRC-TG-484, pp. 11, 17). The CCDR says the 2,500 GDAs lack technical and operational capacity, which "prevents cost recovery" (SRC-TG-482, p. 26). CRDAs themselves have low budgets, so they cannot maintain the upstream works they still manage, and they struggle to recover the sums GDAs owe them (SRC-TG-485, p. 51).
- **Non-payment and resistance to price rises (behavioural).** Unpaid member bills explain most GDA financial difficulties; farmers contest price increases (SRC-TG-485, pp. 28-29). After 2011, reluctance to pay and vandalism of meters worsened the drinking-water groups' situation (SRC-TG-465, tier C).
- **Drought (climatic).** GDA representatives told parliament that successive drought years and falling reserves cut their sales revenue (SRC-TG-484, p. 23).

## Evidence

- Dam water price 0.065 TND/m3 since 2004, 15% of cost in 2018; public schemes 56% of the irrigable area (SRC-TG-482, p. 8).
- Cost recovery in sampled PPIs (DGGREE tariff study launched 2015, results 2017): see table in SRC-TG-402, p. 90.
- Irrigation debts 2016: 50 MTND total; GDAs 37 MTND; private farmers to CRDAs 11.4 MTND; SMVDA 1.7 MTND (SRC-TG-402, p. 91).
- Three GDAs in Jendouba and Siliana owe their CRDA about twice their expected annual revenue, a "quasi-faillite" (SRC-TG-485, p. 29).
- Drinking-water GDAs (1,459 groups, about 1,830 systems, 1.6 million people): debts 10.7 MTND (3 SONEDE, 7.7 STEG); cost 0.863 TND/m3 (0.4-1.8) vs tariff 0.730 TND/m3 (0.3-1.5); 20% cover full maintenance (SRC-TG-484, p. 16). A 2014 WMC report relaying SONEDE workshop data said 1,327 drinking-water groups owed 38 MTND, including 20.7 MTND to CRDAs and 1.8 MTND to SONEDE; the reported STEG amount is garbled in the article and is excluded (SRC-TG-465, tier C).
- GDA representatives: "total inability" to pay debts to STEG accumulated since 2000 (SRC-TG-484, p. 23). STEG cuts power to borehole schemes for unpaid bills (SRC-TG-485, p. 28).
- In the analyzed sample of 681 drinking-water GDAs across 15 governorates, users paid a weighted average 0.71 TND/m3; the report compares the typical 41 m3/year user (about 10 m3 a quarter) with SONEDE's 0.2 TND/m3 first block (SRC-TG-487, pp. 1, 3). It explicitly says the results do not cover all Tunisian GDAs (p. 25); GDA tariffs range from 0.200 to 1.500 TND/m3 (SRC-TG-402, p. 93). The ARP committee recommended unifying water tariffs for all citizens (SRC-TG-484, p. 113). So rural users of GDAs pay more for lower-quality service, while the GDAs still do not cover their costs.

**Sources disagree, or measure different things; all values are kept.**
- *Irrigation cost coverage:* 15% for dam water to public schemes in 2018 (SRC-TG-482); 23-64% for sampled perimeters depending on type and on whether renewal is counted (SRC-TG-402); "no more than 40%" with a full cost of 1.45-1.60 TND/m3 against tariffs of 0.20-0.60 TND/m3 (SRC-TG-489, tier C, citing ONAGRI 2023). These cover different scopes (bulk dam water vs perimeter-level service), so they do not contradict each other, but they cannot be combined.
- *GDA debts:* 37 MTND for irrigation GDAs to CRDAs in 2016 (SRC-TG-402); 10.7 MTND for drinking-water GDAs to SONEDE and STEG around 2021 (SRC-TG-484); 38 MTND for drinking-water groups in 2014, mostly to CRDAs (SRC-TG-465); "close to 600 MTND" for GDAs and SDAs to STEG and SONEDE (SRC-TG-489, opinion column, unidentified source). The 600 MTND figure is far above every official figure and is not verified.

## Why confidence is what it is

The pricing facts and the debt mechanism rest on a ministry report (tier A), the parliamentary record (tier A) and CIRAD/World Bank reports (tier B). The trend `worsening` rests on the CIRAD statement that GDA debts to CRDAs "continuent souvent d'augmenter" (SRC-TG-485), on the GDA representatives' account of debts to STEG accumulated since 2000 (SRC-TG-484) and on the price frozen since 2004; there is no official debt time series. The three-GDA debt ratio is dated 2016 from the guide's writing date (the guide does not state the year of the accounts). The GDA-to-STEG debt total is not established (sources range from 7.7 MTND to an unverified 600 MTND), and the CIRAD evidence is a sample of 11 northern GDAs. Hence `medium`.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture.** Cheap irrigation water feeds overuse: the CCDR says the low cost of irrigation water has led to its over-exploitation (SRC-TG-482, p. 8). The preferential tariff for "strategic" crops is an agricultural subsidy channelled through the water price (SRC-TG-402).
- **Energy.** Pumped schemes and boreholes depend on STEG electricity; unpaid STEG bills lead to power cuts and thus irrigation cuts (SRC-TG-485), and drinking-water GDAs owe STEG 7.7 MTND (SRC-TG-484). The STEG debt sits on the energy utility's balance sheet.
- **Groundwater.** The minister told parliament the ministry was grouping farmers with unauthorised wells (Kebili) into water groups to control consumption (SRC-TG-484, p. 12); illegal wells are covered in FND-TG-400.

## Verification

The price, cost-recovery and group-debt figures are tied to their source years and survey populations. The title and period now refer to the historical evidence through 2021. No later national tariff, debt trajectory or maintenance outcome is inferred.
