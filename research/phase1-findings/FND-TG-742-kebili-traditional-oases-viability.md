## Problem

The old collective oases of Kebili, the three-layer palm, fruit-tree and crop systems, are under a combined squeeze. Their area is small and nearly static: legal oases (which may also include the few state-created ones) covered at most 10,485 ha in 2018, against an estimated 32,700 ha of extensions in 2020 (SRC-TG-740). Dagher et al. round this to 10,500 ha of "traditional oases" (SRC-TG-741). Plots get water only every 25 to 60 days from collective boreholes (SRC-TG-741). The typical three layers and high crop diversity are now found less often than expected in traditional oases (SRC-TG-745). Real labour costs for date production grew 14.5% a year from 2015 to 2022 (SRC-TG-741). A 2030 business-as-usual scenario projects that labour will be 86% of Deglet Nour production costs in traditional oases and that Deglet Nour income there will turn negative. Common varieties would remain profitable (SRC-TG-741). The authors also report rising numbers of abandoned plots in 2023 and expect this to raise pest and disease pressure (SRC-TG-741).

## Root causes

Falling income and abandonment are the symptoms. The causes:

- **Water access.** Artesian wells had all dried out by the early 2000s, and pumped flows in collective networks were decreasing (SRC-TG-740). From the 2000s, nearby extensions "led to reduced water resource availability within these oases" (SRC-TG-740). Administrators also blame inefficient management of collective networks and poor maintenance (SRC-TG-740). Some farmers in traditional oases drill illegal private boreholes to top up their supply (SRC-TG-741).
- **Labour.** Young people are less interested in farming and migrate. Labour costs have risen faster than inflation, and Deglet Nour harvesting and tending are labour-intensive (SRC-TG-741).
- **Land and soil.** Fragmentation and falling fertility made old oases less profitable (SRC-TG-740). Soil fertility loss is worsened by saline irrigation water (SRC-TG-745). Deep pumping raised shallow water tables and caused drainage problems (SRC-TG-740).
- **Link to the variety choice.** In traditional oases common varieties were already more profitable than Deglet Nour in 2022 when stress is accounted for. But farmers rarely replant there, because new trees would mean removing existing palms (SRC-TG-741). See FND-TG-740.

## Evidence

- Area: traditional oases are 18,590 ha nationally (32.4% of oasis area, 2018) (SRC-TG-743). In Kebili legal oases covered at most 10,485 ha in 2018 (SRC-TG-740); the source does not say whether this includes state-created oases, so the traditional area may be somewhat smaller.
- Water turns: 25-60 days between turns in traditional oases (SRC-TG-741).
- Agro-biodiversity: in a 52-plot survey (2020), high crop diversity and three vertical layers were less frequent than expected in traditional oases. Water availability and irrigation frequency were the key factors behind the change (SRC-TG-745).
- Projection: by 2030, under continued labour-cost growth, labour would be 84% (extensions) and 86% (traditional oases) of Deglet Nour production costs. Deglet Nour income in traditional oases would become negative (SRC-TG-741).

## Why confidence is what it is

Low. The projection comes from a single peer-reviewed study (SRC-TG-741) based on 123 farmers in two parts of Kebili. It is a simple extrapolation of one cost trend, with no water, climate or price modelling. The 2030 result depends heavily on labour costs continuing to grow at 14.5% a year in real terms. The water-access and biodiversity evidence is solid in direction but rests on small samples (SRC-TG-745) and interviews (SRC-TG-740). Abandonment is reported qualitatively only. Gabes, Tozeur and Gafsa traditional oases are not covered by the sources opened, so this finding is limited to Kebili.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water:** the decline of traditional oases is driven partly by falling collective water supply and by the extensions' pumping from the same aquifers (SRC-TG-740). Shallow water tables rising to cause drainage problems and salinity are water-quality issues for the water lane (SRC-TG-740, SRC-TG-745).

## Leads for phase 2

- Promotion of common date varieties (Kenta, Alig, Gosbi) in traditional oases as a lower-labour, lower-risk option (SRC-TG-741)
- PDES "programme d'économie d'eau dans les oasis" (Japanese-funded concrete seguias from 1985), whose networks are now reported as leaking (SRC-TG-742)
- Nefzaoua and APIOS projects: integration of adjoining extensions into collective water turns, late 1980s (SRC-TG-740)
- Water user associations (GDA) managing collective boreholes (SRC-TG-741)
