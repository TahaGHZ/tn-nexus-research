## Problem

Since the 1970s, Tunisia has managed water scarcity mainly by building storage and moving water. Large works were programmed under the Water Master Plans for the North, Centre and South. A national mobilisation strategy, in its second decade in 2001-2010, aimed to mobilise more than 95% of conventional resources (SRC-TG-430). In 2007 the ministry's plan was 42 large dams by 2010 and 49 by 2015, with surface mobilisation peaking around 2,500 Mm3 in 2015. The plan also included an interconnection network linking most resources, with the Medjerda-Cap Bon canal extended to Sousse and Sfax (SRC-TG-430). By the end of 2017 the country had 37 large dams with 2,285 Mm3 of current capacity, and a surface mobilisation rate of 92% against the 95% target (SRC-TG-402). The model worked as designed: it made the coastal cities depend on a few northern reservoirs. That dependence is now the weak point of the system in drought years (FND-TG-425).

## Root causes

- **The decision.** The 2017 national water report itself says that the strategies since independence were built on supply management. It also says that awareness of the limits of this supply-centred policy dates back about three decades (SRC-TG-402, section I.1). Extract, not yet in SRC-TG-402:

  > La Tunisie a établi depuis l'indépendance des stratégies des ressources en eau selon une approche axée essentiellement sur la gestion de l'offre. [...] Depuis près de trois décennies, la prise de conscience s'est faite sur les limites d'une politique, centrée sur le développement de l'offre.

- **Why transfers.** 82% of surface water is in the north, against 12% in the centre and 6% in the south. SONEDE's policy is therefore to move water north to south and west to east (SRC-TG-428). The 2007 plan formalised this as an inter-connected reservoir network (SRC-TG-430). About 19% of water-sector investment went to reservoir dams and transfer mains, and 42% to irrigation schemes (SRC-TG-430).
- **What the strategy did not fix.** The targets were stated as volumes to mobilise. The plan did not address how the stock behaves in multi-year droughts, the energy cost of pumping, or how a growing drinking-water demand would compete with the irrigation the canal was first built for. These are effects observed later. This record does not claim that the planners ignored them; the strategy text itself was not read (see data_gaps).
- **Pricing.** SECADENORD's extra energy cost for Sidi Barrak water is about 75 millimes/m3, while its sale price to SONEDE is 56 millimes/m3. The energy overruns are paid by the state budget (SRC-TG-402, section VII.3 and energy section).

## Evidence

Current effects of the past decision (figures dated; all extracts below are from SRC-TG-402 and not yet pasted into that record):

- **Concentration on a few dams.** In 2017, 86.7% of SONEDE's surface water came from SECADENORD works on seven dams: Sidi Barrak, Sejnane, Joumine, Ziatine, Sidi Salem, Bouhertma and Nebhana. Only 13.3% came from SONEDE's own dams, Beni Metir, Kasseb and Masri (SRC-TG-402, drinking-water section, after Figure 13 "Répartition du volume produit en 2017 selon la source").
  > à partir des ouvrages gérés par la SECADENORD (ouvrages de transferts des barrages Sidi Barrak, Sejnane, Joumine, Ziatine, Sidi Salem, Bouhertma, et Nebhana) : 86,7% du total des eaux de surface prélevé.
- **One canal dominates the north.** Withdrawals from the Medjerda-Cap Bon canal account for 76% of SONEDE's total water intake (SRC-TG-428, p. 2-18; the JICA sentence gives no year). The same passage says 84% of SONEDE's total withdrawals are purchased from SECADENORD.
- **Demand on the canal grew fast.**
  > Le volume prélevé sur le Canal Medjerda Cap Bon a évolué de 205 Mm3 en 2008 à 317 Mm3 en 2017, soit 112 Mm3 (55%) de plus en 10 ans. (SRC-TG-402)
- **Drinking water displaced irrigation on the canal.**
  > aujourd'hui deux tiers des eaux disponibles à partir du canal Medjerda ­ Cap Bon sont réservés à l'eau potable, alors qu'à l'ouverture du canal les proportions étaient inversées (2/3 pour l'irrigation) [...] Un problème similaire est posé concernant l'exploitation des eaux du barrage Nehbana pour l'irrigation et l'eau potable au Sahel. (SRC-TG-402)
- **Transfer capacity flat while demand rises.** Surface transfer capacity is 800 Mm3, the same figure for 2016, 2017 and 2018 (SRC-TG-402, Tableau 12). The report expects transfers from the north to have to increase and calls for reinforcing the Sidi Barrak-Sejnane-Joumine-Medjerda line and the Medjerda-Cap Bon transfer. It considers a north-centre transfer possible once supply-demand balances are fixed (SRC-TG-402, section VII.3).
- **Energy.** Massive transfers from Sidi Barrak raised SECADENORD's energy spending by 63% in 2017 over 2016 (SRC-TG-402, executive summary). SECADENORD's electricity use for pumping went from 71.6 GWh (2015) to 101.9 GWh (2016) and 156.9 GWh (2017), +54% in one year. Its cost went from 13.0 to 18.3 and then 30.0 MDT, +63% (SRC-TG-402, Tableau 23). Of the 2017 total, the four transfer complexes (Sidi Barrak, Sedjnane-Joumine, Barbara, Zyatine) used 118.6 GWh (Tableau 24).
- **Planned versus achieved.** The 2007 plan foresaw 42 large dams by 2010 and 49 by 2015 (SRC-TG-430). The 2017 report counts 37 at the end of 2017 (SRC-TG-402). The same 2007 plan already forecast that useful storage would fall from 2,200 Mm3 (2005) to 1,800 Mm3 (2020) once siltation was counted (SRC-TG-430). The strategy thus knew its stock would shrink (FND-TG-427).

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture.** The canal was opened mainly for irrigation; two thirds of its water now goes to drinking supply, which squeezes Cap Bon irrigation (Nabeul) and Sahel irrigation from Nebhana (SRC-TG-402).
- **Energy.** SECADENORD's pumping electricity more than doubled, from 71.6 GWh in 2015 to 156.9 GWh in 2017, and its cost rose 63% in 2017 alone. The report attributes the rise to massive transfers from Sidi Barrak and the Sedjnane-Joumine complex (SRC-TG-402). Transfer energy is a structural cost of the chosen model. In 2017, a dry year, it rose with the volume pumped from the far north.
- **Public finance.** Energy overruns are covered by the state budget (SRC-TG-402).
