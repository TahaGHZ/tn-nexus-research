## Problem

A run of dry years culminated in the 2022/23 season. The latest INS annual previous-year-price series reports a 16.1% contraction for agriculture, forestry and fishing in 2023 (SRC-TG-890). In its spring 2024 publication, the World Bank reported an 11% fall in agricultural value added (SRC-TG-883) and GDP grew only 0.4%. The World Bank estimates growth would have been 1.8% had agriculture and agro-industry grown at their 2022 rate (SRC-TG-883).

- **Water restrictions.** The government introduced irrigation restrictions from March 2023 (SRC-TG-883, SRC-TG-882). In April 2023 it began night-time cuts to household water supply and prohibited "l'utilisation de l'eau pour l'irrigation ou l'arrosage des espaces verts et d'autres zones publiques" (the use of water for irrigation or watering green spaces and other public areas) (SRC-TG-881). The scope of that prohibition for farm irrigation is not clear from the source.
- **Cereals.** The harvest collapsed to a fraction of normal, and the 2022/23 durum wheat crop was not enough to guarantee seed for the next season (SRC-TG-700).
- **Recovery.** Output recovered in 2024 (about 1.5 million t of cereals) and 2025 (about 1.7 million t, 18% above the five-year average) (SRC-TG-700).

The evidence supports "a severe multi-year drought with large agricultural losses, followed by recovery". It does not support "a continuous 2016-2024 drought" or "an observed long-term decline in rainfall".

## Root causes

The symptom is crop failure and a fall in value added. The direct cause is the climatic shock: several below-average rainfall years ending in the very dry, hot 2022/23 season (SRC-TG-883, SRC-TG-700). The cause of the large economic effect is structural exposure:

- **Rainfed dominance.** About 90% of arable land is rainfed (SRC-TG-881), and irrigated wheat is under 15% of wheat area (SRC-TG-700).
- **Irrigation buffer reduced.** In the worst year the authorities restricted irrigation, reducing the irrigated sector's buffer (SRC-TG-883).
Warming is a background aggravating factor. The 4th National Communication attaches about +2.1°C to both 1978-2012 and 1951-2010 (INM 2015) (SRC-TG-885). The abstract of a peer-reviewed 16-station study reports significant warming of about 0.03°C per year over 1958-2020 (SRC-TG-889, abstract only). Over 62 years, that rate implies about 1.9°C, consistent with the NC4's 1951-2010 dating; the discrepancy is with its 1978-2012 dating, not with the amount of warming over the longer period. The warming direction is consistent across these sources.

## Evidence

**Measured damage.**

- **Risk financing and loss allocation.** These facts describe who bears the losses, not why output fell: agricultural insurance was 0.42% of premiums in 2021 (SRC-TG-881). The CCDR reports, citing the agriculture ministry through a trade magazine, that 40% of TND 345 million in climate-hazard losses over 2014-2022 was borne by the state (SRC-TG-881).

- **Value added:**
  - -9% in real terms in H1 2023 against H1 2022 (SRC-TG-882).
  - -11.2% in first-half value added between 2019 and 2023 (SRC-TG-882).
  - -11% for full-year 2023 (SRC-TG-883).

  The -11% and the -11.2% are different measures and should not be confused.
- **Cereal output.** The estimates for 2023 disagree:
  - CCDR: a forecast fall "from 750,000 to 250,000 t", barely enough for seed (SRC-TG-881).
  - World Bank: -61% against 2022 on Office des Céréales sales data, just over 22% of the 2019 level (SRC-TG-883).
  - GIEWS: 302,000 t in November 2023, revised to 520,000 t in May 2024 (SRC-TG-700).

  OdC sales measure grain collected, not the full harvest, which partly explains the spread. Detailed cereal analysis is in FND-TG-701.
- **Seed system.** Four consecutive drought years weakened the seed production system (SRC-TG-700).
- **Labour.** Unemployment rose from 15.2% (Q4 2022) to 16.4% (Q4 2023). The World Bank attributes this partly to agriculture and construction, but gives no agriculture-only figure (SRC-TG-883).

**How long was the drought? Sources disagree.**

- **Sixth year:** the World Bank's Autumn 2023 monitor calls 2023 the sixth consecutive year of below-average rainfall (SRC-TG-882).
- **Fourth year:** its Spring 2024 monitor calls it the fourth (SRC-TG-883).
- **Four years since 2019:** the CCDR speaks of four drought years since 2019 (SRC-TG-880, SRC-TG-881).

  The Spring 2024 monitor is internally inconsistent: it calls 2023 the fourth consecutive drought year and also describes dry conditions over the previous four years (SRC-TG-883). FAO GIEWS likewise describes four consecutive years in its May 2024 brief (SRC-TG-700). The Autumn 2023 monitor's sixth-year framing remains inconsistent with these later accounts (SRC-TG-882).

Cereal output in 2023 is described as just over 22% of the 2019 level (SRC-TG-883), which implies 2019 was a good harvest year. A continuous drought from 2016 is therefore not supported; the common framing of "2016-2024" should be read as "several dry spells, worst in 2020-2023". The 4th National Communication separately refers to a 2016-2018 drought (SRC-TG-885).

**Long-term observed rainfall trend.**

- **The 4th National Communication contradicts itself:**
  - It reports a slight, non-significant upward rainfall trend for 1978-2012 (SRC-TG-885, p. 19).
  - It also cites INM's 2015 finding of a 2-16% decline relative to 1961-1990, and an increase in drought-affected area and in extreme-drought frequency (SRC-TG-885, p. 130).
- **Peer-reviewed station study:** the abstract of a 16-station study for 1958-2020 reports no statistically significant annual rainfall trend at any station (SRC-TG-889, abstract only; full text not opened).

The robust observed signal is warming; an observed rainfall decline is contested. The drying is a projection (FND-TG-880), not yet an established measured trend.

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium.

- INS and the World Bank provide separate vintages; their sector labels and reference periods are retained (SRC-TG-883, SRC-TG-890).
- **Contested or weak:**
  - the cereal tonnage for 2023 (several estimates);
  - the number of consecutive drought years (sources disagree);
  - the long-term rainfall trend is contested; the NC4's 2.1°C figure is internally misdated on p. 19 but is consistent with the longer 1951-2010 period and the independent station-study rate;
  - the TND 345 million loss figure, which rests on trade press (tier C).
- **Not opened:** official INM rainfall bulletins and INS national accounts were not opened directly, and the peer-reviewed trend study was read as an abstract only.
- **Trend set to unknown.** Annual output swings with rainfall (recovery in 2024-2025), and warming makes dry years more damaging, but drought frequency is not yet measurably rising in the sources opened.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water.** The drought turned into a water-allocation crisis. Irrigation was restricted from March 2023, and from April 2023 night-time household water cuts were added to a prohibition on using water for irrigation and for watering public green spaces (SRC-TG-881, SRC-TG-883).
- **Groundwater.** The 4th National Communication reports that after the 2016-2018 drought farmers offset surface-water shortfalls with groundwater, even saline groundwater. This kept measured production losses small but transferred the stress to aquifers (SRC-TG-885); see the water lane's groundwater records.
- **Fiscal.** Cereal import requirements for 2023/24 were forecast at 4.7 million t, about 30% above the previous year's average requirement (SRC-TG-700). This adds to the import bill and the subsidy burden (see FND-TG-700, FND-TG-702).

## Leads for phase 2

- The CCDR mentions budget compensation through a fund for climate-related agricultural losses ("Fonds pour les pertes agricoles liées au climat"), and for SMEs through the 2011 Fund and Law 24-2019 (SRC-TG-881, p. 11)
- Index-based or area-yield agricultural insurance (recommended in the World Bank CCDR and the Autumn 2023 Economic Monitor)
- Plan National de la Sécheresse 2020 (updated National Drought Plan), NDC adaptation measure 8 (SRC-TG-884)
- Hydrometeorological services and early warning (recommended in the CCDR and the 4th National Communication)
- Emergency seed supply for the season after a drought harvest (gap flagged by GIEWS, SRC-TG-700)

## Verification

INS’s 3 August 2026 national accounts give -16.1% for calendar 2023 and +8.6% for 2024 in agriculture, forestry and fishing at previous-year prices. The spring 2024 World Bank estimate (-11%) is preserved as an earlier publication, separate from half-year measures. The annual accounts do not isolate the causal share attributable to drought (SRC-TG-890, SRC-TG-883).
