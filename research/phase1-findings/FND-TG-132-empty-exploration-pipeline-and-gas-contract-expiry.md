## Problem

The ministry's 2035 strategy warns that, on current trends, the energy deficit (excluding the
gas royalty) could reach 95% of demand by 2035, against about 57% in 2021 (SRC-TG-103). FND-TG-102
records this projection from the power-mix angle. This finding looks at the supply side the
strategy relies on to avoid it. To keep dependence between 33% and 76% in 2035, the strategy
counts on developing fossil resources: the Zarat gas field "d'ici au plus tard 2030" and an
exploration relaunch with "un objectif indicatif de 30 puits d'ici 2035" (SRC-TG-103). Three
years into the plan, exploration has barely moved. No permit was granted in 2024, 2025 or
January-September 2026. One exploration well was drilled in 2024, none in 2025 and two in
January-September 2026. There was one discovery in 2024 and none since (SRC-TG-221, SRC-TG-100).
The 2026 pace roughly matches what the target needs, but it starts from a base of only 11 valid
permits. The Zarat research permit
reached the end of its validity on 11 August 2025 (SRC-TG-221). Meanwhile the main import
contract, Sonatrach-STEG, was renewed in 2020 only to 2027, with a two-year option (SRC-TG-140,
tier C).

## Root causes

- **Depleting reserve base, inherited (technical).** Production has fallen since 2010
  (FND-TG-130, FND-TG-131). New discoveries take years to bring on stream, so the 2035 picture is
  largely set by exploration done in the late 2020s.
- **Exploration barely restarting (institutional, legacy).** Valid permits fell from 25 in March
  2020 to 11 in September 2026 (FND-TG-131). The strategy target of 30 wells over 2023-2035 means
  about 2-3 exploration wells a year (computed from SRC-TG-103). The observed rate was 1 in 2024,
  0 in 2025 and 2 in January-September 2026 (SRC-TG-221, SRC-TG-100). With no new permits granted,
  the acreage available to drill keeps shrinking.
  The causes of the stall are not documented in the sources opened (see FND-TG-130 for the
  historical factors).
- **Single-supplier contract horizon (economic, legacy).** The 1997 gas contract was extended in
  2020 by eight years to 2027, "avec possibilité d'extension de 2 ans", with a 20% volume increase
  from 2025 (SRC-TG-140). Purchased Algerian gas was 57% of national gas supply in 2025
  (SRC-TG-221). The contract therefore comes up for renegotiation at a time of growing dependence.
  This dependence is not a problem in itself, but it leaves little bargaining room. The contract
  dates rest on a press relay of a Sonatrach communiqué only.

## Evidence

- Projection: 95% deficit in 2035 on the trend; 33-76% under the strategy scenario; 57% in 2021
  (SRC-TG-103). The base year is consistent with ONEM's 2021 balance. That balance shows a deficit
  of 5,598 ktep without royalty against 9,714 ktep of demand, about 58% (SRC-TG-133, computed).
- Strategy supply measures: Zarat development by 2030, 30 exploration wells by 2035, STIR
  refinery extension before 2030, LNG infrastructure 2027-2030 (SRC-TG-103).
- Observed exploration: 2019 had 6 permits granted, 6 exploration wells and 4 discoveries
  (SRC-TG-131). 2024 had 0 permits, 1 well and 1 discovery. 2025 had 0 permits, 0 wells, 0
  discoveries and 3 permits abandoned (SRC-TG-221). January-September 2026 had 0 permits, 2 wells
  and 0 discoveries (SRC-TG-100).
- Contract: Sonatrach-STEG amendment to the 3 March 1997 contract, renewed to 2027 with a 2-year
  option and +20% volume from 2025 (SRC-TG-140).

## Why confidence is what it is

Low. The 95% projection comes from one scenario document published only as a synthesis, with no
production assumptions (SRC-TG-103). The exploration record is official and solid (SRC-TG-221,
SRC-TG-100), but a few years of low activity do not by themselves prove the 2035 outcome. A single
discovery or the Zarat development could change it. The contract expiry rests on a single tier C
source (SRC-TG-140). No independent forecast (IEA, OIES, ETAP) was opened.
