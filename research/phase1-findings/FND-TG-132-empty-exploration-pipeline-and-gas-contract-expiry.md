## Verification status

The strategy target and ONEM activity series were checked separately. ONEM’s December 2025 well history explains the interim count of one and the final count of zero. Later permitting outcomes and the strategy’s field-level assumptions remain open questions.

## Problem

The Ministry of Industry and UNDP's 2035 energy-strategy synthesis sets out a supply-side pathway that includes developing the Zarat gas field by 2030 at the latest and relaunching oil and gas prospecting with an indicative target of 30 wells by 2035 (SRC-TG-103). It states that the strategy scenario's energy-dependence rate in 2035 would range from 33% to 76% depending on the pace of fossil-resource development. Separately, it says the deficit could reach 95% of demand by 2035 if current resource and demand trends continue; FND-TG-102 covers that aggregate outlook. These are scenario statements, not observed outcomes or a field-by-field forecast.

The implementation indicators show limited activity in the recent period. ONEM's December 2025 full-year bulletin records no newly granted permits in 2024 or 2025, one exploration well and one discovery in 2024, and no exploration wells or discoveries in 2025 (SRC-TG-221). Its September 2026 bulletin records no permits granted and no discoveries in January-September 2026, two exploration wells in that period, and 11 valid permits at end-September, of which one was a prospecting permit (SRC-TG-100). The September bulletin's table reports one well for January-September 2025 but zero for full-year 2025; the year-end reports give zero for 2025. The December bulletin records the November reclassification of the well drilled in August (SRC-TG-221, printed p. 30).

The December 2025 ONEM bulletin lists the Zarat research permit's validity ending on 11 August 2025 (SRC-TG-221). This establishes the permit expiry, not the field's subsequent concession or development status. ONEM's 2015 and 2025 tables also show lower national dry-gas production (2,231 to 1,129 ktep-pci) and lower crude-oil-and-condensate resources (2,394 to 1,231 ktep-pci) (SRC-TG-221). These historical series contextualize the strategy's resource-development measures; they do not by themselves demonstrate reserves, future production, or why exploration has been limited.

## Root causes and interpretation

- **Technical supply context.** The strategy explicitly conditions its 2035 dependence range on the pace of fossil-resource development and names Zarat and the 30-well target among its measures (SRC-TG-103). ONEM's historical production/resource series show declines in national dry gas and crude oil/condensate between 2015 and 2025 (SRC-TG-221). The evidence supports describing a declining production context and a plan dependent in part on future resource development. It does not establish a quantified reserve-depletion forecast or prove that discoveries after a specific date would arrive too late for 2035.
- **Observed implementation gap, cause not established.** ONEM's permit, well and discovery series document low recent exploration activity, alongside a small number of valid permits and expiry of the Zarat research permit (SRC-TG-221, SRC-TG-100). The sources reviewed do not identify the administrative, financial, geological or commercial causes. The institutional classification is provisional taxonomy for the implementation gap, not a demonstrated causal diagnosis; `legacy_decision` is false because no specific legacy decision is established.

## Evidence and projections

- **Strategy scenarios (2035):** about 57% energy deficit in 2021 excluding the gas royalty; a possible 95% deficit by 2035 if current trends continue; and 33%-76% energy dependence in 2035 under the strategy scenario depending on fossil-resource development pace (SRC-TG-103). The 95% deficit and 33%-76% dependence range are different scenario metrics. The public synthesis does not expose field-level fossil-production assumptions.
- **Plan measures:** develop Zarat by 2030 at the latest and relaunch oil and gas prospecting with an indicative cumulative target of 30 wells by 2035 (SRC-TG-103). The same synthesis lists other supply measures, including STIR expansion and LNG infrastructure; they are not treated as evidence that the exploration measures are already underway.
- **Observed exploration:** 2024: zero permits granted, one exploration well and one discovery. Full-year 2025: zero permits, zero wells, zero discoveries and three permits abandoned. These are reported in ONEM's December 2025 bulletin (SRC-TG-221). January-September 2026: zero permits granted, two exploration wells and zero discoveries. At end-September 2026, ONEM lists 11 valid permits, including one prospecting permit (SRC-TG-100).
- **Interim and final counts.** The August well was classified as exploration in September and reclassified as development on 21 November 2025. The two figures refer to successive classifications, not two different wells (SRC-TG-100, SRC-TG-221, printed p. 30).
- **Historical production context:** ONEM reports national dry-gas production of 2,231 ktep-pci in 2015 and 1,129 ktep-pci in 2025; its crude oil and condensate resource row reports 2,394 and 1,231 ktep-pci respectively (SRC-TG-221). The series are retrospective national quantities, not a reserve estimate or a future production projection.
- **Base-year cross-check:** ONEM's 2021 key-figures publication gives a 5,598 ktep deficit excluding royalty against 9,714 ktep demand, or 57.6% calculated (SRC-TG-133). This is consistent with the strategy's rounded 57% base-year statement; the values are kept distinct as a direct strategy figure and an independently calculated ONEM ratio.

## Why confidence is what it is

Low. The strategy's scenarios and measures are directly stated in the Ministry/UNDP synthesis, but the synthesis does not publish the fossil-production assumptions needed to assess the pathway quantitatively (SRC-TG-103). ONEM provides official exploration and production series (SRC-TG-100, SRC-TG-221); the December bulletin documents the subsequent reclassification of the August well. The available evidence documents low activity and declining historical production, but it neither identifies the causes nor establishes the 2035 production outcome. The Zarat permit's subsequent status also remains unconfirmed.
