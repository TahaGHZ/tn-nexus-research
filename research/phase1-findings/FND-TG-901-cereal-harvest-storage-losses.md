## Problem

Part of Tunisia's cereal harvest is lost before it reaches mills, at harvest, in on-farm storage and at collection centres. Until the FAO/Italy project of 2016-2018 no chain-level estimate existed; the FAO evaluation calls that project's work the first-ever estimate of losses in the cereal and milk chains (SRC-TG-904), and the project's own diagnostic said in 2017 that few data exist on the scale of losses at each stage (SRC-TG-901). The one measurement, in Bizerte and Siliana, reached the public only through press. One article gives average farm-level losses of 4.6% in Bizerte to 7.3% in Siliana and collection losses of 0.38% and 0.24% (SRC-TG-908); another gives post-harvest losses of 3.6% to 5.4% for the same governorates (SRC-TG-909). The losses were estimated to cost each cereal farmer 832 TND a year in Bizerte and 578 TND in Siliana (SRC-TG-908). In a country that depends heavily on cereal imports (FND-TG-700), a loss of a few percent of a harvest that already swings with rainfall (FND-TG-701) matters for import needs, but its national size is unknown.

## Root causes

- **Harvest machinery.** The FAO/ministry diagnostic says mechanical harvesting in Tunisia is "réputée pour les importantes pertes qu'elle occasionne". It cites the ministry for 60% of all farm machines being over 25 years old, which "occasionne une perte de 100 millions de dinars par an" (SRC-TG-901); the figure covers all farm machinery and the nature of the loss is not stated, so it is not a grain-loss estimate. Its example of 10% harvest losses (1.7 million quintals on a 17 million quintal harvest) is a hypothetical illustration, not a measurement (SRC-TG-901). Small farmers depend on combine owners and have little bargaining power over the service (SRC-TG-901).
- **On-farm storage.** Farmers who store grain to sell gradually are not trained in storage practice. The gap between harvest and collection (collection is 40-50% of the harvest) is usually counted as self-consumption, seed and feed, but probably contains losses that are hard to quantify (SRC-TG-901).
- **Collection centres.** The diagnostic lists manual handling, old or missing equipment, old concrete silos, frequent open-air storage or uncovered "Australian" bunker silos, fragile plastic sacks and, in good rainy years, extra open-air storage that exposes grain to birds, rodents, rain and theft (SRC-TG-901).
- **Institutional set-up (legacy decision).** The Office des Céréales fixes prices and margins at every stage, and the CGC covers its "sales at a loss", so most storage and logistics costs fall on the state (SRC-TG-901). Collection privatisation looks like sub-contracting rather than real privatisation, and the Office does not pay millers for storage, so they under-invest in silos (SRC-TG-901). The history of this system is in FND-TG-702.
- **Coordination.** In Bizerte and Siliana, the study notes weak contracting, small farms with little access to technical support, and a parallel circuit of intermediaries and informal trade (SRC-TG-901).

## Evidence

See indicators. Measured rates exist only for two northern governorates and come from press summaries of the FAO/ministry phase 2 study (SRC-TG-908, SRC-TG-909). The two articles disagree; they may describe different scopes (farm stage only vs all post-harvest stages), but neither says so, so both values are recorded. The FAO "19%" for cereal losses and waste is a Near East and North Africa regional estimate, not a Tunisian one (SRC-TG-901).

**Future.** The only projection found concerns stored wheat in north-western Tunisia for 2041-2070 under RCP4.5 and RCP8.5. Hours suitable for cooling grain by ambient aeration fall by 25% on average, weakening a key pest-control method, but the modelled lesser grain borer (*Rhyzopertha dominica*) reproduces 20-30% less and survives 5-10% less than in 1970-1997 (SRC-TG-905). The study does not estimate losses and its results point in opposite directions, so it does not support a "predicted" finding that storage losses will rise. Only the abstract was read.

## Why confidence is what it is

Low. The causes rest on one FAO/ministry diagnostic (tier B) that itself says the losses were not yet measured. The only loss rates are tier C (press summaries of an unpublished or unfound report), cover two governorates and one season, and disagree with each other. There is no national figure from FAOSTAT or the SDG database. The 60% machinery age figure is second-hand (the diagnostic citing the ministry) and covers all farm machinery, not combines only.

## Links to other sectors

- Water: none found.
- Energy: none found.
- Climate and drought: the size of the harvest, and so of open-air storage, varies with rainfall (FND-TG-701; SRC-TG-901); climate change reduces aeration hours for stored wheat (SRC-TG-905).

## Leads for phase 2

- National action plans and loss-reduction charter for the cereal chain with the central wheat cooperative, FAO project GCP/RNE/004/ITA (SRC-TG-904)
- Planned integration of loss measurement into the ministry's annual agricultural surveys (SRC-TG-904)
- Investment code 2017 implementing decrees: up to 50% support for renewing farm machinery (SRC-TG-901)
- Ambient-air aeration and adaptive pest management for stored wheat (SRC-TG-905)
- World Bank-supported grain value chain reforms, including digital tracking of durum distribution (in FND-TG-702, SRC-TG-705)
