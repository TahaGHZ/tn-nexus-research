## Verification status

Article 5 of the 15 July 2016 joint order documents the quarterly fuel-price procedure (SRC-TG-175). The IMF’s January 2014 benchmark and announced changes to the future review frequency are separate events (SRC-TG-163). The IMF announcement of 15 October 2022 confirms only a staff-level agreement conditional on Board approval (SRC-TG-165). STEG guarantees reported in the press are not an audited liability; the national budget execution figures are distinct from utility cash receipts.

## Problem

Tunisia’s state budget execution report records total subsidies of 11,999 million TND in 2022, 11,479.5 million in 2023, 11,347.5 million in 2024 and 11,593 million provisionally in 2025. Its “Carburants” line records 7,628, 7,030, 7,086 and 7,112 million TND respectively (SRC-TG-160). For 2024 the Ministry report explicitly describes the subsidy amounts as paid; however, the 7,086 million TND total and the 4,019 million TND STEG component match finance-law allocations (SRC-TG-161, SRC-TG-173), so the report’s cash-versus-booked interpretation remains unclear. The 2025 provisional values equal the 2025 finance-law appropriations. This is the Ministry report’s “Carburants” line; it does not split every year between STIR and STEG. In 2024 total subsidies were reported as 20% of budget expenditure and 7.1% of GDP; the Ministry report labels 4,019 million TND for STEG and 3,067 million for STIR as paid (SRC-TG-161). The STEG and STIR amounts equal the PLF 2024 allocations (SRC-TG-173), so it is unclear whether the execution report represents cash paid or booked appropriations. Subsidies to STEG rose from 1% of GDP in 2021 to 2.5%
in 2023 (SRC-TG-105). Even after subsidy STEG is in financial distress: it lost 5.0 billion dinars
before subsidy in 2022 and 4.5 billion in 2023, has cumulative losses of 3.68 billion dinars,
negative equity of 1.59 billion and current liabilities of 10.1 billion at end-2023, and its
auditors qualified its 2021-2023 accounts (SRC-TG-105). The World Bank PAD, describing conditions at its 2025 preparation, says average electricity tariff cost recovery was 60% and that the government was not considering tariff increases at that stage (SRC-TG-105). Press reports say fuel prices were held after 2022 increases and the adjustment mechanism suspended (SRC-TG-168), but no official decision or last retail price-change record was located.

## Root causes

Symptom: a large, oil-price-driven subsidy bill and a utility that cannot pay its suppliers.
Causes:

1. **Tariffs below cost, by choice.** The average electricity tariff covers 60% of the supply cost
   (SRC-TG-105). The government prefers efficiency measures to tariff increases because of social
   vulnerability and competitiveness (SRC-TG-105, paras. 3 and 11). According to the PLF 2027 as
   reported in press, the subsidy covers 44% of the real cost of household low-voltage electricity
   and 60% of household low-pressure gas, against 18% for high and medium voltage (SRC-TG-167).
2. **Fuel-price adjustment commitment and reported suspension.** The authorities’ 2016 memorandum described a symmetric automatic petroleum-product formula to be adopted in May 2016, introduced quarterly from July and monthly from January 2017 (SRC-TG-163). The IMF reported a 2% monthly cap in 2021 (SRC-TG-164). A press report counts four increases by 21 October 2022, up 15.9% cumulatively (SRC-TG-169); another counts five for all of 2022 and says the mechanism was then suspended (SRC-TG-168). These reports are not an official suspension record, so the timing and current operation remain unconfirmed. The October 2022 IMF staff-level agreement proposed a 48-month, US$1.9 billion EFF, subject to Board approval, and described regular international-price-linked adjustments (SRC-TG-165). The consulted IMF country page records the latest Article IV consultation but does not settle the EFF outcome; that outcome remains a data gap.
3. **Late and incomplete subsidy payment, and unpaid public bills.** Subsidy delays and
   shortfalls force STEG to borrow short and medium term and delay payments to suppliers
   (SRC-TG-105). Public-sector customers owed STEG 22 months of bills at end-2023 (SRC-TG-105). At
   end-June 2020 the government owed all SOEs 8.8 billion dinars in arrears, and the IMF called for
   an audit and clearance starting with STIR and STEG (SRC-TG-164). The 2027 draft budget includes
   about 500 MDT to settle part of STEG's claims for 2017-2023 (SRC-TG-167, press). Together these sources document linked fiscal, utility and customer-payment pressures, but do not quantify the current circular-debt stock or establish that all supplier delays are caused by these same arrears.
4. **Import-price exposure.** Gas for power is largely imported and partly indexed to Brent
   (SRC-TG-105; see FND-TG-101). A press account of draft 2027 budget documents attributes an estimated 172 million TND increase in subsidies to each US$1/bbl Brent increase and 59 million TND to each 10-millime rise in the US dollar exchange rate; a separate press-cited expert estimate is 140–150 million TND per Brent dollar (SRC-TG-170, SRC-TG-168). These are estimates, not outturns. The same press account reports Brent at US$91.21/bbl through August 2026, versus US$71.21/bbl in the same period of 2025, and an expected 2026 average of US$90/bbl, compared with the 2026 budget assumption of US$63.3/bbl (SRC-TG-170).
5. Network losses add to STEG's costs (18.4% in 2023, SRC-TG-105); this is covered in FND-TG-221.

**The 2022 peak and subsequent reported series.** Total subsidies were 11,999 MDT in 2022 and the “Carburants” line was 7,628 MDT (SRC-TG-160); STEG's loss before subsidy was 5.0 billion TND the same year
(SRC-TG-105). After 2022 hydrocarbon subsidies fell only slightly, to 7,030-7,112 MDT in 2023-2025
(SRC-TG-160). Pre-2022 amounts in dinars were not sourced; subsidies to STEG alone were 1% of GDP
in 2021 against 2.5% in 2023 (SRC-TG-105). The nominal budget line is not inflation-adjusted and does not prove a causal link to retail prices.

**Who decides:** SRC-TG-163 describes the 2016 formula in the authorities’ memorandum; the Ministry-of-Industry decree benchmark elsewhere in that report refers to an earlier SBA and is not evidence of the legal instrument for the 2016 formula. The July 2016 quarterly order is documented in SRC-TG-175. No subsequent official suspension decision was located. The authority that sets electricity tariffs is not sourced here (see data gaps). SRC-TG-161 splits the 2024 “Carburants” payment between STIR and STEG. The government said it was not considering tariff increases at the time of World Bank appraisal (SRC-TG-105). STEG, MIME and the Ministry of Finance signed a five-year performance contract on 5 February 2025 (SRC-TG-105).

## Evidence

- Subsidy series 2022-2025, total and "Carburants" (SRC-TG-160, p. 8). The 2025 values equal the
  LF 2025 appropriation exactly and are provisional.
- 2024: 11,348 MDT subsidies, 20% of expenditure, 7.1% of GDP; hydrocarbons 7,086 MDT = STIR 3,067
  + STEG 4,019 (SRC-TG-161, p. 35).
- STEG: losses before subsidy, subsidy of TND 4.0 billion in 2023, cost recovery 60%, collection
  rates, cumulative losses, negative equity, current liabilities, qualified audits (SRC-TG-105,
  pp. 1, 3, 21).
- Fuel pricing: 2016 authorities’ memorandum (SRC-TG-163), 2021 2% monthly cap (SRC-TG-164), and attributed 2022 increase/suspension reports (tier C; SRC-TG-168, SRC-TG-169).
- 2025-2027 budget figures from PLF 2026 and PLF 2027 as reported in press (SRC-TG-166,
  SRC-TG-167, SRC-TG-170).

**Disagreements recorded, not resolved:**
- 2025: 7,112 MDT in the Ministry’s provisional “Carburants” execution line (equal to LF 2025 appropriation, SRC-TG-160) versus 5,719 MDT for electricity and petroleum subsidies cited from PLF 2026 by Managers (tier C, SRC-TG-166). The scopes and vintages differ and are not reconciled. A reported 8,060 MDT PLF 2025 figure remains unverified.
- Subsidy to STEG: TND 4.0 billion in 2023 (SRC-TG-105) and 4,019 MDT in 2024 (SRC-TG-161) are
  different years and are kept separate.
- Brent assumption for LF 2026: US$63.3/bbl in three press reports (SRC-TG-166, SRC-TG-167, SRC-TG-170) versus US$73/bbl in another (SRC-TG-168); unresolved press discrepancy.
- Number of 2022 fuel price increases: four by 21 October (SRC-TG-169) vs five for the year
  (SRC-TG-168).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium for the core finding: the subsidy scale is supported by a Ministry execution series and the 2024 Ministry report, while STEG’s financial stress is documented in a World Bank appraisal drawing on company accounts and mainly describes end-2023. The press-only fuel-price freeze, 2025 comparison with a different reported budget scope, and 2026–2027 draft-budget sensitivities are low-confidence attributed claims; they do not establish the core trend or current status. The provisional 2025 execution value equals appropriation, and the 2024 “paid” amount may be booked appropriation.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water:** none found in the sources opened. Electricity tariffs for pumping (SONEDE, irrigation)
  were not covered.
- **Agriculture:** none found in the sources opened. Diesel and electricity subsidies may reach
  farms but no source quantified this.
- Within energy: the subsidy bill depends on gas import dependence (FND-TG-101) and on network
  losses (FND-TG-221).
