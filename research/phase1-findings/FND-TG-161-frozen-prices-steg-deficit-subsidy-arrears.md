## Problem

Tunisia spends about 11.3-12.0 billion dinars a year on subsidies, of which about 7.0-7.6 billion
goes to hydrocarbons (fuels through STIR, electricity and gas through STEG) in 2022-2025
(SRC-TG-160). In 2024 subsidies were 20% of budget expenditure and 7.1% of GDP, and STEG alone
received 4,019 million dinars (SRC-TG-161). Subsidies to STEG rose from 1% of GDP in 2021 to 2.5%
in 2023 (SRC-TG-105). Even after subsidy STEG is in financial distress: it lost 5.0 billion dinars
before subsidy in 2022 and 4.5 billion in 2023, has cumulative losses of 3.68 billion dinars,
negative equity of 1.59 billion and current liabilities of 10.1 billion at end-2023, and its
auditors qualified its 2021-2023 accounts (SRC-TG-105). The government is not considering tariff
increases (SRC-TG-105), and press sources report that retail fuel prices have been frozen since
the automatic adjustment mechanism was suspended after the 2022 increases (SRC-TG-168).

## Root causes

Symptom: a large, oil-price-driven subsidy bill and a utility that cannot pay its suppliers.
Causes:

1. **Tariffs below cost, by choice.** The average electricity tariff covers 60% of the supply cost
   (SRC-TG-105). The government prefers efficiency measures to tariff increases because of social
   vulnerability and competitiveness (SRC-TG-105, paras. 3 and 11). According to the PLF 2027 as
   reported in press, the subsidy covers 44% of the real cost of household low-voltage electricity
   and 60% of household low-pressure gas, against 18% for high and medium voltage (SRC-TG-167).
2. **Automatic fuel pricing adopted, then abandoned.** The mechanism committed to in 2016
   (FND-TG-160, SRC-TG-163) was operating in 2021 with monthly moves capped at 2% (SRC-TG-164).
   In 2022 prices were raised four times by 21 October (15.9% in total) (SRC-TG-169); another press
   source counts five increases in 2022, after which the mechanism was suspended (SRC-TG-168).
   The IMF staff-level agreement of October 2022 (US$1.9 billion, 48 months) was built on regular
   price adjustments linked to international prices and awaited board approval in December 2022
   (SRC-TG-165). The IMF country page in November 2026 lists the last Article IV consultation in
   February 2021 and shows no approval item (SRC-TG-171); the outcome itself was not sourced.
3. **Late and incomplete subsidy payment, and unpaid public bills.** Subsidy delays and
   shortfalls force STEG to borrow short and medium term and delay payments to suppliers
   (SRC-TG-105). Public-sector customers owed STEG 22 months of bills at end-2023 (SRC-TG-105). At
   end-June 2020 the government owed all SOEs 8.8 billion dinars in arrears, and the IMF called for
   an audit and clearance starting with STIR and STEG (SRC-TG-164). The 2027 draft budget includes
   about 500 MDT to settle part of STEG's claims for 2017-2023 (SRC-TG-167, press). This is a
   circular-debt problem inside the state: the budget under-pays STEG, ministries do not pay their
   bills, and STEG's suppliers (including other state firms) wait.
4. **Import-price exposure.** Gas for power is largely imported and partly indexed to Brent
   (SRC-TG-105; see FND-TG-101). With prices frozen, every dollar on Brent lands on the budget:
   about 172 MDT per dollar according to the PLF 2027 as reported (SRC-TG-170), or 140-150 MDT
   according to an expert quoted in press (SRC-TG-168). In 2026 Brent averaged 91.21 dollars to
   end-August against a 63.3-dollar budget assumption (SRC-TG-170).
5. Network losses add to STEG's costs (18.4% in 2023, SRC-TG-105); this is covered in FND-TG-221.

**The 2022 shock.** Total subsidies were 11,999 MDT in 2022, the highest of 2022-2025, and
hydrocarbons 7,628 MDT (SRC-TG-160); STEG's loss before subsidy was 5.0 billion TND the same year
(SRC-TG-105). After 2022 hydrocarbon subsidies fell only slightly, to 7,030-7,112 MDT in 2023-2025
(SRC-TG-160). Pre-2022 amounts in dinars were not sourced; subsidies to STEG alone were 1% of GDP
in 2021 against 2.5% in 2023 (SRC-TG-105). The link between the flat post-2022 bill and frozen
retail prices is plausible but not established by the sources opened.

**Who decides:** the automatic fuel formula was to be approved by a Ministry of Industry decree
(SRC-TG-163); the authority that sets electricity tariffs is not sourced here (see data gaps).
The hydrocarbon subsidy line in the state budget is split between STIR and STEG (SRC-TG-161). The
government has said it is not considering tariff increases (SRC-TG-105). STEG, MIME and the
Ministry of Finance signed a five-year performance contract on 5 February 2025 (SRC-TG-105).

## Evidence

- Subsidy series 2022-2025, total and "Carburants" (SRC-TG-160, p. 8). The 2025 values equal the
  LF 2025 appropriation exactly and are provisional.
- 2024: 11,348 MDT subsidies, 20% of expenditure, 7.1% of GDP; hydrocarbons 7,086 MDT = STIR 3,067
  + STEG 4,019 (SRC-TG-161, p. 35).
- STEG: losses before subsidy, subsidy of TND 4.0 billion in 2023, cost recovery 60%, collection
  rates, cumulative losses, negative equity, current liabilities, qualified audits (SRC-TG-105,
  pp. 1, 3, 21).
- Fuel pricing: 2016 commitment (SRC-TG-163), 2% monthly cap in 2021 (SRC-TG-164), 2022 increases
  (SRC-TG-169), suspension (SRC-TG-168).
- 2025-2027 budget figures from PLF 2026 and PLF 2027 as reported in press (SRC-TG-166,
  SRC-TG-167, SRC-TG-170).

**Disagreements recorded, not resolved:**
- 2025 hydrocarbon subsidies: 7,112 MDT (Ministry execution report, SRC-TG-160) vs 5,719 MDT (PLF
  2026 per press, SRC-TG-166). A third value, 8,060 MDT for the PLF 2025, appears in press but was
  not opened.
- Subsidy to STEG: TND 4.0 billion in 2023 (SRC-TG-105) and 4,019 MDT in 2024 (SRC-TG-161) are
  different years and are kept separate.
- Brent assumption in the 2026 finance law: US$63.3 (SRC-TG-166, SRC-TG-167, SRC-TG-170) vs US$73
  (SRC-TG-168).
- Number of 2022 fuel price increases: four by 21 October (SRC-TG-169) vs five for the year
  (SRC-TG-168).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The size of the subsidy bill is solid: it comes from two Ministry of Finance documents
(tier A) that agree for 2024. STEG's financial position comes from one World Bank appraisal
document (tier B) based on STEG's own accounts, which auditors qualified, and is dated end-2023;
newer audited figures were not found. The fuel price freeze and the suspension of the mechanism
rest only on press (tier C), so that part of the finding is low confidence on its own. The 2025
figure is provisional and conflicts with a press figure. The 2026-2027 numbers are press reports
of draft budget documents.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water:** none found in the sources opened. Electricity tariffs for pumping (SONEDE, irrigation)
  were not covered.
- **Agriculture:** none found in the sources opened. Diesel and electricity subsidies may reach
  farms but no source quantified this.
- Within energy: the subsidy bill depends on gas import dependence (FND-TG-101) and on network
  losses (FND-TG-221).
