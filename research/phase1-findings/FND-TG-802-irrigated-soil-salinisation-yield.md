## Problem

Where Tunisian farmers irrigate with saline groundwater, salts can accumulate in soils and yields can fall. In a small applied study in Kairouan and Mahdia, summer crops irrigated with saline water often lost more than 50%, and sometimes 75%, of maximum yield. The authors observed that farmers continued because family-farming costs were low (SRC-TG-810). In oases, the PAN-LCD cites a GIZ 2014 estimate that irrigation with water at 3.5 g/l deposits at least 60 t/ha of salt, difficult to remove when drainage is inefficient (SRC-TG-800).

The measured size of the problem is uncertain. A 2020 government programme cites about 10,000 ha of soil salinised through irrigation (Daghari et al. 2012); a 2011 thesis abstract says about 50% of irrigated land is "at high risk" (SRC-TG-800, SRC-TG-811). These figures describe different things and neither method was available in the cited extracts. The evidence supports yield and soil-salinity problems in specific perimeters and oases, but not a precise nationwide affected area or loss of irrigated land.

## Root causes

- **Saline irrigation water.** A 2006 study describes saline irrigation water and aquifer overexploitation in arid Tunisia; this is a local and older diagnosis, not a current national trend series (SRC-TG-810). Water salinity itself is a water-lane topic. This finding covers its reported effect on soils and crops.
- **Drainage.** Oasis drainage networks exist but are poorly maintained: annual public budgets are insufficient, GDAs show little interest and community ties have weakened (SRC-TG-800). Oases near chotts drain badly because the land is flat, and illicit extensions add excess water (SRC-TG-800).
- **Farm economics.** The study compares yield loss against FAO guidance that losses should not exceed 50% for saline-water use to remain profitable, and reports that farmers continued irrigating because family-farming costs were low (SRC-TG-810). This is a small-site observation, not proof of a nationwide farmer response.

## Evidence

- **Natural vs irrigation-induced salinity.** The PAN-LCD cites a 1994 estimate of about 1.5 Mha naturally salt-affected and a 2012 estimate of about 10,000 ha salinised through irrigation (SRC-TG-800). It says irrigation-induced salinity is the bigger concern because of the economic value of affected soils. These are secondary citations, not independently opened source studies.
- **Risk share.** The 2011 thesis abstract says about 50% of Tunisia's irrigated area is considered at high risk of salinisation; its underlying estimate year and method are not given (SRC-TG-811). This is a risk classification, not measured affected area.
- **Plot results.** Kairouan and Mahdia hold about 20% of Tunisia's irrigated land. In six plots at Bou Hajla (Kairouan) and Mahdia, irrigation with water of about 7 dS/m (Bou Hajla) and 5.7-7.8 dS/m (Mahdia) raised soil salinity in the short term, and rain lowered it again. Summer-crop yield losses often exceeded 50% and reached 75%; winter crops lost less than 50% thanks to rain (SRC-TG-810).
- **Geothermal greenhouses (Tozeur).** Several geothermal-water perimeters are salinised, especially Hamma 1 bis. Soil salinity becomes excessively high after the fourth year of irrigating the same greenhouse, and farmers shift to ever more salt-tolerant crops or move irrigation to new neighbouring land (SRC-TG-813).
- **Oases.** Oases cover 41,710 ha, 9% of irrigated perimeters (GIZ 2012), and suffer salinisation that varies by zone (SRC-TG-800).
- **Trend.** The PAN-LCD lists weak leaching, declining water quality, intense evaporation and climate change as risks to worsening salinity (SRC-TG-800). This is qualitative, not a numeric projection; `trend` remains unknown.

## Why confidence is what it is

Low. National area figures are secondary: studies from 1994 and 2012 are cited in a 2020 programme, while the thesis abstract's roughly 50% high-risk estimate has no stated method or estimate year and measures risk rather than observed salinity. The yield evidence is plot-scale, from six plots in a conference paper (SRC-TG-810). The Tozeur greenhouse study is peer-reviewed but local (SRC-TG-813). No official monitoring series of soil salinity in public irrigated perimeters was found. Salinity and yield effects are supported in specific sites; the national affected area and production impact remain uncertain.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water.** The cause is the salinity of irrigation water and the overexploitation of aquifers (SRC-TG-810). That belongs to the water lane (themes water-quality, groundwater, irrigation). Expanding irrigation with brackish water moves the problem from the water into the soil. Drainage of oases (SRC-TG-800) also connects to the dates-oases theme in this lane.
