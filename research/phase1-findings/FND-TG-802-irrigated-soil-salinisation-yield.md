## Problem

Where Tunisian farmers irrigate with saline groundwater, salt builds up in the soil and yields fall. In central Tunisia, summer crops irrigated with water above 4-5 g/l lose often more than 50%, and sometimes 75%, of their maximum yield. Farmers keep irrigating because family labour keeps costs low (SRC-TG-810). In oases, irrigation with 3.5 g/l water deposits at least 60 t/ha of salt, which is hard to remove when drainage fails (GIZ 2014, cited in SRC-TG-800).

The measured size of the problem is small and uncertain. The figure the government cites is about 10,000 ha of soil salinised through irrigation (Daghari et al. 2012, in SRC-TG-800). A Lund thesis says about 50% of irrigated land is "at high risk" (SRC-TG-811). The evidence supports a localised, serious yield problem in some perimeters and oases. It does not support a measured, nationwide loss of irrigated land.

## Root causes

- **The water is the cause; the soil shows the symptom.** Irrigation water in arid Tunisia carries more and more salt, aquifers are overexploited, and several perimeters use water above 6 dS/m (SRC-TG-810). Water salinity itself is a water-lane topic. This finding covers its effect on soils and crops.
- **Drainage.** Oasis drainage networks exist but are poorly maintained: annual public budgets are insufficient, GDAs show little interest and community ties have weakened (SRC-TG-800). Oases near chotts drain badly because the land is flat, and illicit extensions add excess water (SRC-TG-800).
- **Farm economics.** Saline-water use is unprofitable once yield loss exceeds 50% (FAO guidance), yet farmers continue because their crop costs are low (SRC-TG-810). The loss is therefore absorbed rather than avoided.

## Evidence

- **Natural vs irrigation-induced salinity.** Naturally salt-affected soils cover about 1.5 Mha, about 10% of the country (Hachicha and Job 1994). The PAN-LCD says the irrigation-induced kind is the bigger worry because of the economic value of those soils, and puts it at about 10,000 ha (Daghari et al. 2012). Both are cited in SRC-TG-800.
- **Risk share.** About 50% of irrigated areas are considered at high risk of salinisation (SRC-TG-811). No method or year is given in the abstract.
- **Plot results.** Kairouan and Mahdia hold about 20% of Tunisia's irrigated land. In six plots at Bou Hajla (Kairouan) and Mahdia, irrigation with water of about 7 dS/m (Bou Hajla) and 5.7-7.8 dS/m (Mahdia) raised soil salinity in the short term, and rain lowered it again. Summer-crop yield losses often exceeded 50% and reached 75%; winter crops lost less than 50% thanks to rain (SRC-TG-810).
- **Geothermal greenhouses (Tozeur).** Several geothermal-water perimeters are salinised, especially Hamma 1 bis. Soil salinity becomes excessively high after the fourth year of irrigating the same greenhouse, and farmers shift to ever more salt-tolerant crops or move irrigation to new neighbouring land (SRC-TG-813).
- **Oases.** Oases cover 41,710 ha, 9% of irrigated perimeters (GIZ 2012), and suffer salinisation that varies by zone (SRC-TG-800).
- **Trend.** The PAN-LCD expects the situation to worsen through weak leaching, declining water quality, intense evaporation and climate change (SRC-TG-800). This is qualitative, not a projection with numbers, so `trend` is set to unknown.

## Why confidence is what it is

Low. The national area figures are secondary (1994 and 2012 studies quoted in a 2020 programme) and disagree by an order of magnitude with the "at high risk" share. The yield evidence is plot-scale, from a conference paper on six plots (SRC-TG-810). The Tozeur greenhouse study is peer-reviewed but local (SRC-TG-813). No official monitoring series of soil salinity in public irrigated perimeters was found. The problem is real where it occurs, but its national scale is unknown.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water.** The cause is the salinity of irrigation water and the overexploitation of aquifers (SRC-TG-810). That belongs to the water lane (themes water-quality, groundwater, irrigation). Expanding irrigation with brackish water moves the problem from the water into the soil. Drainage of oases (SRC-TG-800) also connects to the dates-oases theme in this lane.
