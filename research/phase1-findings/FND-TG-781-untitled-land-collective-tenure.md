## Problem

A large part of Tunisian farmland does not carry a title that a bank can take as security, though the national figures are old and second-hand. Law 64-28 was substantially amended in 2016: collective land may now be leased, alienated, contributed to company capital and mortgaged to banks with management and regional supervisory-council approval, and private attribution to members is provided for (SRC-TG-795). Citing the World Bank (2006), the FAO/CIHEAM study states that only half of the land potential is registered, nearly a third of farms have no title or possession certificate, and about 60% of titles are "frozen", meaning the legal record no longer matches who actually holds the land after inheritance, sales or gifts (SRC-TG-781). Collective land, of which FAO/CIHEAM reported half of about 3 million ha in the centre and south settled into private plots, was governed by restrictive 1964 rules; Law 2016-69 replaced those rules with member attribution and conditional alienation and mortgage (SRC-TG-781, SRC-TG-783, SRC-TG-795). Both FAO/CIHEAM and FTDES say most small farmers lack titles and are therefore shut out of bank credit (SRC-TG-781, SRC-TG-782). In 2003-04, 87.5% of farm heads did not apply for any credit (SRC-TG-780). In July 2026 a draft decree-law was reported to aim at converting southern collective land into individual, transferable and bankable titles through a Land Court with special powers (SRC-TG-792). This suggests the problem is still open.

## Root causes

The symptom is land whose private attribution and registration can remain incomplete. The sources identify a legacy restrictive regime and a newer attribution process whose statutory deadline has elapsed; whether current delays are caused by that process is not directly measured:

- **Legacy legal restriction (1964-2016).** The original law 64-28 restricted alienation and mortgage to ministerially approved cases and official agricultural lenders (SRC-TG-783). Law 2016-69 replaced article 5: alienation, lease, mogharsa and company contributions now require management-council agreement and regional supervisory-council approval; mortgage to banks and financial institutions is permitted for specified purposes under those approvals (SRC-TG-795). This legacy regime may have constrained tenure and finance before amendment; its present effects are not quantified.
- **Slow, fragmented registration.** Registration and settlement involve at least three ministries: State Domains (register), Equipment (survey and mapping) and Justice (the Land Court) (SRC-TG-781).
- **Titles not updated after inheritance.** Inheritance is the main way owner-operated parcels are acquired (SRC-TG-780). When heirs do not record the division, titles "freeze" (SRC-TG-781). This connects the title problem to the holding fragmentation in FND-TG-780.
- **Bank collateral requirements.** Lenders require real collateral and profitability; FTDES names the land title as the collateral most small farmers cannot provide (SRC-TG-781, SRC-TG-782).

## Evidence

- Registration: 50% of land registered; nearly one-third of farms without title or possession certificate; about 60% of titles frozen; settlement handled by three ministries (SRC-TG-781, citing World Bank 2006 and Gharbi 1998).
- Collective land: half of about 3 million ha in the centre and south settled into private property; habous land fell from 1 million ha after independence to about 80,000 ha (SRC-TG-781).
- Legal regime: original articles 1 and 5 of law 64-28 and replacement article 5, attribution rules and five-year deadline in Law 2016-69 (SRC-TG-783, SRC-TG-795).
- Credit, 2003-04: 87.5% of farm heads did not apply; 6.2% applied and were refused. Non-applicants cited access difficulty (28%) and debt risk (27%); 2% said they were already indebted and 1.8% cited high interest (SRC-TG-780). Help from relatives was a more frequent source of investment finance than bank credit (SRC-TG-780).
- Qualitative: "a large majority" of farmers have no title or only uncertain titles (SRC-TG-781); "most" farmers lack a title (SRC-TG-782).
- Current policy signal: draft decree-law (July 2026) to convert southern collective land reserves into individual, transferable and bankable titles (SRC-TG-792).
- Investment law: law 2016-71 frees investors to acquire, lease or operate non-agricultural real estate only, so agricultural land stays outside this liberalisation (SRC-TG-794).

## Why confidence is what it is

Confidence is low:

- The quantitative title figures are second-hand. FAO/CIHEAM cites them from a 2006 World Bank study we could not find and from a 1998 book (SRC-TG-781). They describe the situation roughly 20-28 years ago.
- The claim that missing titles cause credit exclusion is an assertion in two tier B reports (SRC-TG-781, SRC-TG-782). It is not tested against data. The only national credit data (SRC-TG-780) show very low demand for credit and cite access difficulty and debt risk. They do not break results down by title status, so they are equally consistent with low income and climate risk being the main barrier, as FAO/CIHEAM itself also says (SRC-TG-781).
- The original 1964 legal text (SRC-TG-783) was checked against OCR; Law 2016-69 was reopened as a clean JORT/FAOLEX PDF (SRC-TG-795). Later amendments after 2016 have not been checked. The statute required attribution within five years (five years from publication in the JORT of the law of 10 August 2016), but current completion rates are not established here.
- The 2026 reform signal rests on tier C press (SRC-TG-792).

The older registration figures and the extent to which title status, rather than poverty and risk, drives credit exclusion remain unestablished. Later amendments to Law 64-28 after 2016 were not checked.
