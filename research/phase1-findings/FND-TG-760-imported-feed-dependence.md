## Problem

Tunisia grows essentially no maize and no soybeans. In MY 2024/25 it imported 1,033 thousand t of maize, all of it for feed (SRC-TG-763), 507 thousand t of soybeans (447 thousand t crushed; USDA's table lists a further 75 thousand t under feed and waste use, and the report says millers are directing more soybeans to full-fat soybean and expeller meal for direct feed use) and 158 thousand t of soybean meal (SRC-TG-764). Barley is the main home-grown feed grain, but it fails in dry years. The 2023 drought harvest gave 90 thousand t against domestic use of 965 thousand t, and 774 thousand t were imported (SRC-TG-763). Only 108 thousand of the 332 thousand ha seeded to barley were harvested (SRC-TG-704). As a result, feed costs follow world grain prices, the dinar and import logistics. ITES reports that feed import values rose 75% between 2019 and 2021. It attributes this mainly to larger volumes (soybean meal +85%, barley +64%), plus world price volatility and the dinar's devaluation; USDA volume data do not confirm the barley volume jump (see Evidence). Concentrate prices were 35% higher in 2022 than in 2019 (SRC-TG-706). In 2022 a fall in barley imports partly caused a temporary feed shortage, which reduced milk availability and produced "light shortages for a few weeks"; ITES says intermediaries’ opportunism and some traders’ speculation appear to have worsened the temporary crisis (SRC-TG-706).

## Root causes

- **Past decision (legacy):** in the early 1990s the dairy take-off rested on several measures. These included facilities to import dairy cows and a "guaranteed regular supply of concentrate feed, mainly through imports" (SRC-TG-760). Intensive dairy and poultry systems use imported inputs; the World Bank report also notes corn and soybean meal benefit poultry and dairy operations, especially larger farms (SRC-TG-921). Feed input subsidies were then cut under structural adjustment. They peaked at 49 million TND in the 1989 drought and fell to 4 million TND in 1992-93 (SRC-TG-921). The cuts removed the price buffer but left the import dependence in place (see FND-TG-920).
- **Climate (driver of the barley gap):** barley and roughage are rainfed. In drought years barley output collapses (SRC-TG-763) and roughage production falls, so farmers rely more on compound feed (ministry, SRC-TG-766).
- **Economics:** feed is bought in foreign currency. ITES names world price volatility and dinar devaluation alongside import volumes (SRC-TG-706).
- **Demand growth:** USDA expects soybean demand to keep rising with animal-protein demand (SRC-TG-764). ITES cites a scenario assumption of doubled feed demand by 2035, with no base year specified (SRC-TG-706).

The symptom is that "feed is expensive or short". The cause is a production model built on grains Tunisia does not grow, plus rainfed barley that fails when it is most needed.

## Evidence

- Maize: production 0, imports 1,033 thousand t and feed use 1,050 thousand t in MY 2024/25. USDA estimates 1,327 thousand t of imports for MY 2025/26. Imports were 730 thousand t in MY 2000/01 and 1,117 thousand t in MY 2015/16 (SRC-TG-763).
- Soybean: "Tunisia does not produce soybeans". Imports were 507 thousand t in MY 2024/25, 60% from Brazil and 36% from the US. Soybean meal imports were 158 thousand t, and 70% of soybean meal goes to poultry and eggs (SRC-TG-764).
- Barley: production 90 thousand t, use 965 thousand t and imports 774 thousand t in MY 2023/24. Production was 660 thousand t and imports 600 thousand t in MY 2025/26 (SRC-TG-763). USDA describes barley as consumed "mainly in feedlots and as supplemental feed, especially when and where rangelands are stressed" (SRC-TG-704).
- **Sources disagree on barley MY 2024/25.** The April 2026 GAIN report gives imports of 620 and feed use of 800 thousand t (SRC-TG-704). The PSD file updated in May 2026 gives 519 and 700 thousand t (SRC-TG-763). Both are USDA; the later figure is a revision.
- Prices and value: ITES writes that the value of feed imports in 2021 rose significantly over 2019, "soit 75% contre 26%". The "contre 26%" is not explained; it may be a comparison with another product group or period, so the 75% is read as the 2019-2021 change with that caveat. ITES attributes the rise mainly to volumes (soybean meal +85%, barley +64%). It also cites world price volatility and the dinar's devaluation. Import price rises in 2021 were 48% for maize, 30% for barley and 18% for soya. Concentrate prices rose 35% between 2019 and 2022 (SRC-TG-706).
- **The volume claim is not confirmed by USDA.** USDA barley imports were 778 thousand t in MY 2019/20, 988 in MY 2020/21 and 888 in MY 2021/22. Maize imports were 1,006, 1,213 and 889 thousand t (SRC-TG-763). Marketing years (July-June) differ from ITES's calendar years and the bases are not comparable. Even so, USDA does not show a 64% jump in barley volumes. Both are recorded. No source isolates the price and currency effects.
- Barley import dependence averages about 50% (51% in the 2035 trend scenario, which simply holds the ten-year average constant) (SRC-TG-706).
- Policy response: the ministry says feed prices were capped together with the Ministry of Commerce (SRC-TG-766; tier C press report of the ministry's answer).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. Import volumes come from USDA's dataset and post reports. These are solid and consistent in direction, but they are USDA estimates, not INS statistics, and they get revised (barley MY 2024/25). The price and value changes and the 2022 shortage come from one Tunisian state think-tank study (ITES, tier B) that compiles DGPA data. The 2035 doubling of feed demand is second-hand (SOLIDAR and WFP, 2020, not opened) and is not a modelled projection. A species split for maize is missing, so the dependence cannot be pinned on dairy alone. Soybean meal at least is mainly a poultry input.

## Links to other sectors

- **Water:** the barley gap opens in drought years (SRC-TG-763, SRC-TG-704). Water scarcity cuts roughage production and pushes farmers toward compound feed (SRC-TG-766). Imported maize and soybeans also embed foreign water; see FND-TG-860 for the virtual water framing. The water lane may want to quantify the virtual water in feed imports.
- **Energy:** none found.

## Leads for phase 2

- Feed price caps agreed between the Ministry of Agriculture and the Ministry of Commerce (SRC-TG-766)
- National plan to promote fodder production, in preparation (SRC-TG-766)
- Liberalised private barley imports outside the Office des Céréales (SRC-TG-704)
- New soybean pressing unit for full-fat soybean and expeller meal by a feed miller (SRC-TG-764)
- Diversification into rapeseed and sunflower encouraged by the ministry without a formal programme (SRC-TG-764)
