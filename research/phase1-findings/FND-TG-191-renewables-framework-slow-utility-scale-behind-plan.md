## Problem

The 2015 Tunisian Solar Plan set a 14% renewable share of electricity generation for 2020 and 30% for 2030 (SRC-TG-192). ONEM reported 2.6% in 2020 excluding self-producers (SRC-TG-194). Later ONEM series include self-produced PV: 5.2% in 2024, 5.3% in 2025, and 8.3% for January-September 2026 (SRC-TG-193, SRC-TG-100). These are not a fully comparable uninterrupted series: ONEM began counting renewable self-production in January 2024, and concession solar plants in the IPP series from December 2025 (SRC-TG-100).

Utility-scale deployment has progressed slowly. The 500 MW solar concession tender closed in July 2019; the ministry announced results on 20 December 2019, while project agreements were approved in December 2021 (SRC-TG-195). ONEM reports that the Kairouan 100 MW plant entered service in December 2025, Sidi Bouzid's 50 MW in January 2026 and Tozeur's 50 MW in the first quarter of 2026; Gafsa's 100 MW is planned to enter service in 2027 (SRC-TG-193). These dates establish the sequence, not an award-to-operation duration for each project. Of 54 authorisation projects granted agreements in principle for 261 MW during 2017-2020, 15 projects (four 10 MW and eleven 1 MW; 51 MW total) were in service by 2025 (SRC-TG-193).

Rooftop and other self-production expanded faster. ONEM reports about 420 MW of low-voltage self-production installed by end-2025, and 543 MW of residential rooftop PV by end-September 2026 (SRC-TG-193, SRC-TG-100). The 2015 Plan scheduled 392 MW of net-metering PV for households and business buildings by 2025; its household Prosol Elec component was 240 MW for 2025 and 390 MW for 2030 (SRC-TG-192). The plan values cover different categories and years from the later ONEM totals, so these comparisons show scale and direction rather than a like-for-like programme evaluation.

## Root causes

The evidence identifies several constraints, but does not quantify their contribution to individual project delays:

- **Offtaker exposure and project finance (policy/economic).** Under the 2015 law, authorised domestic-market producers sell all output exclusively to the public utility (art. 22); self-producers originally could sell surpluses only to it (art. 9). The 2019 amendment replaced article 9, allowing eligible self-producers to sell to consumers above a specified subscribed-capacity threshold while retaining the surplus-sale route to the public utility (SRC-TG-191, SRC-TG-200, SRC-TG-201, SRC-TG-202, SRC-TG-203). The amendment therefore qualifies, rather than eliminates, STEG's central role in utility-scale PPAs. The 2023 policy paper says banks' main security is the STEG electricity-sale contract, reports investor requests for comfort letters and says they were issued in August 2022 (SRC-TG-195). The World Bank states that STEG's difficult finances as main buyer hindered private renewable investment (SRC-TG-105); this is a reported barrier, not a quantified causal estimate. STEG's finances are treated separately in FND-TG-161.
- **Administrative process (institutional).** Articles 12 and 13 of the 2015 law provide annual-needs notice, technical-commission review and parliamentary steps for domestic authorisations and concessions. Article 25's parliamentary approval by law concerns export concessions, not the domestic projects discussed here (SRC-TG-191). Law 2019-47 amended article 9, not articles 12-13 (SRC-TG-200, SRC-TG-201, SRC-TG-202, SRC-TG-203). The 2016 implementing decree sets application, commission, grid-study and project-development stages (SRC-TG-200, SRC-TG-201, SRC-TG-202, SRC-TG-203). A 2023 policy paper reports that procedures take at least three years before production starts; it does not identify a sample or project-level durations, so this is a reported assessment rather than a measured national average (SRC-TG-195). The same paper says the ministry handled authorisations through calls within annual needs quotas (SRC-TG-195).
- **Grid connection (technical/economic).** The IRENA-ANME report says STEG grid works face long public-procurement procedures, and the 2023 policy paper identifies the ageing transmission grid and lengthy connection processes as barriers (SRC-TG-106, SRC-TG-195). Article 8 of the 2015 law generally places connection and needed reinforcement costs on the producer. Article 21 of Decree-law 2022-68 added an exception allowing the public body to bear them when the State proposed the site (SRC-TG-191, SRC-TG-200, SRC-TG-201, SRC-TG-202, SRC-TG-203). The sources do not quantify connection waits or curtailment.
- **Land and local acceptance (institutional).** The policy paper says land access was not prepared in the 2017-2022 programme, reports difficulty using agricultural land classified as conservation zones, and links some land problems to limited local-community involvement and high local employment expectations (SRC-TG-106, SRC-TG-195). The 2019 and 2022 amendments provide for renewable projects on private land and authorised state or local land; the 2022 text states that such projects do not require a change in agricultural land allocation (SRC-TG-200, SRC-TG-201, SRC-TG-202, SRC-TG-203). These provisions do not establish how often land access blocked a project or the scale of local opposition.
- **Finance, bidding and governance (economic/institutional).** The policy paper reports very low bids by some inexperienced or speculative entrants followed by financing difficulties. The World Bank also cites early market learning, post-COVID cost increases, bureaucratic inefficiencies and financial risks (SRC-TG-195, SRC-TG-105). The policy paper records investors' request for an independent electricity regulator because of overlapping STEG roles, but the evidence does not show that regulator absence caused particular projects to fail (SRC-TG-195).

## Evidence

- **Electricity shares and targets.** ONEM's key figures report renewable shares (hydro, wind and solar) of 2.8% in 2019 and 2.6% in 2020 excluding self-producers (SRC-TG-194). IRENA reports 6% for 2023 (SRC-TG-190). ONEM reports 5.2% in 2024 and 5.3% in 2025 including self-produced PV (SRC-TG-193), and 8.3% for January-September 2026 including self-production (SRC-TG-100). ONEM says self-production enters the series from January 2024 and concession plants from December 2025 (SRC-TG-100). A September 2026 press article attributes 9.2% at end-June 2026 to ONEM, but this remains tier C sourced reporting (SRC-TG-199). The 2015 Plan itself gives a 14% interim target for 2020; the press article reports 12% as the target adopted in 2016 and 3.1% as the 2020 outcome, unlike ONEM's 2.6% excluding self-production (SRC-TG-192, SRC-TG-199, SRC-TG-194). Keep those source and accounting differences explicit. The 2026 bulletin says renewable output rose 66% in January-September 2026 year over year (SRC-TG-100).
- **Installed capacity and scope.** IRENA reports 1,086 MW at end-2024, representing 15% of total capacity in its table (SRC-TG-190). The World Bank reports 516 MW at end-2023, 45% STEG-owned and 55% private (SRC-TG-105). ONEM reports 339 MW in 2024 and 463 MW in 2025 in the net generating fleet excluding self-production (SRC-TG-193). These are estimates with different dates and scopes. The proposed explanation that the difference is mostly rooftop/self-production is plausible but not demonstrated by the cited source records; the capacity totals should remain unreconciled.
- **2018 concession tender.** The 500 MW PV tender on State-proposed sites closed in July 2019. IRENA reports five bids with prices of 2.44-2.86 US cents/kWh; the 2023 policy paper reports eight financial offers, with a best offer of about 2.4 US cents/kWh (SRC-TG-106, SRC-TG-195). These descriptions may refer to different stages and do not establish a direct contradiction. The policy paper says results were announced on 20 December 2019 and the five project agreements approved by decree-laws in December 2021; do not describe that as a verified award-to-approval interval (SRC-TG-195). ONEM's project table reports Kairouan 100 MW in service December 2025; Sidi Bouzid 50 MW January 2026; Tozeur 50 MW in service in Q1 2026; Gafsa 100 MW planned for 2027 and its agreement signed 8 May 2024; it lists a 200 MW Tataouine lot without a project status (SRC-TG-193). Separately, the World Bank's April 2025 appraisal document says 200 MW was under construction and 300 MW nearing financial close (SRC-TG-105).
- **Later tenders and authorisations.** ONEM reports that the 2022 1,700 MW tender included 800 MW PV on private sites, 600 MW wind on private sites and 300 MW at two State sites; project agreements for 300 MW and 198 MW were signed on 24 March 2025, with service expected in 2027 for the 300 MW projects (SRC-TG-193). The World Bank separately says a 500 MW solar round was awarded in December 2024 at 3.1-3.3 US cents/kWh (SRC-TG-105). ONEM records 54 agreements in principle for 261 MW across four authorisation calls in 2017-2020, of which 15 projects totaling 51 MW were in service by 2025; its fifth call (October 2024-June 2025) produced 186 agreements in principle for 288 MW (SRC-TG-193). ONEM reports 124 MW of solar under authorisation and concession entered production in 2025 (SRC-TG-193). Agreements and planned service dates are not treated as operating capacity.
- **Self-production.** The 2023 policy paper describes Prosol Elec support as a 30% grant plus seven-year bank loans repaid through the STEG bill; it reports more than 600 installer firms and capacity above 200 MW at end-2022 (SRC-TG-195). The World Bank's April 2025 appraisal document describes 250 MW installed “to date” under the residential and commercial rooftop programme; the measurement date is not separately specified (SRC-TG-105). ONEM reports about 420 MW low-voltage self-production at end-2025 and 70 MW of medium- and high-voltage projects in service by end-2025 (SRC-TG-193); by end-September 2026, it reports 543 MW residential rooftop PV and 150 MW at medium/high voltage (SRC-TG-100). These labels have different sector and voltage scopes. IRENA's report says about 11.3 MWp was connected on the low-voltage grid at end-2019, while the 2015 Plan scheduled 30 MW of net-metering PV by 2015; these are an actual and a plan value, not a direct statistical contradiction, and scope equivalence is not demonstrated (SRC-TG-106, SRC-TG-192). ONEM's 2025 key figures report 721 GWh self-production and 37 GWh solar IPP output in 2025; the September 2026 bulletin's full-year 2025 column gives 81 GWh for solar IPPs. Preserve the unresolved ONEM discrepancy without assuming a reconciliation (SRC-TG-193, SRC-TG-100).

## Why confidence is what it is

Confidence is medium. Outcome evidence is mainly tier A ONEM and IRENA reporting, but some data are partial-year, differently scoped or methodologically discontinuous; the 37 versus 81 GWh ONEM figures remain unreconciled. The causal mechanisms are described by tier B policy and World Bank reports and are rarely quantified at project level. The FNF policy paper was co-written with ministry officials, so its account is near-official but still a report rather than project-level administrative data (SRC-TG-195). The tier C June 2026 share is clearly attributed as press reporting. The trend is improving because ONEM reports a 66% year-over-year increase in renewable output in Jan-Sep 2026 and concession plants began entering service, while reported shares remain below the 2015 Plan's 30% 2030 goal; the accounting break and distinct denominators limit direct comparison (SRC-TG-100, SRC-TG-193, SRC-TG-192).

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture.** Renewable projects interact with agricultural land rules, and sources report land-access barriers. The 2022 amendment permits projects on private or authorised public land and states they do not require a change in agricultural land allocation; no source quantifies agricultural land used or sought for renewable projects (SRC-TG-200, SRC-TG-201, SRC-TG-202, SRC-TG-203, SRC-TG-195).
- **Energy finances.** The World Bank identifies STEG's financial condition as a barrier to private renewable investment; see FND-TG-161 for the utility's finances (SRC-TG-105).

## Verification status

Article 7 of Law 2019-47 replaced article 9 on self-production. Article 14 of the original 2016 decree sets domestic authorisation ceilings of 10 MW for photovoltaic and 30 MW for wind projects. Neither provision measures the contribution of permitting to delays. Dated outcomes, accounts of individual project delays and general legal requirements are reported separately (SRC-TG-200, SRC-TG-201, SRC-TG-191).

**Outstanding verification:** The original laws and implementing decree are identified. The relative contribution of financing, procedural and project-specific delays remains unverified.
