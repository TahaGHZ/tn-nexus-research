## Problem

Law 2015-12 opened power generation from renewables to private investors through three regimes:
self-consumption, authorisations and concessions for the domestic market, and concessions for
export (SRC-TG-191). The 2015 Solar Plan expected renewables to reach 14% of generation in 2020
and 30% in 2030 (SRC-TG-192). Renewables were about 2.6% of generation in 2020, excluding
self-producers (SRC-TG-194), 5.3% in 2025 including self-produced PV (SRC-TG-193), and 8.3% in
January-September 2026 (SRC-TG-100). Large plants came very late: the 500 MW solar concession
tender was pre-qualified in May 2018 (SRC-TG-106) and awarded in December 2019 (SRC-TG-195), and
its first plant, Kairouan 100 MW, entered service in December 2025 (SRC-TG-193). Of 54 projects
(261 MW) given agreements in principle under the authorisation regime in 2017-2020, 15 projects,
about 51 MW, were in service by 2025 (SRC-TG-193). The exception is rooftop solar: about 420 MW of
low-voltage self-production at the end of 2025 (SRC-TG-193), against 392 MW of net metering
planned for 2025, and 543 MW residential at end September 2026 (SRC-TG-100), against 240 MW
planned for household Prosol Elec in 2025 and 390 MW in 2030 (SRC-TG-192).

## Root causes

The low share and the delays are symptoms. The causes documented are:

- **Single buyer (policy, from the 2015 law).** As enacted in 2015, authorised producers must sell
  "exclusivement et en totalité" to the public utility at prices set by ministerial order
  (SRC-TG-191, art. 22), and self-producers could sell surplus only to it (art. 9); Law 2019-47
  later allowed self-producers to sell to other large consumers (corporate PPAs) (SRC-TG-106). Banks' main security is therefore the
  contract with STEG as "المشتري الوحيد والحصري" (sole and exclusive buyer), and many investors asked
  for a comfort letter, issued in August 2022 (SRC-TG-195). There was no guarantee against
  off-taker risk (SRC-TG-106). The World Bank states that STEG's finances as main buyer "has
  hindered scaling up private investment in RE" (SRC-TG-105), and that energy SOE deficits make
  private renewable investment harder to attract (SRC-TG-196). STEG's deficit itself is covered in
  FND-TG-161.
- **Layered approvals (institutional, from the 2015 law).** In the 2015 text, authorisations
  follow an annual notice of national needs and the technical commission's opinion, and contracts
  go to a special parliamentary committee; concession agreements must be approved by parliament
  (SRC-TG-191, art. 12, 13, 25). Whether the 2019 amendment changed these steps was not checked. After a long debate on guaranteed tariffs, authorisations were granted only
  through ministry calls within an annual quota (SRC-TG-106). The 2019 concession agreements were
  approved by five decree-laws ("مراسيم") in December 2021, two years after the award
  (SRC-TG-195). Developers
  struggle with procedures spread over many ministries (SRC-TG-106), and procedures take "مالا
  يقل عند ثلاث سنوات" (at least three years; wording as printed) before production (SRC-TG-195).
- **Grid (technical).** STEG's grid works go through long public procurement, so plants can be
  finished before they can be connected (SRC-TG-106). The ageing transmission grid and long
  connection procedures are named among the main obstacles (SRC-TG-195). Producers pay their own
  connection and any reinforcement (SRC-TG-191, art. 8).
- **Land and acceptance (institutional).** The 2017-2022 programme did not plan land access
  (SRC-TG-106). Projects struggle to get permission to use agricultural land classified as
  conservation zones; Decree-law 2022-68 opened such land case by case (SRC-TG-195). The same
  paper says land problems partly come from local communities not being involved, and that high
  local job expectations make acceptance a major challenge (SRC-TG-195).
- **Financing failures and regulation (economic).** Some newcomers bid very low prices for
  speculation or from inexperience and then could not raise finance (SRC-TG-195). The World Bank
  adds a learning phase, post-COVID cost increases and "bureaucratic inefficiencies and financial
  risks" (SRC-TG-105). Investors' most urgent demand is an independent regulator, as STEG's
  overlapping roles are not in line with good governance (SRC-TG-195).

## Evidence

- **Share of generation.** 2019: 2.8% and 2020: 2.6% (hydro + wind + solar, excluding
  self-producers) (SRC-TG-194). 2023: 6% (IRENA) (SRC-TG-190). 2024: 5.2% and 2025: 5.3%,
  including self-produced PV (SRC-TG-193). January-September 2026: 8.3% (SRC-TG-100); end June
  2026: 9.2% (press quoting ONEM, tier C) (SRC-TG-199). ONEM counts self-production only from
  January 2024 and concession plants from December 2025 (SRC-TG-100), so shares before and after
  2024 are not strictly comparable. The 2020 interim target is 14% in the plan itself
  (SRC-TG-192) but 12% in a press article (SRC-TG-199); the 2020 outcome is 2.6% excluding
  self-producers (SRC-TG-194) or 3.1% per the same article (SRC-TG-199).
- **Capacity: sources disagree.** IRENA: 1,086 MW at end 2024, 15% of capacity (SRC-TG-190).
  World Bank: 516 MW at end 2023, 45% STEG and 55% private (SRC-TG-105). ONEM, excluding
  self-production: 339 MW (2024) and 463 MW (2025) in the net fleet, 8% of the STEG + IPP fleet in
  2025 (SRC-TG-193). The gap is mostly rooftop and self-production capacity, which IRENA appears
  to include and the other two exclude.
- **Concessions.** 2018 tender of 500 MW on state sites: Kairouan 100 MW in service December 2025,
  Sidi Bouzid 50 MW January 2026, Tozeur 50 MW first quarter 2026, Gafsa 100 MW expected 2027,
  Tataouine 200 MW no status given (SRC-TG-193). IRENA reports five bids at 2.44-2.86 US
  cents/kWh in 2019 (SRC-TG-106); the FNF paper reports eight financial offers, the best at about
  2.4 US cents (SRC-TG-195). The sources disagree on the number of bids. As of April 2025, 200 MW was under construction and 300 MW near financial close
  (SRC-TG-105). 2022 tenders: 300 MW signed in March 2025 for 2027, 200 MW more awarded, a 198 MW
  project signed, one 75 MW wind project awarded (SRC-TG-193); 500 MW of solar awarded in December
  2024 at 3.1-3.3 US cents/kWh (SRC-TG-105).
- **Authorisations.** Four calls in 2017-2020 (SRC-TG-195), 54 agreements for 261 MW, 15 projects
  in service (SRC-TG-193). A fifth call (October 2024 - June 2025) gave 186 agreements for 288 MW
  (SRC-TG-193). 124 MW of authorisation and concession solar entered production in 2025
  (SRC-TG-193).
- **Self-production.** Prosol Elec combines a 30% grant from the national energy management
  fund (as named in the source) with 7-year bank loans repaid on the STEG bill; more than 600 installer firms were created and capacity
  passed 200 MW at the end of 2022 (SRC-TG-195). The World Bank gives 250 MW of rooftop capacity in
  2025 (SRC-TG-105), ONEM about 420 MW low voltage at end 2025 (SRC-TG-193) and 543 MW residential
  at end September 2026 (SRC-TG-100). IRENA's 2021 report gives only about 11.3 MWp on the LV grid
  at end 2019 (SRC-TG-106), which conflicts with the plan's 30 MW of rooftop PV at end 2015
  (SRC-TG-192); the sources do not explain this. Medium and high voltage self-production is much
  smaller: about 30 MW connected out of 70 MW authorised at end 2022 (SRC-TG-195), 70 MW in service
  at end 2025 (SRC-TG-193). Self-producers supplied 721 GWh in 2025, against 37 GWh from solar IPPs
  (SRC-TG-193); the September 2026 bulletin gives 81 GWh for 2025 solar IPPs (SRC-TG-100), which
  may include concession output counted from December 2025. The two ONEM documents disagree.

## Why confidence is what it is

The outcome figures (shares, project status, rooftop capacity) come from official ONEM statistics
and agree in direction; they are solid, with the series break in 2024 noted. The causes rest on
two tier B reports, one written with ministry officials (SRC-TG-195) and one by IRENA with ANME
(SRC-TG-106), plus World Bank documents and the law itself. They are consistent with each other
but rarely quantified: no source measures how much each barrier delayed which project. The
capacity figures disagree by a factor of two depending on scope. Hence medium. Trend is
"improving" because generation from renewables rose 66% in January-September 2026 (SRC-TG-100)
and the first concession plants are now producing (SRC-TG-193); the gap to the targets remains
large (FND-TG-192).

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture.** Utility-scale solar competes for agricultural land; projects on land
  classified as conservation zones need case-by-case exemption under Decree-law 2022-68
  (SRC-TG-195). No data were found on how much farmland has been used or requested.
- **Energy finances.** The single-buyer design ties renewable growth to STEG's finances
  (SRC-TG-105); see FND-TG-161 on the STEG deficit.
