## Problem

The brief described a dependence on imported certified seed, cereals included, and an erosion of local varieties. The evidence supports only part of that.

For **cereals**, seed is overwhelmingly domestic. More than 80% of wheat seed comes from farm-saved or informal channels. Certified seed adoption is below 20% nationally and below 30% even in irrigated or high-potential areas, under the FAO benchmark of 30% (SRC-TG-827). Certified cereal seed production was 30,000 t in 2023 (SRC-TG-827). The cereal problem is low use of certified seed, not import dependence. The country does import about 1.6 Mt of wheat grain a year (SRC-TG-827), which is a separate problem covered in FND-TG-700.

For **vegetables**, seed for sowing is imported: 372.6 t worth US$16.3 million in 2023, up from US$12.4 million in 2013 (SRC-TG-824). The share of needs this covers, and whether any vegetable seed is produced or exported domestically, is not known.

**Local varieties**: the national gene bank says traditional varieties have been abandoned in favour of improved commercial seed and is reintroducing them (SRC-TG-830). The 2024 national treaty report lists "l'appauvrissement de la palette de variétés cultivées" (the narrowing range of cultivated varieties) among the threats (SRC-TG-828). The same report admits that genetic erosion is not measured to standard criteria and lacks baselines (SRC-TG-828).

## Root causes

- **Cereal seed system (current, institutional).** The wheat seed system is highly centralised around the Office des Céréales and the DGPA, with regional services, cooperatives and collectors on the margins. The paper argues this limits local adaptability and innovation adoption (SRC-TG-827); the low certified-seed adoption figure is consistent with that diagnosis but is not shown to result from centralisation alone.
- **Seed law (context, effect unproven).** Law 99-42 of 10 May 1999 (JORT no. 39, 14 May 1999, p. 706) replaced the 1976 law. It created an official catalogue for varieties that are distinct, stable, uniform and of significant cultivation value (art. 4), and allows only seed of catalogued varieties to be marketed (art. 9) (SRC-TG-829). Its effect on local varieties is not established in the sources opened.
- **Reported variety replacement (low confidence).** The gene bank reports that traditional varieties have been abandoned in favour of improved commercial seed, while noting that some local varieties yield less (SRC-TG-830). The treaty report's Deglet Nour example is about date palms and belongs to the dates-and-oases finding, not cereal or vegetable seed (SRC-TG-828; see FND-TG-740).
- **Vegetable seed imports.** Imports are recorded (SRC-TG-824), but the export query returned no record, which counts as missing data rather than zero. Why vegetable seed is imported (breeding capacity, F1 hybrids, plant breeders' rights) was not documented.

## Evidence

- Wheat seed adoption, farm-saved share and certified production: SRC-TG-827 (peer-reviewed; the figures are cited from DGPA 2024).
- Vegetable seed imports in 2013 and 2023: SRC-TG-824.
- Legal framework: SRC-TG-829 (law 99-42, JORT scan) and SRC-TG-828 (2024 treaty report listing 99-42 and its decrees 2000-101, 2000-102 and 2000-1282).
- Gene bank activity and its statements on variety loss: SRC-TG-830 (press, tier C).
- Qualitative threats and missing erosion baselines: SRC-TG-828.

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium for the cereal part: one recent peer-reviewed paper, relaying DGPA figures, gives consistent numbers (below 20% adoption, over 80% farm-saved). Vegetable seed import values come from UN Comtrade, a tier A dataset, but the dependence share is unknown. The loss of local varieties rests on qualitative official statements, one tier B report and one tier C press article, and is low confidence. The link from the seed law to erosion is not established.

## Links to other sectors

Water: the gene bank head says local varieties withstand climate shocks better than some imported varieties, which need more water, fertiliser and pesticides (SRC-TG-830). This is the official's claim, not measured data. Energy: none found.

## Leads for phase 2

- National cereal extension programme with INGC aimed at improving farm-saved wheat seed quality (SRC-TG-827).
- Subsidies for certified cereal seed prices and strategic cereal seed reserves under the Office des Céréales, recommended by FAO (SRC-TG-820): https://www.fao.org/3/cc6217fr/cc6217fr.pdf
- Contracts with cereal growers for certified seed multiplication (SRC-TG-820).
- BNG on-farm conservation programmes, started with 200 producers and now covering more than 300 ha, and the repatriation of about 6,000 samples (SRC-TG-830).
- National gene bank database (www.tn-grin.nat.tn) (SRC-TG-828).
- Digital platforms and participatory governance for the wheat seed system (SRC-TG-827).
