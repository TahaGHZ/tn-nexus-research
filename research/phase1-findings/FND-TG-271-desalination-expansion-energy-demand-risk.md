## Problem

The ministry's Eau 2050 synthesis projects seawater desalination at 192 million m³/year in 2030
and 265 million m³/year by 2035, remaining at that level through 2050 (SRC-TG-276). This implies
a larger electricity-consuming water-supply component, but the strategy provides no electricity
use forecast. Its stated policy direction is to base sustainable desalination on renewable energy.

## Root causes

The strategy links increased desalination to projected water scarcity and declining conventional
inflows. It does not quantify the associated power-system impact.

## Evidence

The water-balance scenario is in SRC-TG-276; the current SONEDE energy-intensity indicator is in
SRC-TG-270 and should not be extrapolated directly to desalination.

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium for the water-volume scenario; low for any implied electricity demand, which is not
calculated in the cited strategy.

## Links to other sectors

The finding connects energy supply to water-supply planning, while retaining energy as its main
sector.

## Leads for phase 2

- Integrating renewable generation with desalination planning (SRC-TG-276).

## Verification status

Draft retained. Outstanding exact issue: the Eau 2050 table gives projected water volumes but no electricity intensity, so the additional power demand of the desalination expansion cannot be verified or quantified.
