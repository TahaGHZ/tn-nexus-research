## Problem

Since 2018 Tunisia has applied quotas to dam water for its public irrigated perimeters (PPI) to protect drinking-water supply (SRC-TG-528, p. 57). In the drought of 2021-2024 the quotas were cut until several perimeters stopped. The national dam-water quota went from 235 Mm3 in 2020-21 to 168 Mm3 in 2021-22, when 142 Mm3 was consumed (SRC-TG-528, p. 76). Consumption from dams then fell to 88.4 Mm3 in 2022-23 and 46.7 Mm3 in 2023-24 (SRC-TG-530, p. 39). The ministry describes the quota cut as about two thirds between 2021 and 2023 (SRC-TG-529, p. 38). In 2023, releases from Sidi Salem stopped from 10 February to 31 May, and the lower Medjerda perimeters of Manouba and Ariana stopped irrigating. Distribution in dam-fed PPIs was limited to two months, and Sidi Salem's 70,600 ha received 18.7 Mm3 against needs above 100 Mm3 (SRC-TG-529, pp. 8, 38). In 2023-24 Sidi Salem perimeters received 12 Mm3 of a 22 Mm3 quota. Nebhana's perimeters, specialised in greenhouse early vegetables, received no water and stopped entirely (SRC-TG-530, pp. 7, 39). Vegetable area fell from 152,500 ha to 122,161 ha (-20%, derived) and production from 3,996 to 3,649 thousand t (-8.7%, derived) between 2021-22 and 2022-23. The ministry attributes this "essentiellement" to planting restrictions in PPIs (SRC-TG-529, p. 49).

## Root causes

- **Symptom vs cause.** Empty reservoirs are the trigger (FND-TG-425). The cause of the irrigation collapse is how the shortage is shared out. Drinking water has priority on the same dams and transfers. Since 2018 quotas have made irrigation the adjustment variable (SRC-TG-528, p. 57). The 2024 report sums it up: the measures "ont pu assurer l'approvisionnement en eau potable, mais l'agriculture irriguÃƒÂ©e a ÃƒÂ©tÃƒÂ© clairement affectÃƒÂ©e" (SRC-TG-530, p. 7). This allocation is the late form of a legacy choice. The Medjerda-Cap Bon canal, opened mainly for irrigation, now reserves two thirds of its water for drinking water, and Nebhana faces the same conflict (SRC-TG-402, p. 72; FND-TG-426).
- **Administrative crop rules.** The ministry notified CRDAs not to plan summer crops or water-hungry crops (cucurbits, vegetables). Remaining quotas went to fruit trees and "strategic" crops (fodder, sugar beet, field crops) (SRC-TG-530, p. 39). In 2022-23 quotas were "rÃƒÂ©servÃƒÂ©s principalement pour le maintien de l'arboriculture" (SRC-TG-529, p. 38).
- **Losses between quota and farm (technical).** CRDAs downstream receive much less than their quota because the old infrastructure leaks (SRC-TG-528, p. 76). In 2023-24 illicit pumping of water released into the Medjerda was one of the "causes essentielles" why only 12 of 22 Mm3 reached Sidi Salem perimeters (SRC-TG-530, p. 39). See FND-TG-527 for network efficiency.
- **Fallback onto groundwater (behavioural).** Farmers responded with illicit river pumping and boreholes (SRC-TG-528, p. 76). The 2023 report states that groundwater overexploitation partly results from quota cuts "qui a poussÃƒÂ© des agriculteurs ÃƒÂ  exploiter les eaux souterraines en compensation" (SRC-TG-529, p. 84). A similar episode occurred in 2016: deep-aquifer exploitation rose from 119% (2015) to 133% (2016), which the 2017 report attributes to surface-water rationing, rising crop water needs and illicit boreholes together (SRC-TG-402; FND-TG-400). In 2017 a ministerial circular of 27 February even authorised boreholes inside public perimeters of BÃƒÂ©ja, Bizerte, Ariana and Manouba (SRC-TG-402, pp. 69-70).

**Representativeness.** The quota policy applies to public perimeters supplied from surface water: 23% of irrigation water on dry-year average (SRC-TG-528, p. 76). The ministry notes that the quota system's reach is limited because 77% of irrigated perimeters run on groundwater (SRC-TG-528, p. 84). The lower Medjerda and Nebhana cases are therefore the hardest-hit end of the system, not the national average. They concentrate in the north-east and the Sahel and in high-value crops (vegetables, stone fruit, citrus). The irrigated sector supplies 95% of vegetable and 70% of tree-crop output (SRC-TG-528, p. 76), so the national effect is still visible in those crops.

## Why confidence is what it is

High for the facts of the cuts. Dates, volumes and per-dam figures come from three successive editions of the ministry's annual water report (tier A), and they agree in scale. Medium for production effects. Only the vegetable decline and the Manouba and Ben Arous peach losses are attributed mainly to the water cuts by the source. Other crop declines have several causes, and no 2023-24 crop data were found. The trend is `worsening` because the 2024 report calls the quota system "encore plus sÃƒÂ©vÃƒÂ¨re en 2024" (SRC-TG-530, p. 43). The episode follows a 2015-2018 precedent, and climate projections point to lower inflows (FND-TG-500).

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture:** output, income and prices of vegetables and fruit (above). Nebhana perimeter land is reported as abandoned and exposed to urban sprawl (SRC-TG-530).
- **Drinking water:** the cuts are the counterpart of protecting SONEDE supply on the same dams and transfers (FND-TG-451). In 2023, SECADENORD's billed volume to SONEDE and CRDAs fell 13% (456 to 403 Mm3) (SRC-TG-529, p. 37).
- **Groundwater:** compensation pumping and illicit pumping (FND-TG-400).
- **Governance and finance:** less water sold means less GDA revenue and more debt along the chain to CRDAs, SECADENORD and STEG (SRC-TG-528, p. 79; FND-TG-477).
- **Energy:** the state paid 41 MTND in 2023 (20 MTND in 2022) for the energy of reinforced north-south transfers that kept drinking water flowing (SRC-TG-529, p. 8).
