## Problem

Tunisia's 2030 renewable target is large compared with what is built. The 2015 Solar Plan set
3,815 MW, about 30% of generation and about 35% of installed capacity for 2030 (SRC-TG-192). The World Bank cites the updated
strategy as 4,800 MW and 35% "in the energy mix" by 2030, and 8,350 MW by 2035 (SRC-TG-105,
SRC-TG-103). Against this, IRENA counts 1,086 MW at the end of 2024 (SRC-TG-190), and renewables
supplied 8.3% of generation in January-September 2026 (SRC-TG-100). Meeting 3,815 MW means about
3.5 times the end-2024 capacity; meeting 4,800 MW means about 4.4 times, from the end-2024 baseline to the 2030 target year (six years) (our
arithmetic from SRC-TG-192, SRC-TG-105 and SRC-TG-190; IRENA's 1,086 MW includes 66 MW of hydro,
while the Solar Plan's 3,815 MW is a distinct plan target with its own stated scope). No opened
source forecasts whether the target will be met. The Mediterranean TSOs' adequacy study instead
assumes it is: renewables at 46.9% of 10,520 MW of capacity in 2030, with wind at 15% of the fleet
(SRC-TG-226), against 245 MW of wind today (FND-TG-190).

## Root causes

- **Delivery pace (institutional).** Under the 2015 framework the first concession tender took
  from pre-qualification in May 2018 (SRC-TG-106) to first output in December 2025 (SRC-TG-193),
  and procedures take at least three years before production (SRC-TG-195). Projects awarded in
  2024-2025 are expected in service in 2027 (SRC-TG-193). The causes of this pace are set out in
  FND-TG-191.
- **Targets without a delivery projection (policy).** The World Bank-cited updated strategy gives a 4,800 MW 2030 capacity target. The sources reviewed do not provide its year-by-year delivery pathway. The 2015 Solar Plan has intermediate milestones, and TEREG gives a 3,300 MW milestone for 2028; these are separate planning references, not a pathway proving delivery of the updated target. The 2035 strategy synthesis gives discrepant 2035 figures, 8,530 MW on p. 12 and 8,350 MW on p. 17 (SRC-TG-103).
- **Finance (economic).** The World Bank says scale-up "will depend on the availability of
  financing" (SRC-TG-196) and that STEG's finances as main buyer have held back private
  investment (SRC-TG-105). FND-TG-162 covers the projected STEG deficit.

## Evidence

**Targets and metrics.** These sources do not establish a single revised generation-share series; capacity targets and share targets should be read separately.
- 30% of generation and 3,815 MW in 2030; the same document says this capacity is about 35% of installed capacity (SRC-TG-192, p. 10).
- 30% of the electricity mix in 2030, as summarized in the 2035 strategy synthesis
  (SRC-TG-103).
- 35% of the "energy mix" in 2030 and 4,800 MW (World Bank, SRC-TG-105).
- 35% of "electricity system capacity" in 2030 (World Bank brief, SRC-TG-196).
- 35% of the electricity mix (FNF paper, SRC-TG-195) and 35% of generation (press quoting ANME,
  tier C, SRC-TG-199).
Capacity share and generation share are distinct measures: IRENA reports 15% of installed capacity in 2024 and 6% of generation in 2023 (SRC-TG-190). The World Bank brief explicitly describes the target as 35% of electricity system capacity; the updated strategy metric in the appraisal summary is only called “energy mix.” The official definition for the updated 35% statement remains unresolved.

**What the regional adequacy model assumes.** The Med-TSO mid-term adequacy study, built on
TSO-submitted data, assumes renewables at 30.1% of 7,316 MW in 2027 and 46.9% of 10,520 MW in 2030,
with reported component shares of 25% solar and 4% wind in 2027, and 30% solar, 15% wind and 5% batteries in 2030 (SRC-TG-226). These components do not reconcile with the respective 30.1% and 46.9% RES shares; the 2030 components total 50%. Using the separately reported RES shares, the implied totals are approximately 2,200 MW in 2027 and 4,930 MW in 2030 (about 1,580 MW of wind), the latter close to the 4,800 MW target (SRC-TG-105). These are approximate derived values from model inputs, not forecasts or evidence the capacity will exist.
The ELMED appraisal also models a "reduced RE development scenario" in which Tunisia stays a net
importer from Italy in 2030 and 2040 (SRC-TG-224), but its extract does not give the capacity
assumed.

**What is in the pipeline at end 2025 (SRC-TG-193).** Concessions not yet in service: Gafsa
100 MW (2027), three 100 MW projects of AO-01-2022 (signed March 2025, service 2027), two 100 MW
projects of the second round (awarded), El Khobna 198 MW (signed March 2025), one 75 MW wind
project (awarded). The listed concessions total 873 MW of awarded or signed capacity. Separately, the fifth authorisation call produced agreements in principle for 288 MW; these are not equivalent to commissioned or signed capacity. Tozeur
and Sidi Bouzid (100 MW together) entered service in early 2026, after the 2025 count.

**Our arithmetic, not a forecast.** 1,086 MW at end-2024 (SRC-TG-190) + 124 MW added during 2025 (SRC-TG-193) + 100 MW commissioned in early 2026 (SRC-TG-193) + 873 MW of awarded/signed concessions + 288 MW of authorization agreements in principle (SRC-TG-193) gives about 2,470 MW. This conditional order-of-magnitude calculation assumes every listed mixed-stage project is built by 2030 and includes no other additions; on that assumption, the total is about 1,350 MW below 3,815 MW and 2,330 MW below 4,800 MW. The sum mixes IRENA's all-regime total with ONEM's project list, so it is
only an order of magnitude. Rooftop solar is growing fast (543 MW residential at end September
2026, SRC-TG-100), and further tender rounds of the 1,700 MW programme are planned (SRC-TG-105),
so the gap could narrow; no source quantifies by how much.

**Intermediate milestone.** The World Bank programme targets 3,300 MW by 2028, from 516 MW at
end 2023 on its own count (SRC-TG-105). The baseline is half of IRENA's (SRC-TG-190), so the two
cannot be compared directly.

## Why confidence is low

The targets are well sourced, but the finding is about a future gap and no projection was found.
The gap shown is our own arithmetic on sources with different scopes (IRENA all regimes, ONEM
project list, World Bank count excluding most rooftops). The official metric of the 35% target
was not found in a ministry or ANME text. Recent acceleration (renewable output +66% in
January-September 2026, SRC-TG-100) could change the picture faster than the historical pace
suggests.

## Links to other sectors

None found directly. If the target is missed, the gap is filled by gas-fired generation and
imports (FND-TG-102, FND-TG-222).
