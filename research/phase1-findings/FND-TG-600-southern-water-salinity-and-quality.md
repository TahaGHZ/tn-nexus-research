## Problem

Tunisia’s national climate contribution gives a 2024 drinking-water baseline of 1.5–3 g/L across the north and south (SRC-TG-605). It does not assign a mean or a sampling distribution to any governorate.

Salinity constrained water quality in southern Tunisia during the historical periods documented by the sources. The 2001 national environment report states that average drinking-water salinity in the South improved from 2.4 to 1.4 g/L, but gives no measurement years or method (SRC-TG-603). A SONEDE official's 2017 presentation classifies only 3 Mm3 of 859 Mm3 of southern groundwater resources as below 1.5 g/L salinity, though the data year is unspecified (SRC-TG-604). That resource estimate does not establish the quality of water delivered to households, which can be affected by treatment and blending; no current estimate is supported here.

## Root causes

The available evidence documents saline source water as a constraint: the SONEDE presentation's salinity table shows almost all southern groundwater above 1.5 g/L, although its data year and method are unspecified (SRC-TG-604). The 2001 environment report says new northern reservoirs and southern groundwater desalination, combined with a water-mixing programme, improved drinking-water quality (SRC-TG-603). The evidence does not isolate the contribution of each intervention or establish a current causal trend.

## Evidence

The 2001 report gives southern average drinking-water salinity as 2.4 then 1.4 g/L, without dating the two measurements (SRC-TG-603). In a 2017 presentation, SONEDE reports 3 Mm3 below 1.5 g/L out of 859 Mm3 of southern groundwater resources; nationally, its table gives 381 Mm3 out of 1,898 Mm3 below that threshold (SRC-TG-604). The 2001 report also states phosphogypsum disposal at 13,000 tonnes/day nationally and mentions Gabès among affected industrial regions, but this does not substantiate a 2017 stop order for Gabès discharges (SRC-TG-603).

## Why confidence is what it is

Confidence is medium for the existence of a historical salinity constraint because the report and SONEDE presentation are official sources and the figures are explicit. Confidence is limited for present-day exposure: the older drinking-water figures lack dates and methods, and the later salinity table concerns groundwater resources, with no stated reference year, rather than delivered water. The specific allegation of a 2017 Gabès discharge stoppage is not evidenced here.

## Links to other sectors

Water desalination links saline source quality to energy demand; the cited sources describe desalination programmes but do not quantify associated energy use (SRC-TG-603, SRC-TG-604).

## Leads for phase 2

- SONEDE's regional programme to improve drinking-water quality through desalination and water mixing (SRC-TG-603, SRC-TG-604).

## Verification

The dated drinking-water baseline is distinct from the earlier groundwater resource inventory. Historical water-quality observations without sampling dates remain narrative evidence; the 2035 target is prospective (SRC-TG-605, SRC-TG-603, SRC-TG-604).
