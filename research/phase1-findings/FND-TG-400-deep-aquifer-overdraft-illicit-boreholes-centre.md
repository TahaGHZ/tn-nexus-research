## Problem

Tunisia's deep aquifers were drawn at 133% of their estimated resource in 2016 (1,895 Mm3 against 1,422 Mm3) and at 134% in 2020 (SRC-TG-402, SRC-TG-403). The national figure hides a sharp regional pattern. Withdrawals from the deep aquifers of the DGRE "Centre" region doubled from 262 Mm3 in 2006 to 534 Mm3 in 2016, 162% of its 330 Mm3 resource. Nabeul (Cap Bon) reached 195%, Kairouan 127%, Kasserine 120% and Sidi Bouzid 114%. The North as a whole stayed below its resource at 88% (SRC-TG-402). A growing part of the pumping goes through boreholes drilled without authorisation: an estimated 48% of boreholes in 2016, and 60% of deep-aquifer water points (21,279) in the 2023 indicators. These illicit boreholes drew 28% of the volume (SRC-TG-403; 22% in 2016, SRC-TG-402). On the Kairouan plain, pumping rose from 31.3 Mm3 in 1970 to 111.43 Mm3 in 2016, and the water table fell by 16 m at Draa Affane between 1987 and 2017 (SRC-TG-408).

## Root causes

The symptom is a falling water table and exploitation rates above 100%. The causes the ministry itself names are these (SRC-TG-402):

1. **Illicit drilling.** Withdrawals by illicit boreholes are estimated to have risen from 67 Mm3 (5%) in 2011 to 416 Mm3 (22%) in 2016 (SRC-TG-402), and to about 541 Mm3 in the 2023 indicators (SRC-TG-403). The ministry estimates illicit boreholes at 4,559 in Kebili (also a 171% deep-aquifer rate, outside the central hotspots), 2,500 in Kairouan, 2,100 in Sidi Bouzid, 1,300 in Kasserine and 1,531 in Nabeul (SRC-TG-402). A 2026 press report attributes the rise to long authorisation procedures, drought, and the spread of irrigated crops and greenhouses (SRC-TG-409, tier C).
2. **Enforcement that does not reach the borehole.** The 1975 Water Code gives the minister the tools: prohibition and safeguard perimeters, suspension and demolition of works, and a fine of one tenth of the cost of the works (SRC-TG-406). In practice the CRDAs "can only record the facts". In 2017 Sidi Bouzid's CRDA wrote 80 infraction reports and made 2 seizures, but plugged no boreholes, and reports often fail on procedural defects (SRC-TG-402). The perimeters decreed decades ago, for example in Nabeul, no longer match where pumping happens (SRC-TG-402). This is an institutional failure that predates the illicit-drilling surge, so `legacy_decision` is true. Monitoring is shrinking at the same time: 1,940 piezometric points in 2016 against 2,183 in 2010, with very few piezometers installed after 2007 (SRC-TG-402).
3. **Drought as a trigger, not the root.** The 2016 jump from 119% to 133% came when drought rationing of dam water pushed irrigators onto groundwater (SRC-TG-402). Withdrawals were already rising before the drought: Centre deep-aquifer pumping went from 262 Mm3 (2006) to 413 Mm3 (2015) (SRC-TG-402).
4. **Free energy for pumping.** The ministry's own report says the state subsidy for photovoltaic irrigation pumps encourages waste by making the water "totalement gratuite", with no STEG bill to pay (SRC-TG-402).

On the Kairouan plain, a groundwater model attributes the decline mainly to pumping rather than to the upstream dams (Sidi Saad, El Houareb). It also notes that "legislation related to groundwater access" is cited as a main factor and that since the spread of deep wells "it is very delicate to define even the number of wells" (SRC-TG-408).

## Evidence

- National deep-aquifer rate: 82% (2006), 92% (2010), 119% (2015) and 133% (2016). Only the 2014-2016 rates reconcile with the current resource; the earlier ones were computed on an older base (SRC-TG-402, notes). The current resource is 1,422 Mm3, about 47% of which is non-renewable (SRC-TG-402). The 2023 indicators give 82% (1996), 90% (2010) and 134% (2020) (SRC-TG-403). The two disagree slightly on 2010 (92% vs 90%).
- Regional deep-aquifer rates in 2016: Nord 88%, Centre 162%, Sud 139%. The source does not list which governorates DGRE puts in "Centre". It is tagged `centre-west` here because the overexploited central governorates it names are Kairouan, Kasserine and Sidi Bouzid, but it may also include Sahel governorates. Governorates: Nabeul 195%, Kairouan 127%, Kasserine 120%, Sidi Bouzid 114% (SRC-TG-402).
- Central Tunisia's aquifers are described as the most exploited, at about 160%, in the 2023 indicators (SRC-TG-403).
- Illicit boreholes: 13,463 of 28,158 (48%) in 2016 (SRC-TG-402), and 21,279 of 35,428 water points (60%) in the 2023 indicators (SRC-TG-403). The World Bank CCDR gives 20,350 illicit against 14,117 authorised boreholes in 2021, close to the 2023 indicators, and extraction rates of up to 400% in some regions (SRC-TG-482). By governorate, Sidi Bouzid rises from 2,100 to 4,700, Kasserine from 1,300 to 2,430 and Nabeul from 1,531 to 1,796 between the two sources. Kairouan is not listed in the 2023 extract, which gives 9,252 for Kebili, the largest figure (SRC-TG-403).
- Against a 2016 target of 107.3%, the outcome was 133% (SRC-TG-402).
- Kairouan plain: pumping went from 31.3 Mm3 (1970) to 111.43 Mm3 (2016). Piezometer 12737/4 fell about 19 m over 1970-2017 (-0.4 m/yr), and the Draa Affane piezometer fell 16 m over 1987-2017 (SRC-TG-408).
- Consequences named by the sources: rising salinity and seawater intrusion in some coastal aquifers (SRC-TG-403).

## Why confidence is what it is

Medium. The direction and the order of magnitude are solid, and they come from the Ministry of Agriculture's own reports based on DGRE data (tier A). But:
- Illicit-borehole counts are explicitly estimates built from partial CRDA inventories (SRC-TG-402), and the two official sources differ by about 8,000 boreholes. The difference may be real growth or a change of method; the 2023 count has no stated year.
- A 2026 press report quoting the director-general of water resources gives 205 unauthorised deep wells in 2010 and 3,229 in 2024 (SRC-TG-409). This is an order of magnitude below the ministry's published estimates and is probably a narrower category or a reporting error. It is not used for any indicator.
- The ministry report labels its headline rate as both excluding and including illicit boreholes. Its own table, though, shows that the 1,895 Mm3 total contains the 416 Mm3 illicit estimate (22.0%) (SRC-TG-402). Likewise, 1,381 + 541 Mm3 in SRC-TG-403 makes about 1,922 Mm3, roughly 135% of 1,422 Mm3. Headline rates therefore already include *estimated* illicit pumping. The real overdraft would be higher only if those estimates are too low; SRC-TG-408 notes that official pumping figures may be underestimates.
- Exploitation rates depend on the resource estimate (the denominator), which DGRE revises periodically (see FND-TG-401).
- Kairouan piezometric data come from one peer-reviewed model study. Equivalent openable series for Sidi Bouzid, Kasserine and Cap Bon deep aquifers were not found.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture:** the overdraft is driven by irrigation. In 2018, 84% of the 2.4 billion m3 used for irrigation came from groundwater (SRC-TG-504). Drought rationing of surface irrigation water and rising crop water needs drive withdrawals (SRC-TG-402). The ministry also notes that perimeters growing high-export-potential crops are overexploited "portée par une course à l'augmentation de revenus" (driven by a race for higher income) (SRC-TG-402). The crops it names are dates and early vegetables (primeurs), so this applies to oases and market gardening more than specifically to the central hotspots.
- **Energy:** the state subsidy for solar pumping removes the electricity cost of pumping and is described by the ministry as working against conservation (SRC-TG-402). Falling water tables also raise pumping energy costs (stated by the DGRE director-general in SRC-TG-409, tier C).
- **Water quality:** coastal salinisation from the same pumping, including Cap Bon (Korba), is covered in FND-TG-502.
- **Drinking water:** SONEDE supplies in groundwater-fed regions suffer when piezometric levels drop in dry years (SRC-TG-402). The regions it names (Gafsa, Metlaoui, Redayef, Kef, Zaghouan) are outside this finding's hotspots, and the effect is not quantified here.

## Leads for phase 2

- New Water Code (projet de code des eaux), pending since at least 2017 (SRC-TG-402).
- Draft law no. 18/026 on regularising unauthorised deep wells (SRC-TG-409).
- Smart meters and a real-time information system on well withdrawals announced by DGGREE (SRC-TG-409).
- Artificial aquifer recharge programme, including with treated wastewater (SRC-TG-402).
- AGIRE project (Appui à la Gestion Intégrée des Ressources en Eau): training on public water domain infractions and a manual of infraction procedures (SRC-TG-402).
- Local intervention committees and enforcement brigades against illicit boreholes (SRC-TG-402).
- Revision of the Nabeul prohibition and safeguard perimeters (SRC-TG-402).
- Projet Eau Kasserine, a piezometric and hydrochemical monitoring experience (mentioned in SRC-TG-402).
