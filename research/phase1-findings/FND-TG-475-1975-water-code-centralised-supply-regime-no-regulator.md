## Problem

Tunisia's water law is still the Water Code promulgated by Law 75-16 of 31 March 1975 (SRC-TG-406). The code organised the State's drive to mobilise water: it makes all surface and groundwater public domain, administered by the Minister of Agriculture (art. 1-4), and leaves water charges and drinking-water and irrigation tariffs to ministerial orders and decrees (art. 63, 92, 106 bis) (SRC-TG-406). By 2015 that drive had mobilised 98% of mobilisable resources, with water management centralised in the Ministry of Agriculture (SRC-TG-479). The regime's supply phase is therefore over, but its institutions and pricing rules remain, because the replacement code started in 2008-2009 had still not been adopted in 2026 (SRC-TG-479, SRC-TG-488). This record covers the past decision; its current effects on prices and debts are in FND-TG-476 and FND-TG-477.

## Root causes

- **Symptom vs cause.** The symptoms (overdrawn aquifers, operators in deficit, unpaid bills) are covered elsewhere. The cause recorded here is the legal and institutional design of 1975 and the failure to replace it.
- **A supply-oriented code (decision of 1975).** The ministry's own explanatory memorandum to the 2019 bill says aquifers were exploited beyond allowable limits because the current code is "not comprehensive and oriented mainly towards supply" (SRC-TG-484, p. 4). The bill's sponsors told parliament that much of the problem comes from "the governance model of the 1975 code", which is "highly centralised" and does not set shared responsibility (SRC-TG-484, p. 15).
- **Tariffs set by political decision, with no regulator.** The code does not set pricing principles. Tariffs are fixed after the operator files a case with its supervising ministry, sometimes up to an interministerial council, and are published by ministerial order; there is no independent regulator (SRC-TG-455, p. 19). In 2009 the World Bank already described an "incomprehension" between the State and SONEDE/ONAS over tariffs and recommended a regulatory body "en priorité" (SRC-TG-455, p. 9). In 2018 it still described tariff adjustment as "ad hoc" (SRC-TG-481, p. 8).
- **Fragmented operators (decisions of 1968-1987).** SONEDE (1968) serves urban and part of rural drinking water (SRC-TG-481). User associations were given legal form as "associations d'intérêt collectif" in 1987 (art. 154, SRC-TG-406), and management of rural water systems has been handed to them since 1987 (SRC-TG-484, p. 16). SONEDE and ONAS have no joint planning body and compete for tariff revisions, which the State generally never grants both in the same year (SRC-TG-455, p. 45).
- **A stalled replacement (2008 onwards).** The revision started in 2008 according to the press (SRC-TG-488) or 2009 according to the OTE (SRC-TG-479). A first version is dated 2012 or 2014 (the OTE brief gives both, SRC-TG-479). The UGTT opposed articles it saw as opening water services to privatisation; a compromise came only in May 2019 (SRC-TG-479). The bill was referred to parliament on 3 October 2019; the agriculture committee finished its work on 18 June 2021 and approved it unanimously, as amended (SRC-TG-484, pp. 1, 114). The process was then interrupted in 2021. An amended version was sent to the competent services on 30 June 2026 and, as of 9 August 2026, had not yet gone to the Council of Ministers (SRC-TG-488, tier C).

## Evidence

- Law 75-16, adopted 18 March 1975, JORT no. 22 of 1 April 1975 (SRC-TG-406, p. 5).
- Art. 4: the public water domain is administered by the Minister of Agriculture, assisted by a National Water Council (SRC-TG-406, p. 8). The OTE says the council has been "barely active since its creation" (SRC-TG-479, p. 1).
- Art. 63: the charge on water withdrawals is a revisable scale set by joint order of the Agriculture and Finance ministers (SRC-TG-406, p. 26). Art. 92: domestic use above norms pays a progressive tariff (p. 36). Art. 106 bis (added 1988, amended 2001): irrigation water supply and pricing in public perimeters by CRDAs is set by a decree-approved specification (p. 40).
- 98% of mobilisable resources mobilised in 2015 (SRC-TG-479, p. 3).
- ARP committee record: referral 3 October 2019, committee referral 26 December 2019, end of work 18 June 2021, unanimous approval (SRC-TG-484, pp. 1, 114).
- The committee recommended that water tariffs be unified for all citizens (SRC-TG-484, p. 113), which shows that two tariff systems (SONEDE and GDA) coexist.

**Sources disagree on these points; both values are kept.**
- *Was the code amended?* The World Bank CCDR says the 1975 code "n'a jamais été modifié" (SRC-TG-482, p. 26, footnote 99). The official consolidated text shows amendments in 1987, 1988, 2001 and 2004 (SRC-TG-406). The primary text refutes the CCDR statement; what is true is that the code was never replaced and its governance model was not reformed.
- *Start of the revision:* 2008 (SRC-TG-488) or 2009 (SRC-TG-479).
- *Referral to parliament:* 3 October 2019 (SRC-TG-484, official record) or "une première transmission au Parlement en 2020" (SRC-TG-488, press).

## Why confidence is what it is

The legal facts rest on tier A texts (code, ARP report). The causal claim that the code's supply orientation and centralisation drove the problems rests on the ministry's own statements to parliament (SRC-TG-484) and on tier B reports (SRC-TG-479, SRC-TG-455), which is reasonable but not a measured effect. The 98% figure comes from an NGO brief citing a book. The post-2021 status rests on one press article (tier C). Hence `medium`.

`timeframe: past` describes the 1975-2015 supply-mobilisation regime, which has played out (resources are almost fully mobilised). The governance model it left behind is still in force, which is why `trend` is `stable` rather than improving.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture.** The code and the 1987 associations frame irrigation management: CRDAs and GDAs run public irrigated perimeters under art. 106 bis and 154 (SRC-TG-406). The 2019 bill's memorandum names the "failure" of GDAs and of oversight of their finances as a reason for reform (SRC-TG-484, p. 5). Pricing and debt consequences are in FND-TG-477.
- **Energy.** None found at the level of the code itself. The energy link appears in the operators' costs (FND-TG-476, FND-TG-477).
