## Problem

Tunisia’s Water Code was promulgated by Law 75-16 of 31 March 1975 (SRC-TG-406). In the consolidated text inspected, it assigns administration of the public water domain to the Minister of Agriculture (article 4) and provides for specific charges or tariffs through revisable ministerial scales, ministerial orders and decree-approved specifications (articles 63, 92 and 106 bis); it does not establish the actual tariff levels in those provisions (SRC-TG-406). OTE reports that 98% of mobilisable resources had been mobilised nationally in 2015, attributing the figure to a cited 2018 book; this is a reported estimate, not a directly inspected administrative series (SRC-TG-479). The official Assembly committee report says a replacement bill was referred on 3 October 2019 and committee work ended on 18 June 2021 (SRC-TG-484). WMC attributes to the Agriculture Ministry a transmission of an amended draft on 30 June 2026 and reports that it still awaited Council of Ministers review as of 9 August 2026; this tier C account does not establish current legal status (SRC-TG-488). Current tariff and debt consequences are addressed in FND-TG-476 and FND-TG-477.

## Root causes

- **Symptom vs cause.** The symptoms (overdrawn aquifers, operators in deficit, unpaid bills) are covered elsewhere. The cause recorded here is the legal and institutional design of 1975 and the failure to replace it.
- **A supply-oriented code (decision of 1975).** The ministry's own explanatory memorandum to the 2019 bill says aquifers were exploited beyond allowable limits because the current code is "not comprehensive and oriented mainly towards supply" (SRC-TG-484, p. 4). The bill's sponsors told parliament that much of the problem comes from "the governance model of the 1975 code", which is "highly centralised" and does not set shared responsibility (SRC-TG-484, p. 15).
- **Tariffs set by political decision.** The code does not set tariff levels or a cost-linkage rule in the cited provisions: withdrawal charges use a revisable joint ministerial scale, domestic overuse has a progressive tariff, and irrigation supply and pricing in public schemes are governed by a decree-approved specification (SRC-TG-406, arts. 63, 92, 106 bis). The 2009 World Bank review explicitly described the absence of an independent regulator at that time and recommended creating one as a priority (SRC-TG-455, pp. 9, 19). Its 2018 concept-stage project document described tariff adjustment as ad hoc (SRC-TG-481, p. 8). These sources establish the historical arrangement and recommendation, not whether a regulator was established after 2018 or the present tariff-setting process.
- **Fragmented operators (decisions of 1968-1987).** SONEDE (1968) serves urban and part of rural drinking water (SRC-TG-481). User associations were given legal form as "associations d'intérêt collectif" in 1987 (art. 154, SRC-TG-406), and management of rural water systems has been handed to them since 1987 (SRC-TG-484, p. 16). SONEDE and ONAS have no joint planning body and compete for tariff revisions, which the State generally never grants both in the same year (SRC-TG-455, p. 45).
- **A stalled replacement (2008 onwards).** OTE’s 2019 brief describes the first initiative as 2009 and says a first proposal followed in 2014; WMC’s 2026 press article dates the revision to 2008 (SRC-TG-479, SRC-TG-488). These are attributed descriptions with differing milestones, not a single established start date. The UGTT opposed articles it saw as opening water services to privatisation; a compromise came only in May 2019 (SRC-TG-479). The official Assembly report records referral to parliament on 3 October 2019, referral to committee on 26 December 2019, and committee work ending on 18 June 2021; it records unanimous committee approval of the amended bill, not plenary enactment (SRC-TG-484, pp. 1, 114). WMC reports an interruption in 2021 and says an amended version was sent to competent services on 30 June 2026 and still awaited Council of Ministers review on 9 August (SRC-TG-488, tier C).

## Evidence

- Law 75-16, adopted 18 March 1975, JORT no. 22 of 1 April 1975 (SRC-TG-406, p. 5).
- Art. 4: the public water domain is administered by the Minister of Agriculture, assisted by a National Water Council (SRC-TG-406, p. 8). The OTE says the council has been "barely active since its creation" (SRC-TG-479, p. 1).
- Art. 63: the charge on water withdrawals is a revisable scale set by joint order of the Agriculture and Finance ministers (SRC-TG-406, p. 26). Art. 92: domestic use above norms pays a progressive tariff (p. 36). Art. 106 bis (added 1988, amended 2001): irrigation water supply and pricing in public perimeters by CRDAs is set by a decree-approved specification (p. 40).
- 98% of mobilisable resources mobilised in 2015 (SRC-TG-479, p. 3).
- ARP committee record: referral 3 October 2019, committee referral 26 December 2019, end of work 18 June 2021, unanimous approval (SRC-TG-484, pp. 1, 114).
- The committee recommended that water tariffs be unified for all citizens (SRC-TG-484, p. 113), which records the committee’s recommendation to unify tariffs; that recommendation alone does not specify the full number or structure of tariff systems.

**Sources disagree on these points; the primary record is preferred where available.**
- *Was the code amended?* The World Bank CCDR says the 1975 code "n'a jamais été modifié" (SRC-TG-482, p. 26, footnote 99). The official consolidated text shows amendments in 1987, 1988, 2001 and 2004 (SRC-TG-406). The primary text refutes the CCDR statement; what is true is that the code was never replaced and its governance model was not reformed.
- *Start of the revision:* 2008 (SRC-TG-488) or 2009 (SRC-TG-479).
- *Referral to parliament:* 3 October 2019 (SRC-TG-484, official record) or "une première transmission au Parlement en 2020" (SRC-TG-488, press).

## Why confidence is what it is

The legal facts rest on tier A texts (code, ARP report). The causal claim that the code's supply orientation and centralisation drove the problems rests on the ministry's own statements to parliament (SRC-TG-484) and on tier B reports (SRC-TG-479, SRC-TG-455), which is reasonable but not a measured effect. The 98% figure is a secondary report in an NGO policy brief attributed to a cited 2018 book, not a direct statistical series. The later draft-status account rests on one press article (tier C). Hence `medium`.

`timeframe: past` refers to the 1975 legal decision and the historical mobilisation period. The 98% figure is a reported 2015 estimate and does not demonstrate that mobilisation is complete or establish the current legal/institutional situation. `trend: stable` describes the enduring legal framework as last directly documented in the consolidated text; a current legal check is still required before asserting the present regulatory arrangements.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture.** The code and the 1987 associations frame irrigation management: CRDAs and GDAs run public irrigated perimeters under art. 106 bis and 154 (SRC-TG-406). The 2019 bill's memorandum names the "failure" of GDAs and of oversight of their finances as a reason for reform (SRC-TG-484, p. 5). Pricing and debt consequences are in FND-TG-477.
- **Energy.** None found at the level of the code itself. The energy link appears in the operators' costs (FND-TG-476, FND-TG-477).

The historical statute and legislative filing are dated separately. The ARP’s filing date takes precedence over press accounts; disputed revision-start dates and the current status of the replacement bill are outside the verified historical claim.
