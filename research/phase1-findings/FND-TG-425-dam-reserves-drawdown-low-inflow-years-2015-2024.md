## Problem

Tunisia's surface storage sits mainly in 37 large dams at end-2017 (SRC-TG-402). About 90% of their mean annual inflow (1,613 of 1,802 Mm3) comes from the northern dams (SRC-TG-438). Over the ten hydrological years 2015/16 to 2024/25, inflows were below the long-term mean printed in the official bulletins in nine years and below half of it in six (SRC-TG-433 to SRC-TG-442). The national stock at the end of the hydrological year (1 September) fell from 1,184 Mm3 in 2015 to 542 Mm3 in 2024, a printed fill rate of 23.1% (SRC-TG-432, SRC-TG-441). On that date only 298 Mm3 of the 542 Mm3 was counted as still exploitable (SRC-TG-441). Sidi Salem, the largest reservoir, held 105 Mm3 (18.1%), of which 25 Mm3 was exploitable. Nebhana, which serves the Sahel, held 1.9 Mm3 (2.9%) with nothing exploitable (SRC-TG-441). One wet year (2018/19, 146% of mean) refilled the system to 1,450 Mm3 (64.4%) on 1 September 2019, and the following dry years emptied it again within four years (SRC-TG-436, SRC-TG-440).

## Root causes

**Symptom.** Low fill rates. **Causes**, in order of what the data show:

1. **Inflow deficit.** Cumulative inflows per hydrological year, each against the long-term mean printed in the same bulletin:
   - 2015/16: 650 vs 2,083 Mm3 (31%), from the total row of the bulletin (SRC-TG-433). The bulletin's summary block prints a different mean of 1,925.78 Mm3 (34%), so the baseline is not stable even within one bulletin. The 2017 bulletin prints 684.246 Mm3 for 2015/16 in its prior-year column, a revision of the 650.378 Mm3 (SRC-TG-434).
   - 2016/17: 835 vs 2,015 Mm3 (41%) (SRC-TG-434)
   - 2017/18: 1,079 vs 1,664 Mm3 (65%) (SRC-TG-435)
   - 2018/19: 2,575 vs 1,762 Mm3 (146%) (SRC-TG-436)
   - 2019/20: 791 vs 1,780 Mm3 (44%) (SRC-TG-437)
   - 2020/21: 804 vs 1,888 Mm3 (43%) (SRC-TG-438)
   - 2021/22: 1,109 vs 1,888 Mm3 (59%) (SRC-TG-439)
   - 2022/23: 694 vs 1,814 Mm3 (38%) (SRC-TG-440)
   - 2023/24: 668 vs 1,814 Mm3 (37%) (SRC-TG-441)
   - 2024/25: 982 vs 1,814 Mm3 (54%) (SRC-TG-442)

   The mean used as the baseline changes from bulletin to bulletin (1,664 to 2,015 Mm3), so the percentages are only roughly comparable. The national water report for 2017 already noted a third consecutive rainfall deficit in the north and centre-west, where the large dams are concentrated (SRC-TG-402, section IV.1).
2. **Demand that kept growing.** Withdrawals from the Medjerda-Cap Bon canal rose from 205 Mm3 in 2008 to 317 Mm3 in 2017 (SRC-TG-402, drinking-water section). This dependence is a legacy of the transfer-based supply model (FND-TG-426). The bulletins' release totals cannot show whether allocations were cut quickly enough in dry years: the inflow and release totals do not close the stock balance, and releases are not split by use (see data_gaps). This record therefore does not claim over-release.
3. **Shrinking buffer.** Silt occupies part of the capacity of the largest dams, for example 234 of 814 Mm3 at Sidi Salem at the 2018 survey (SRC-TG-438). This reduces how much a wet year can carry over into dry years (FND-TG-427).

- **Caution on percentages.**
  - The fill rate is the stock divided by the useful capacity at the last bathymetric survey. That denominator still counts silt deposited since the survey, so the true fill of the remaining space is slightly higher than printed.
  - Total useful capacity grew from 2,104 Mm3 (2015) to 2,347 Mm3 (2024) as new dams were added (SRC-TG-432, SRC-TG-441). Percentages across years therefore mix drought with system expansion. Stocks in Mm3 are the safer series.
  - Successive bulletins revise the previous year's figures. For example, the 1 September 2023 stock is 672.650 Mm3 in SRC-TG-440 and 667.325 Mm3 in the prior-year column of SRC-TG-441.

## Why confidence is what it is

High. All stocks and inflows come from the dams directorate's own bulletins (tier A), read from the PDF tables. The overall pattern of repeated deficits, drawdown and a single refill in 2019 does not depend on any one bulletin. Limits:

- Only one date per year (1 September) was read.
- Percentages before 2019 are not printed.
- The long-term mean used as the baseline is revised between bulletins.
- The split of releases between uses is unknown.

Trend is "unknown". The 2024/25 year partly recovered (712 Mm3, 30.1%), and this record does not project forward; see FND-TG-500 for climate projections.

## Links to other sectors

- **Agriculture.** The 2017 report links drought rationing of surface irrigation water to the jump in groundwater pumping, from 119% to 133% of deep-aquifer resources between 2015 and 2016 (SRC-TG-402, executive summary, already extracted in that record). Low stocks push irrigators onto aquifers. Groundwater itself belongs to block 400-424 (FND-TG-400).
- **Drinking water.** The northern dams supply SONEDE's coastal systems through SECADENORD transfers (FND-TG-426). Supply cuts belong to block 450-474.
- **Energy.** Sidi Salem has a 36 MW hydropower plant, and low stock limits it (plant size from the bulletin row label, SRC-TG-438). No generation data were collected.
