## Problem

SONEDE, the national utility supplying all urban and about half of rural drinking water to more than 2.7 million clients (SRC-TG-481), sells water below what it costs to produce. In 2017 its average cost was 0.900 TND/m3 against an average selling price of 0.700 TND/m3, a recovery rate of 78%, and its deficit rose from 21 to 39 million TND between 2016 and 2017 (SRC-TG-402). In 2021 the World Bank gives an average cost of 1.30 TND/m3 against a selling price of 0.94 TND/m3 (SRC-TG-482), which works out at about 72%. In 2016 SONEDE carried 441 million TND of debt and a negative return on equity (SRC-TG-481). The World Bank and the minister of agriculture link this deficit to deferred maintenance and network renewal (SRC-TG-481, SRC-TG-484).

**This is not a permanent feature.** In 2001-2005 SONEDE made small positive net results and its revenues covered all operating costs including depreciation (operating ratio 1.07 falling to 1.03), helped by regular tariff increases of about 3.2% a year in 1998-2005 (SRC-TG-455). The CEO dates the widening of the cost-price gap to 2007-2009 (SRC-TG-486). Saying that Tunisian drinking water has "always" been sold below cost is wrong for SONEDE.

## Root causes

- **Symptom:** the deficit, the debt and the lack of maintenance money are symptoms.
- **Cause 1, how tariffs are decided (policy, legacy of the 1975 framework).** Tariffs are set case by case by the supervising ministry, sometimes by an interministerial council, with no independent regulator (SRC-TG-455, p. 19; see FND-TG-475). Increases are indexed neither to costs, nor to charges, nor to the cost of living (SRC-TG-402, p. 93). After the regular increases of 1998-2005, there were only four increases between 2006 and 2016, in 2010, 2011, 2013 and 2016 (SRC-TG-481), the 2016 tariff was kept unchanged in 2017 (SRC-TG-402), and the CEO speaks of a "gel du prix de l'eau" (SRC-TG-486, tier C). The World Bank called the adjustment process "ad hoc" in 2018 (SRC-TG-481). In parliament in December 2020 the minister said the current tariff "in no way reflects the real cost borne by the State" (SRC-TG-484, p. 12).
- **Cause 2, rising costs (economic).** Energy rose from 10% of SONEDE's cost in 2007 to 19% at the end of 2018, after cumulative electricity price increases of 62% (SRC-TG-486). Energy is now over 64% of purchased inputs, and staff costs rose faster after 2011 (SRC-TG-402, p. 93). Desalination plants (ten installed in 2017-2018 according to the CEO, SRC-TG-486) and the dinar's depreciation, with 70% of equipment imported, raised costs further (SRC-TG-486). The ministry report itself says the deficit is "not only due to reluctance to raise tariffs" (SRC-TG-402).
- **Cause 3, no compensation for the gap (institutional).** In 2016 SONEDE received no operating subsidy; only part of its investment was covered by the State, and the World Bank links its fragile finances to under-maintenance and lack of investment (SRC-TG-481, p. 4). The minister told parliament SONEDE cannot maintain and renew networks for lack of resources and of State support (SRC-TG-484, p. 12). The gap therefore turns into debt and deferred maintenance. At the level of the whole water cycle the State does bear most costs: 3.1 billion TND, 82% of costs from dam mobilisation to sanitation, in 2018 (SRC-TG-482). How much of that support reaches SONEDE's operating account is not shown in the sources.
- The CEO also blames the doubling of subscribers (1.548 million in 2000 to 2.985 million in 2019, SRC-TG-486). That explains the growth in volume, but not by itself a per-m3 deficit; it is recorded as his view.

## Evidence

- Operating ratio 1.07 (2001) to 1.03 (2005); tariff increases about 3.2% per year 1998-2005 (SRC-TG-455, pp. 45-46).
- Tariff increases only in 2010, 2011, 2013 and 2016 since 2006; debt 441 MTND, ROE -0.01, debt/equity 0.35 in 2016 (SRC-TG-481, p. 4).
- 2017: cost 900 millimes/m3, price 700 millimes/m3, recovery 78%, deficit 39 MTND (21 MTND in 2016); tariffs from 200 millimes/m3 (first block, <20 m3/quarter) to 1,315 millimes/m3 (SRC-TG-402, p. 92).
- 2015-2021: average energy cost of drinking water (CCDR wording: "coût énergétique moyen") 0.82 to 1.30 TND/m3; average selling price 0.56 to 0.94 TND/m3 (SRC-TG-482, p. 8). The ratios 68% and 72% are arithmetic comparisons of those reported values, not validated total-cost recovery rates.
- After the 2021 increase, the first consumption block is about USD 0.06/m3 (SRC-TG-482, p. 7, fn. 32).
- Affordability: water services cost 0.88% of an average household's income in 2017 (SRC-TG-402, p. 93).

**Sources disagree, or use different metrics; all values are kept.**
- Cost coverage: 78% in 2017 (ministry, 900/700 millimes per m3, SRC-TG-402); 65.4% in 2019 against 100.4% in 2000 (press, undefined "taux de couverture des coûts", SRC-TG-486); 68% and 72% are calculated comparisons of CCDR energy-cost values against average selling prices, not comparable recovery rates (SRC-TG-482). These series use different definitions and are not joined into a single trend.
- Prior-year recovery: the ministry report says "en 2017 il était de 89%" right after giving 78% for 2017, probably a typo for 2016 (SRC-TG-402).
- The CCDR labels the 0.82-1.30 TND/m3 series an "energy cost". With energy at about 19% of total cost (SRC-TG-486), it is more likely the total average cost. It is used here with that caveat.

## Why confidence is what it is

**Trend.** `worsening` describes the long run: from revenues covering all operating costs in 2001-2005 (SRC-TG-455) to recovery of 65-78% in 2017-2021 (SRC-TG-402, SRC-TG-486, SRC-TG-482), a deficit that nearly doubled in 2016-2017 (SRC-TG-402), and the World Bank's 2018 statement that finances deteriorated over "the last decade" (SRC-TG-481). The short-run series are mixed (calculated 68% in 2015, 78% in 2017, 72% in 2021, from different sources), and nothing after 2021 is sourced, so the recent direction is not established.

**Start date.** "Since about 2007" combines the CEO's statement that the gap widened in 2007-2009 (SRC-TG-486, tier C) with the World Bank's statement that the imbalances date from the last decade and that only four tariff increases came after 2006 (SRC-TG-481). The PPIAF page on SONEDE's financial sustainability was not opened.

The direction of the problem is well supported: a ministry report (tier A), two World Bank documents and the minister's statement to parliament all say tariffs are below cost and the deficit is growing. Confidence is `medium`, not high, because no SONEDE financial statement was opened, the figures come from different documents with different definitions, the 2019 coverage rate and the cost drivers rest on a tier C press report of CEO statements, and nothing after 2021 is sourced.

## Links to other sectors

- **Energy.** SONEDE is the largest single electricity consumer in Tunisia, its electricity use was 0.67 kWh/m3 in 2021, and desalination raised its costs (SRC-TG-482, p. 8). Electricity price increases (cumulative +62%) were a main cost driver (SRC-TG-486), so electricity pricing decisions feed the water utility's deficit directly.
- **Water losses.** The minister links the deficit to SONEDE's inability to maintain and renew networks (SRC-TG-484, p. 12); losses themselves are covered in FND-TG-450 and supply cuts in FND-TG-451.
