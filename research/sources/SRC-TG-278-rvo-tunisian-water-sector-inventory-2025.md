## Extracts

Printed p. 1: "TUNISIAN WATER SECTOR INVENTORY November, 2025"

Printed p. 18, section 1.1.1 "General data":

> Faced with the depletion of conventional resources, Tunisia is strategically turning to non-conventional waters, such as seawater desalination and the reuse of treated wastewater. The current desalination capacity is 50,000 m³/day, with an ambition to reach 775,000 m³/day by 2035. The country has 127 wastewater treatment plants, with a total capacity of about 1 million m³/day. However, this transition to unconventional sources represents a considerable energy and financial cost, with SONEDE's energy bill having increased by 10% between 2022 and 2023.

Printed p. 21:

> The growing reliance on non-conventional water sources, while essential for security, entails a substantial increase in energy intensity.

## Notes

Consultancy inventory for a donor agency; it cites no source for the sentences above. Its
"current desalination capacity" of 50,000 m3/day contradicts SONEDE's own plant pages, which give
50,000 m3/day at Djerba (in service 2018), 50,000 at Zarat (works 2019-2024) and 100,000 at Sfax (in
service 2024) (SRC-TG-271, SRC-TG-272, SRC-TG-273). The figure may be out of date or may count one
plant only. The 10% rise in the energy bill (2022 to 2023) is the only figure we found for the
change in SONEDE's energy spending, and it rests on this one unsourced statement.
