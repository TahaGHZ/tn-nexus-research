## Notes

This is the primary modelling study behind the agricultural projections quoted in the updated NDC (SRC-TG-884), the 4th National Communication (SRC-TG-885) and, through an earlier 2020 deliverable, the World Bank CCDR (SRC-TG-881). Downloaded as PDF (12.5 MB) from ONAGRI and converted with pdftotext; tables were reassembled from the column layout. Page numbers are the printed page numbers.

The yield projections isolate climate effects at constant area and constant technology. Uncertainty is plus or minus 15% around the multi-model mean. The study explicitly does not include technical progress. The rangeland and livestock figures are a citation of a 2010 World Bank report, not output of this study. No agricultural employment percentage (20% or 30%) was found in the parts searched.

Section 3.1.2, p. 105-106:

> Avec des projections basées sur la surface moyenne (2015-2017) et le même niveau de technologie que celui appliqué aujourd’hui

> La tendance des dernières décennies à l’augmentation des surfaces, notamment dans le Centre et le Nord, en particulier en irrigué, compensera au moins partiellement la diminution des rendements dans les grandes régions productrices (Sahel et Sfax) et au Sud. Mais la baisse des disponibilités en eau conventionnelle à moyen et long terme constituera une limitation supplémentaire.

> Encore une fois et plus encore que pour les céréales, ces prévisions sont sujettes aux incertitudes liées aux différents modèles climatiques disponibles et aux données partielles disponibles (pas de distinction entre irrigué et pluvial, observations limitées à une période 15 ans). Dans le cas de certains modèles climatiques, les aires d’aptitude favorables à l’olivier pourraient augmenter dans le cas du scénario RCP8.5 en 2100

English gloss: average production in 2010-2019 was about 900,000 t of oil olives, or about 180,000 t of oil. The irrigated share is poorly known but growing with state-aided planting. The Centre and South give 77% of production. Simulated olive yields fall about 0.4%/yr (RCP4.5) and about 1%/yr (RCP8.5) to 2100. Sfax, Sidi Bouzid, Kairouan and Mahdia (over half of output) could lose 21-40% (RCP4.5) or 51-86% (RCP8.5) by 2100. Favourable suitability area shrinks by a median 14%, and up to 34%, under RCP8.5 by 2100. Production tables assume the 2015-2017 area and current technology. Area expansion, especially irrigated, would partly offset yield losses, but conventional water will decline. The study notes it does not separate irrigated from rainfed groves, has only 15 years of observations, and that some models show favourable areas increasing.
