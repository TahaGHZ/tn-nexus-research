## Extracts

Section I, page 2:

> Indeed, while the conclusions of the World Bank funded study on drinking water supply and sanitation in Tunisia were issued in 2009, its proposed reforms to the legislative framework were not implemented until 2014 with a first proposal for a new water code. The draft law has since been substantially amended, partly because of the UGTT's opposition to a number of articles. In May 2019, 10 years after the first initiative, and after the recent agreement between the UGTT and the Ministry of Agriculture, the final version of the water code has been submitted to the Ministerial Council.

Section II, page 3:

> Thus, since the 1970s, water resources have been systematically mobilized. This supply management policy on which the water code of 1975 is based, has led to a high mobilization rate, which reached 98% of mobilizable water resources in 2015. In addition, this major water mobilization effort has led to the centralization of state water management, mainly led by the Ministry of Agriculture, Hydraulic Resources and Fisheries.

Section II, page 3:

> We can trace the efforts to revise the legislative framework to the 1990s, when a shift in approaches was adopted from a supply management policy to a demand management policy, which included water saving programs, participatory water management and a water pricing more correlated to operating costs.

Section II, page 4:

> But the directives proposed by the WB for Tunisia, such as the creation of a regulatory body and institutional reform, require a real legislative overhaul, and the multiplication of legislative texts has only complicated the governance of Tunisia's water supply.

Section III, page 4:

> The UGTT opposed a number of articles, which they considered harmful to the public good and believed would pave the way to the privatization of the water sector.

> In 2009, the Ministry of Agriculture's National Centre for Agricultural Studies started a study on water management, leading to the implementation of the first version of the new water code in 2012.

Recommendations, page 1:

> Give a more important role to the water council, which has been barely active since its creation.

## Notes

- Opened via: curl+pdftotext; saw: full text.
- The brief is internally inconsistent on the first draft: "a first proposal for a new water code" in 2014 (page 2) and "the first version of the new water code in 2012" (page 4). Both dates are recorded.
- The 98% mobilisation figure is attributed in the brief to Elloumi (2018), p. 34. It was not checked against a ministry series.
- The OTE is an advocacy-oriented NGO and openly opposes PPP clauses in the draft; its account of the drafting history is factual but its policy views are not neutral.
