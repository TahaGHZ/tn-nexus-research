## Extracts

Abstract, read through the Europe PMC record (PMID 27432725); the full text was not opened.

> "The increasing use of Sustainable Urban Drainage Systems (SUDS) for stormwater management raises some concerns about the fate of ubiquitous runoff micropollutants in soils and their potential threat to groundwater."

> "most investigations undoubtedly evidenced a significant accumulation of metals and/or hydrocarbons in SUDS soils, which in the majority of the cases appears to be restricted to the upper 10 to 30cm. These results may suggest that SUDS exhibit an interesting potential for pollution control, but antinomic observations have also been made in several specific cases, and the inter-site concentration variability is still difficult to appraise."

English gloss: infiltration systems accumulate metals and hydrocarbons, mostly in the top 10-30 cm of soil. This suggests good pollution control, but contrary cases exist and site-to-site variability is hard to predict.

## Notes

A review of mostly European and North American studies in temperate climates; no Tunisian or arid-climate case. It concerns soil-based devices (swales, basins, filter media). It does not assess buried plastic tanks that discharge directly into the soil below the surface layer, where the surface soil does not filter the water first; that is an inference in SOL-TG-400, not a finding of this review.
