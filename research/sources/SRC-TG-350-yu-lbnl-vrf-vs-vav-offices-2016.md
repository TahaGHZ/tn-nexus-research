## Extracts

Abstract:

> "The sub-metering data, normalized by climate and operating hours, show that VRF systems consumed much less air conditioning energy by up to 70% than VAV systems. This is mainly due to the different operation modes of both system types leading to much fewer operating hours of the VRF systems."

> "Simulated results show the VRF operation mode leads to much less cooling loads than the VAV operation mode, by 42% in Hong Kong and 53% in Qingdao. The VRF systems operated in the part-time-part-space mode enabling occupants to turn on air-conditioning only when needed and when spaces were occupied, while the VAV systems operated in the full-time-full-space mode limiting occupants' control of operation."

Section 1 (literature review, p. 2):

> "The actual savings from VRF systems would vary depending on several factors including climate, operation conditions, and control strategies"

Conclusion section:

> "VRF systems are more suitable for buildings in part-time-part-space operation mode with high flexibility when the refrigerant pipework can be installed."

## Notes

Five office buildings in China, measured by sub-metering, plus a simulation. The comparison
is VRF against central VAV systems, not against efficient inverter splits. The authors
attribute most of the saving to how the systems are operated (rooms cooled only when
occupied), not to equipment efficiency. Results are for Chinese climates and office use.
