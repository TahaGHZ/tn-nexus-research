> Tunisia's power utility company, Société Tunisienne de l'Electricité et du Gaz (STEG), estimated in 2013 that already 84% of Tunisia's peak power demand was due to ACs alone (STEG 2013). PDF p. 23-24, section 5.1.3 "Peak load reduction calculation":

> Figure 3 illustrates the daily load curve on a typical day in summer and winter in Tunisia, in 2020 (STEG 2020). The peak occurs during summer time, when temperatures are high and AC cooling is used to maintain thermal comfort in residential and commercial buildings.

> Figure 1 shows that peak demand occurs between 10 am and 6 pm, roughly the hottest hours of the day. The energy use analysis shows that ACs are used 5.5 hours a day during summer time. No time-of-use data are available [...]

> Because these peak hours coincide with business hours, it is assumed that all ACs in the commercial sector (40% of ACs) coincide with the peak load. In the residential sector (60% of ACs), it is assumed that 50% of AC use occurs during the day with 50% in the evening and at night. Accordingly, a 70% coincidence factor is calculated between AC use and peak demand.

## Notes

The BAU forecast is a stock-turnover model built on STEG survey ownership trends, with fixed
market shares of efficiency levels and no climate term. It includes heating by reversible units
and commercial ACs, so it is not comparable with STEG's residential cooling-only figures (757 GWh
in 2019, SRC-TG-250). Figure 4 values are read from the chart, not stated in text. The 84% peak
share attributed to STEG 2013 is not explained (it may mean the summer increment, not total
peak); it conflicts with STEG's 44% for 2021 (SRC-TG-250). The report's own peak history
("2,600 MW to 4,300 MW" between 2012 and 2019, p. 23) does not match the ONEM series and is not
used. The commercial share of ACs is 40% here but 30% in the companion market study (SRC-TG-250).
