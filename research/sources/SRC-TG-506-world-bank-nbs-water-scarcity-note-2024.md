## Extracts

Executive Summary, PDF page 3:

> Tunisia is among the 25 countries experiencing "extremely high" water stress (World Resources Institute 2023), with the country's water availability projected to decrease from 357 cubic meters (m3) per capita in 2020 to 268 m3 per capita by 2050 (MARHP 2022). Both values are well below the absolute water scarcity threshold of 500 m3 per capita (FAO et al. 2015).

> Climate change is expected to further reduce water resources, intensify erosion, and worsen land degradation due to a decrease in rainfall, higher temperatures, and greater evapotranspiration rates (Abouabdillah et al. 2014). Tunisia's heavy reliance on groundwater compounds these environmental challenges.

Siliana watershed case study, "Surface water flow (quick flow)", PDF page 5:

> Projections indicate that, by 2050, surface water would increase by 4.11 percent under SSP1 and decrease by 24.5 percent under SSP2 and 27.52 percent under SSP3.

## Notes

- Opened via: curl+pdftotext; saw: full text (56 pages, English followed by French and Arabic versions).
- The 357→268 m3 per-capita figure is a secondary citation of a MARHP 2022 document that was not opened. The note does not say whether the figure counts conventional resources only or includes non-conventional water.
- The Siliana projections cover a single watershed in the north-west (Siliana governorate). They are not national. On its methods page, the note says it used TerrSet's Land Change Modeler for land-use change and "InVEST®, a suite of software tools developed by the Natural Capital Project" for ecosystem services.
- The WRI 2023 "extremely high" water-stress ranking is cited secondarily; the WRI Aqueduct dataset itself was not opened.
