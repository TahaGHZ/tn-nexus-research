## Extracts

Section on desalinated water (report dated April 2021; page numbers not printed in the text extraction):

> The specific energy consumption per cubic metre of seawater is estimated to be 3 kWh/m3 and desalination in Tunisia is mainly powered by fossil energy sources. Renewable energy is only used in the water desalination station at El Ketf port, Ben Guerdane (governorate of Medenine). It was installed in 2019 and has solar panels with a capacity of 212 kW to power a plant with a capacity of 50 m3 per day, expandable up to 100 m3.

> Sfax: A seawater desalination station is being installed in Sfax. This station has a production capacity of 100,000 m3/day expandable to 200,000 m3/day. The cost of desalination for a cubic metre of seawater is around 3 dinars. Energy accounts for 40% of the cost, depreciation 40%, and maintenance 10%, with other costs accounting for the [rest]

> For water supply to be green and sustainable, desalination should be powered by renewable energy, at least on a balance sheet basis (e.g., through bilateral PPAs). To date, however, only a limited number of desalination plants use renewable energy [...]. This could be an obstacle for the implementation of renewable desalination, as Tunisia (in common with other countries) lacks experience in this area.

> Djerba seawater desalination station (12.4 million m3 in 2019), a first of its kind in Tunisia, came into [operation ...]

English gloss: Seawater desalination uses about 3 kWh/m3 and is mostly fossil-powered; the only renewable-powered unit named is a 212 kW solar plant for a 50 m3/day station at Ben Guerdane. Seawater desalination costs about 3 dinars/m3, 40% of it energy. Djerba produced 12.4 million m3 in 2019.
