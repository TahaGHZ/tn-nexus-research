## Extracts

History section:

> With the advent of the independence, Bourguiba's socialist government nationalised the land (1964) (Ibid) and pursued a modernist model of intensive agriculture.

> The plantation was managed by the Société Tunisienne des Industries Laitières (led by two entrepreneurs close to Ben Ali), that went bankrupt in 2002. At the beginning of 2011, it was managed by a societé de mise en [valeur et de développement agricole]

> On January 14th, Ben Ali fled the country. The first thing that the Committee did, in agreement with the local community, was to establish the Association pour la Protection de l'Oasis de Jemna, with the aim to manage the agricultural production in a collective way and for the benefit of the entire community

2016-2017 conflict:

> in 2016 the Ministry of the State Properties and of Land Affairs sent inspectors to Jemna to analyse the situation, and then notified to the APOJ to stop the exploitation of the oasis.

> The Ministry reacted by asking the Central Bank to freeze the bank accounts of the Association and of the purchasers or the dates.

> they could continue to carry on the productive activities, but they had to set up an SMVDA, with participation of the state with 49% of the shares, and the remaining 51% owned by the Association. The property of the land was still not under discussion. The APOJ was not convinced, and it refused the proposal

> in September 2017, they reached an agreement to make a transition toward a different organisational form of exploitation which can fulfil the State's demand for legality, while preserving the experience of SSE created by the Association.

> The agreement, that was still in force when I collected the interviews in early 2020, was expected to terminate in the moment of the release of the SSE law and of the emendation of 1984 Law on UCPA.

Conclusion section:

> The agreement still does not question the ownership of the land itself, that remains property of the State.

## Notes

Socio-legal case study in an open-access university journal; by the author's own statement it relies mainly on journalistic sources plus two semi-structured interviews, so treat factual details as medium reliability. Plantation area and palm counts circulating in the press (about 185 ha) were not found in this text and are not used. The association's founding date conflicts with Gana and Taleb 2019 (SRC-TG-786: March 2012).
