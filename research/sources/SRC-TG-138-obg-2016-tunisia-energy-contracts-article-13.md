## Extracts

Article text:

> requiring parliamentary approval for energy contracts may have an impact on the sector's overall investment attractiveness, increasing scope for politicisation.

> Tunisia imported an estimated 51% of its gas from Algeria in 2014 – that number rose to 54% by 2015, a year in which only three wells were drilled by international oil companies. Domestic oil production has dropped around 10% per year and, according to Tunisian press reports, some upstream companies – such as the UK's Enquest – are leaving the country due a range of issues, from work stoppages to the non-renewal of drilling permits.

> In 2014 the energy committee refused renewal of all hydrocarbons permits, emphasising that Article 13 and its attached laws and by-laws must be completed first in order to proceed in an ethical and legal manner.

English gloss (en source): parliamentary approval may reduce investment attractiveness; only
three wells were drilled by international oil companies in 2015; some operators were leaving
over work stoppages and non-renewal of permits; in 2014 the energy committee refused to renew
all hydrocarbon permits until Article 13's implementing laws were in place.
