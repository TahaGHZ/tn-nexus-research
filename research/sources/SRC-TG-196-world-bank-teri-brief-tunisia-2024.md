## Extracts

Web brief dated January 25, 2024 (no page numbers):

> The GoT plans to reach 35% of renewable energy in the electricity system capacity by 2030, against 3% currently.

> Renewable energy is then expected to cover 50% of the electricity needs by 2035, and 100% of all electricity needs by 2050.

English gloss: the government plans 35% renewables in electricity system capacity by 2030,
against 3% "currently", 50% of electricity needs by 2035 and 100% by 2050.

> The first major photovoltaic concession was granted in 2021, while the technical support in the form of an online bidding tool, review of bidding documents, and improved tendering manuals have helped to improve private sector confidence and responded to their concerns.

> Under the concession regime, the World Bank is helping to enhance the bidding documents for the call for proposals for 1,700 MW of solar and wind energy in 2022.

> The scale-up of renewable energy in Tunisia will depend on the availability of financing and the existence of profitable opportunities to attract the necessary private investments.

English gloss: the first large PV concession was granted in 2021; a 1,700 MW solar and wind call
for proposals followed in 2022; scale-up depends on financing and profitable opportunities.

> High energy costs have also contributed to the growing financial deficits of the state-owned enterprises (SOEs) working in the energy sector.
> Consequently, these effects make it more difficult to attract private investment to the energy sector, particularly for renewable energy.

English gloss: the growing deficits of energy-sector state enterprises make private investment,
especially in renewables, harder to attract.

## Notes

Quotes checked against the raw HTML of the page (tags stripped). The brief states the 2030 target as a share of system
capacity, not of generation; other sources state it as a share of generation (SRC-TG-192,
SRC-TG-103) or of the "energy mix" (SRC-TG-105). Its "3% currently" is not dated and does not
match IRENA's 15% of capacity in 2024 (SRC-TG-190) or ONEM's 8% of the STEG + IPP fleet in 2025
(SRC-TG-193); it may be an old generation share.
