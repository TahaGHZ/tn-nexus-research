## Extracts

p. 5, foreword:

> [...] with a 30/30 target to reduce primary energy demand by 30% in 2030 compared to the trend scenario; and renewable energy to 30% of the electricity production by 2030, in comparison to the installed 373 MW renewable energy in 2019.

English gloss: the "30/30" target is a 30% cut in primary energy demand against trend and 30%
renewables in electricity production by 2030. Installed renewables were 373 MW in 2019.

p. 23, 2.2:

> The monopoly on electricity generation, transmission and distribution from 1962 to 1996 was held by the Tunisian Company of Electricity and Gas (STEG – Société Tunisienne d'Electricité et du Gaz).

> Law No. 96-27 (April 1996) authorises the private generation of electricity through concessions provided by state authorities. It does not allow, however, unsolicited private power production (either from conventional or renewable sources). Only one concession agreement has been granted, authorising the creation and operation of Tunisia's first IPP (Carthage Power Company in Radès, 471 megawatts (MW)). This power station began operation in 2002 under a 20-year power purchase agreement (PPA).

English gloss: STEG held the generation, transmission and distribution monopoly from 1962 to 1996.
Law 96-27 (April 1996) allowed private generation by concession, but not unsolicited private
projects. Only one concession was granted, for the 471 MW Carthage Power Company plant at Radès,
which started in 2002 under a 20-year PPA.

p. 23:

> ANME, a public institution under the Ministry of Energy, Mines and the Energy Transition, became responsible as of 1985 for executing the government's policies for promoting energy efficiency measures and the deployment of renewable energy.

> Law No. 2015-12 sets the framework for renewable energy proliferation [...] Law No. 2015-12, however, was amended by Law 2019-47, dated 29 May 2019, with adjustments to allow for corporate PPAs.

English gloss: ANME has carried out energy efficiency and renewable energy policy since 1985.
Law 2015-12 set the renewable energy framework and was amended by Law 2019-47 (29 May 2019)
to allow corporate PPAs.

English gloss: Decree 96-1125 of 20 June 1996 sets the conditions and procedures for granting
electricity generation concessions.

English gloss: at the end of 2018 installed capacity was 6,147 MW, 74.6% owned by STEG. Natural

p. 13, executive summary:

> The acquisition and implementation of grid transmission infrastructure by the Tunisian Company of Electricity and Gas (STEG) is subject to long-running public procurement procedures, resulting in a time lag between renewable energy plant completion dates and connection to the grid to send out the electricity produced.

> Private-sector developers have faced difficulties understanding the procedures to obtain authorisation for projects, given the considerable number of ministries and public institutions involved in renewable energy projects.

English gloss: STEG's grid investments go through long public procurement, so plants can be
finished before they can be connected. Developers struggle with authorisation procedures
involving many ministries and public bodies.

p. 23, 2.2:

> Law No. 2015-12, however, was amended by Law 2019-47, dated 29 May 2019, with adjustments to allow for corporate PPAs. This provides the right for companies adopting renewables for self-production to sell electricity to other consumers or companies with subscribed power greater than the threshold set by MEMTE electricity 30% and use the national grid network to transport electricity.

English gloss: Law 2019-47 of 29 May 2019 amended Law 2015-12 to allow corporate PPAs:
self-producers may sell to other consumers above a threshold and use the national grid.

p. 33, 3.2:

> Tunisia's total installed renewable power generating capacity had reached approximately 352 MW by the end of 2019, with wind energy at 245 MW, hydropower at 66 MW) and PV at 62 MW (IRENA, 2020b).

> Tunisia's wind generation development began in 2001, when STEG brought online a wind farm at the Sidi Daoud site in the northeast, with an initial capacity of 10 MW and later expanding to 55 MW in 2008.

> STEG launched two other wind farms in 2012 at the sites of Metline and Kchabta (Bizerte region). Together they account for a total of 143 wind turbines with a total capacity of 190 MW. The total electricity production of the three wind farms installed by STEG in 2017 reached 449 GWh, and peak production was recorded in 2014 at 507 GWh, as shown in Figure 17.

English gloss: renewable capacity was about 352 MW at the end of 2019 (wind 245, hydro 66,
PV 62). STEG's wind programme started with Sidi Daoud in 2001 (10 MW, 55 MW by 2008), followed
by Metline and Kchabta in 2012 (143 turbines, 190 MW). The three STEG wind farms produced
449 GWh in 2017; the peak was 507 GWh in 2014.

p. 36:

> Grid-connected solar PV systems are recent in Tunisia. Their installation started upon the issuance of Law No. 2009-7, which authorises self-production of electricity from renewable energy sources. Gridconnected solar PV has mainly been implemented through the Prosol électrique programme, initiated in May 2010 by ANME and STEG.

> By the end of 2019, 3 644 solar PV systems were connected to the LV grid. The total PV capacity installed is evaluated at around 11 298 kWp, 90% of which were installed in the residential sector.

English gloss: grid-connected PV began after Law 2009-7 allowed renewable self-production,
mainly through Prosol Elec, started in May 2010 by ANME and STEG. The report gives 3,644 LV
systems and about 11.3 MWp at the end of 2019.

p. 46, 4.2:

> Following lengthy discussions regarding the guaranteed purchase tariffs and the modalities of their application and revision, however, the decision was taken to grant authorisations only based on calls for projects announced by MIEM and within the limits of the objectives set by the annual notice.

English gloss: after long debate on guaranteed tariffs, authorisations were granted only through
ministry calls for projects within an annual quota, instead of a posted feed-in tariff.

p. 48:

> MIEM launched pre-qualification tenders on 23 May 2018 for the realisation by concession (build, own, operate) of five solar PV plants, totalling a capacity of 500 MW, and four wind farms with a total capacity of 500 MW.

p. 49:

> Despite the delay in the bidding process, five bids were received for the 500 MW solar tender. The proposed bids were all within the range of USD 0.0244/kWh to USD 0.0286/kWh (MEMTE, 2019a).

> For renewable projects under the Authorisation scheme, MISME proceeded in May 2017 to launch the first call for projects for the development of 210 MW from solar PV and wind.

English gloss: concession pre-qualification for 500 MW PV and 500 MW wind was launched on 23 May
2018. Five bids were received for the 500 MW solar tender, at 2.44-2.86 US cents/kWh. The first
authorisation call (May 2017) was for 210 MW of PV and wind.

p. 52:

> The preparatory phase of this programme did not include all the relevant stakeholders and did not consider the various aspects relating to the implementation of energy planning, such as access to land.

p. 54-55:

> With respect to the Authorisation scheme, one of the main concerns for private developers is the absence of standardised contractual documents for bid submissions, thus resulting in discrepancies and lack of transparency, eventually reducing the bankability of PPAs. In general, the absence of any guarantee scheme regarding the off-taker risks limits the attractiveness of PPAs.
> The results of the first call for projects of May 2017 highlighted the constraints in project bankability, where most private sector offers were conditional upon the change in the structure of the PPA.
> Owing to this problem, no wind project has been selected at this round of call for projects.

English gloss: the 2017-2022 programme was planned without all stakeholders and ignored issues
such as land access. Developers lacked standard bid documents, and there was no guarantee
against off-taker (STEG) risk, which reduced PPA bankability. Most offers in the May 2017 call
were conditional on changing the PPA, and no wind project was selected in that round.

## Notes

Prepared with ANME and the ministry. Some capacity data come from interviews with ANME and
STEG. The table's totals (5,260 / 5,315 / 6,147 MW) do not equal the sum of the listed rows for
2018. The 2018 rows (STEG 4,587 + rooftop 62 + IPP 498) sum to 5,147 MW. The gas units (steam
660 + combined cycle 1,612 + open cycle 2,004 + IPP 498) sum to 4,774 MW, about 93%, not the
94.5% stated in the text. This is our arithmetic; the report does not explain the gap. Page numbers are printed page numbers, which match the PDF.
