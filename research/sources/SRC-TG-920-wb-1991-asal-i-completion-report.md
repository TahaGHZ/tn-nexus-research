## Extracts

Para. 1.05 (situation before the 1986 programme):

> Within the agricultural sector itself, there were a number of constraints to development. Negative protection rates and worsening terms of trade with other sectors, particularly industry, made agriculture and related support services unattractive for new private direct investment. [...] Support services were controlled to a large degree by the Government. These operations were often inefficient, wasting resources for the economy at large, and frequently resulting in interruptions of supply to farmers.

Para. 6.11 (prices and incentives framework):

> At the time the loan was developed, prices of both agricultural outputs and inputs (fertilizers, improved seed, herbicides and livestock feed) were below world prices and fixed on the basis of a cost-plus methodology. The medium-term program called for linkage with world market prices to be achieved by the end of the medium-term program (1991)

Para. 6.12:

> Output prices for durum wheat, bread wheat and barley were raised by 12.6%, 3.5% and 6.3% in real terms respectively for the 1987 crop year and by 8.3%, 6.7% and 11.4% in real terms respectively for the 1988 crop year. Nominal protection coefficients rose from .89, .98 and .82 for durum, bread wheat and barley respectively between 1980 and 1985 to 1.04, 1.03 and .90 in 1988

Para. 6.13:

> Nominal protection coefficients comparing producer prices to actual world prices were extremely high due to plummeting values for basic grains on the world markets (NPCs were 1.37, 1.27 and 1.29 for durum, bread wheat and barley respectively). [...] At the time, the Tunisians were more concerned about creating incentives for agricultural production than about the budgetary costs of further price increases.

Para. 6.14:

> It was not foreseen at the time that a situation of implicit taxation of the agricultural sector could become one of over-protection.

Para. 6.15:

> Increases in input prices were suspended for the 1989 season due to the severe drought in 1988 [...] Under ASAL-II the Government expects to resume input price increases for the 1990 season and to gradually eliminate all of the input subsidies by 1992.

## Notes

Bank self-evaluation of its own loan (ASAL I, approved 1986). The NPCs in para. 6.12 compare producer prices with the Bank's moving-average formula price; those in para. 6.13 compare with actual world prices of late 1987. The two series answer different questions and must not be mixed. The text file is OCR from a scanned original; obvious OCR errors (e.g. "Withie", "resoucces") are corrected above. Cereals-only NPCs; no aggregate PSE/NRA for all agriculture is given.
