## Extracts

Abstract:

> "The analysis shows that the VRF system uses 17% and 73% less energy than the rooftop system in cooling and heating seasons, respectively."

Section 4 (cooling season data analysis):

> "The analysis shows that the VRF system uses 10.5% less energy than the baseline RTU system. [...] If this standby energy use is included for the unoccupied hours, the VRF energy savings increase to 17.3% for the same period."

Section 5 (limitations):

> "The discharge air temperature setpoint for the baseline RTU system is fixed to 14 °C even during heating season, which results in excessive reheating energy use."

Section 1 (introduction):

> "several studies show that there are concerns for the application of VRF systems in the United States, including (1) lack of awareness of energy-efficiency advantages, (2) higher initial cost, and (3) code compliance issues"

## Notes

A two-system comparison in one test building in the United States, switching weekly between
a rooftop unit with terminal reheat and a VRF system. The baseline is not an efficient
inverter split. The paper is internally inconsistent on the cooling saving: 17% in the
abstract, 10.5% (17.3% with standby) in the text, and 20% in the conclusion. One of the
authors works for the VRF's manufacturer (Samsung), a conflict of interest. The paper itself
lists limits of its baseline and says further analysis was planned.
