## Extracts

Confidential: shared by the author with the team. Do not publish without his agreement.

> "Two real Tunisian problems, each matched to a system of STC products: VRV air conditioning to cut energy use and peak demand, and ACO StormBrixx to hold storm water on site." (p. 1, Intent)

> "STC (Société Tunisienne de Chauffage) is the exclusive Tunisian distributor of Chaffoteaux, Ariston, Hisense HVAC, Daikin, ACO and Delabie" (p. 1)

> "Summer 2026 peak demand vs STEG's maximum production capacity | ~5,000 MW vs ~4,630 MW | Webdo" and "Share of the summer peak from residential AC (ANME/STEG 2024 study) | ~50% | Webdo" (p. 1, table; figures attributed to press)

> "the VRV outdoor unit can cap its power at roughly 40–80% of nominal when an external adaptor (DTA104A61/62) receives a signal (Daikin VRV IV installer guide). Wired to a timer for 13:00–17:00, it cuts the building's draw exactly when the grid is short." (p. 2)

> "Example with assumed values: 60 kW of electrical input capped at d = 0.7 removes 18 kW from the grid during every peak hour." (p. 2)

> "a patented plastic geocellular system for storm-water attenuation and infiltration (ACO UK). The SD range has a void ratio of 97%" (p. 3)

> "Most Tunisian cities have no separate storm-water network; rain and sewage share the same pipes" (p. 3, attributed to AllAfrica)

> "Example with assumed values: 2,000 m² of asphalt × 0.9 × 0.05 m = 90 m³ of water, so about 93 m³ of StormBrixx SD." (p. 4)

> "a pump (outside STC) feeds garden irrigation in summer. Option B for sandy soil: unlined tank for infiltration and groundwater recharge." (p. 4)

## Notes

A short use-case note by an engineer at STC describing two existing solutions; not an
independent assessment. Its problem figures are taken from press articles (tier C),
and its savings examples use assumed values, not measured results. Product capabilities
are attributed to manufacturer documents (Daikin, ACO). No installed Tunisian case or
measured saving is given.
