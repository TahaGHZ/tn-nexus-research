Printed p. 42, section 5.5 "Tunisia", Demand:

> In 2027, forecasted monthly consumption ranges from 1,668 GWh to 2,699 GWh, while monthly peak demand varies from 3.4 GW to 6.8 GW. Monthly demand refers to the average values across all 36 climatic years analysed, whereas peak hourly demand corresponds to the monthly maximum for all 36 climatic years.

> In 2030, Tunisia monthly demand is projected to range from approximately 1,863 GWh to 3,060 GWh, with monthly peak demand varying from 3.9 GW to 7.7 GW. The increase in peak load from 2027 to 2030 is 0.9 GW, corresponding to approximately 13.2%. Maximum electricity needs are expected in summer (July–August) due to high temperatures and cooling consumption. Because of this monthly load distribution, maintenance of thermal power plants is not allowed during summer (June–August).

English gloss: the peak used is the highest hourly value across 36 simulated climate years, so
it is an extreme-weather peak and not a typical year. Peak demand is 6.8 GW in 2027 and 7.7 GW in
2030, up 0.9 GW (13.2%). The maximum comes in July-August because of heat and cooling demand.

Printed p. 43-44:

> In 2027, the results show frequent periods where the margin approaches zero, particularly during July and August, but also scattered across other months. This indicates a tight adequacy situation, with limited reserve available to cover unexpected outages or demand spikes.

## Notes

Probabilistic Monte Carlo adequacy model (Antares) with TSO-submitted data and proxies. The peaks
are maxima over 36 climate years, so they are not comparable with ONEM's observed annual peak.
The 2030 capacity assumes a large build-out (NGIC 10,520 MW, RES 46.9% of capacity), which goes
well beyond the capacity of about 6 GW installed at the end of 2023 (SRC-TG-105). The text layer
of Tables 4 and 5 is misaligned and is not quoted. The 2030 LOLE figures are internally
inconsistent, as shown above. Two of the three statements (Figure 3 and Figure 39) say 3 h when
isolated and about 0 h when interconnected, which suggests the section 5.5 sentence has the labels
swapped. The contradiction is still unresolved: Table 12 (p. 24) shows zero adequacy exchanges on
every Tunisian link in 2030, which fits neither reading cleanly (with 3 h of isolated loss of load,
some exchange would be expected in interconnected mode). The source does not correct it.
In the p. 25 sentence the text layer reads "600 W" (a dropped glyph); "[M]" is restored from Table 12, which gives 600 MW for IT00-TN00.
