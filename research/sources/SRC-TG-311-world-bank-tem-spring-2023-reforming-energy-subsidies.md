## Extracts

Part A, section 3, printed p. 6 (PDF p. 26):

> At the end of 2021, the Tunisian Company of Electricity and Gas (STEG) in charge of importing gas for electricity production and the Tunisian Company of Refining Industry (STIR) in charge of importing gasoline, had accumulated 7.9 billion dinars of debts with foreign banks and 4.3 billion dinars of debts with suppliers. However, strategy appears to have reached its limits as their rising debt constrains SOEs' ability to increase further their indebtedness.

> With dwindling reserves and increasing SOE debt, the rising CAD has translated in sustained shortages of basic products. This context has made it difficult to secure sufficient foreign exchange to cover the rising import bill. As a result, the economy experienced sustained shortages of many basic products since March 2022, including fuels (October 2022), cereals (March 2022), sugar, dairy products (November 2022), coffee (January 2023) and pharmaceutical products.

> As a result, reserves declined to TND 22 billion TND covering 95 days of imports by mid-February 2023, from TND 23.4 billion, covering 133 days of imports in January 2022.

Part B, printed p. 15 (PDF p. 35):

> In the case of fuels (diesel and petrol), the subsidy is absorbed by the state-owned enterprise (SOE) STIR (Société Tunisienne des Industries de Raffinage), which holds the monopoly of import (of crude and refined oil), refining and wholesale markets. About 90 percent of Tunisia's needs are satisfied directly or indirectly from international markets, where STIR sources these products at international market prices.

Footnote 4, same page:

> 68 percent of fuel consumed in Tunisia in 2022 was imported directly, 22 percent was imported as crude oil and then refined domestically by STIR, and 10 percent was produced (by ETAP) and refined (by STIR) domestically.

English gloss: at end-2021 STEG and STIR together owed TND 7.9 billion to foreign banks and TND
4.3 billion to suppliers, and their debt limited further borrowing. Foreign-exchange constraints
led to shortages of many basic products from March 2022, including fuels from October 2022. STIR
holds the import, refining and wholesale monopoly and absorbs the fuel subsidy; about 90% of needs
are met directly or indirectly from international markets. In 2022,
68% of fuel consumed was imported as product, 22% was refined from imported crude and 10% was
refined from domestic crude.

## Notes

Text extracted with pdftotext; the page layout is two-column, so sentences were reassembled in
reading order. The 22% + 10% share refined by
STIR in 2022 matches ONEM's 2022 STIR coverage rate of 32% (all production, SRC-TG-220). The
STEG and STIR debt figures are combined; the report does not split them. The report does not
quantify how long or how severe the October 2022 fuel shortages were.
