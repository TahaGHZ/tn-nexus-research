## Extracts

Section 1.2.2:

> In 2017, the volume of wastewater treated by the 105 treatment plants analysed during the "Water REUSE 2050" study (there are 125 plants across the country) was 292 million m3, which represents 10% of annual water requirements. The volume reused in the same year was 22 million m3, representing a direct national reuse rate of just 8%.

Section 1.2.3:

> the regulations governing this subsector are currently incomplete and not based on a risk analysis.

> The conditions for using treated wastewater are set out in Decree No. 89-1047 of 28 July 1989, as amended by Decree No. 93-2447 of 13 December 1993. Although it applies solely to agricultural reuse, the decree provides that treated wastewater can only be used for agricultural purposes if authorised by the Minister of Agriculture, after the agreement of the Ministers of the Environment and Public Health.

Section 1.2.6:

> the governance framework is relatively complete. It covers all functions necessary for managing treated wastewater reuse, but its operationalisation remains problematic (centralisation of project authorisation, ownership of projects by the various players, lack of capacity in the teams responsible for monitoring, etc.).

Section 1.3.2:

> The stakeholders we met during the REUSE 2050 surveys raised this problem, and some pointed out that water quality is sometimes the main hindrance to the development of treated wastewater reuse projects.

Section 1.3.3:

> In 2012, the Bank supported ONAS in financing the Treated Wastewater Quality Improvement Project (PAQEE) [...] This project enabled some thirty WWTPs to be rehabilitated, [...] and providing the potential to irrigate 12,500 hectares of farmland (benefiting 4,000 farmers) and green spaces.

Section 2.2.3:

> Currently, the direct reuse presents less than 10% of its treated wastewater (TWW) and discharges continue to be discharged into the environment

Section 3.1.1:

> In October 2022, a tariff increase of around 30% was decided, followed by further forecast increases of 11% in 2023-2024-2025 and 6% from 2027 to 2031.

Section 3.2.2 (environmental and social risks):

> poisoning/illness among farmers and final consumers of agricultural products following the use of purified water produced for irrigation, as well as the spreading of sludge on agricultural land

Climate section (pathogens):

> Pathogen elimination, the most important parameter in irrigation, has rarely been considered as a goal.

Section 2.5.1 and results framework: about 3,357 hectares of farmland to be irrigated from the 18 to 19 plants; 13 solar PV systems at tertiary treatment, 6,000 kWc; 1,887 tCO2eq/year avoided; TWW volume produced 18 million m3/yr to 32 million m3/yr (indicator 3.1); loan EUR 81.9 million of a total project cost of EUR 81.90 million (UA 66.00 million).
