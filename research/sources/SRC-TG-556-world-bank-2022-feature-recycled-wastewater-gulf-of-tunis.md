## Extracts

> 96% of a batch of samples of seawater collected at Raoued in 2020 complied with levels of bacteria considered safe for public health; 97% for the level of detergents. These improvements were the result of channeling treated wastewater underground through pipes to a submarine outfall—the technical term for a pipeline under the sea—stretching 6 kilometers offshore before discharging its effluent 20 meters down the surface.

> A poorly maintained, 30-year-old sewage system that was in place could no longer cope with the volume of waste being generated. Open and above ground, its channels carried wastewater from the Chotrana treatment plant, just south of Raoued, to the El Khelij river, discharging it as the river hit the beach. [...] Even though it was treated, the quality of the wastewater was too poor for agriculture.

> The improvements are the result of a US$60.6 million project that was completed in 2021.

> From the basin, its flow can be regulated for irrigation, making millions of cubic meters of good quality, recycled water available to about 500 farmers in Borj Touil.

> To show the public that recycled wastewater is safe for growing plants, plots belonging to three farmers in Borj Touil have been irrigated with treated wastewater
