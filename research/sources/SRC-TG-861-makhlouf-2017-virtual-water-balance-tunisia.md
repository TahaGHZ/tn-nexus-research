## Extracts

Abstract (p. 11):

> "Our calculations show that Tunisia is a net virtual water "importer". We also surprisingly show that, even though olive production is mostly rain fed, olive oil production is still identified as the most water-demanding commodity (7 m3/kg) in Tunisia due to the need of blue water for its processing. By exporting 145.9 thousand tons of olive oil and 97.8 thousand tons of date in 2013, Tunisia has respectively lost 1079.6 and 283.6 Million m3 of virtual water. On the other hand, by importing 2146.6 thousand tons of cereals and 15.7 thousand tons of potato in the same year, Tunisia has respectively saved 2901.7 and 3.14 Million m3 of water resources."

Résumé (p. 11, French version of the abstract):

> "Nos calculs montrent que la Tunisie est un pays "importateur net " d'eau virtuelle."

English gloss: Our calculations show that Tunisia is a net importer of virtual water.

Section 4 (results, p. 14-15):

> "Unlike grain, the blue virtual water content of vegetable represents around 60% of the total virtual water content due to a couple of reasons such as the period of cultivation which is summer wherein the crop water requirement is the highest"

> "The results obtained show that, more than 80% of the virtual water exported is blue virtual water."

Section 5 (discussion, p. 16-17):

> "Results show that commodities such as olives, almond, oats and barley are among the crops with higher share of green water content compared to the share of blue water (Chouchane et al., 2013). Most of these crops are not traded (exported) and are consumed locally. Concerning virtual water trade, our results show that Tunisia is essentially exporting blue water. This is especially due to the high contribution of water intensive crops such as dates and olive oil to the agricultural export of Tunisia."

Conclusion (p. 17):

> "Finally, it was also shown that Tunisia is a net importer of virtual water. But we believe this is not intentional and is simply due to its dependency from international markets for the import of many necessary food commodities such as cereals, animal feeds, and vegetable oils."

## Notes

Based on the authors' field surveys across bioclimatic zones (single year 2013 for trade flows). The numeric values of Table 1 (virtual water content by crop, including irrigated maltaise citrus and irrigated tomato) and Table 2 could not be extracted: the PDF uses an embedded font whose glyphs do not map to text. The claim that olive oil is 7 m3/kg "due to the need of blue water for its processing" conflicts with SRC-TG-860, which puts olives at 7,100 m3/t green and 420 m3/t blue; the two studies use different methods and system boundaries. Implied blue+green content of exported dates in 2013 is about 2,900 m3/t (283.6 Mm3 / 97.8 kt), lower than the 5,600 m3/t total (5,000 blue) in SRC-TG-860.
