## Extracts

p. 2, abstract:

> Until the revolution, Tunisia had covered its energy needs thanks in particular to the gas royalty fees from the Algerian–Italian pipeline. In 2000, it became a dependant country with a growing deficit.

English gloss: until 2011 Tunisia covered its energy needs largely with royalty gas from the
Algeria-Italy pipeline. From 2000 it became a net-dependent country with a growing deficit.

p. 6:

> Two independent private producers (IPPs) produce the rest of the electricity. Carthage Power Company, the principal and the older of the two, produces and distributes electricity through its combined cycle (natural gas and diesel-fired). In 2012, the company generated 25 per cent of the electricity produced in Tunisia.

English gloss: Carthage Power Company's combined-cycle plant generated 25% of Tunisian
electricity in 2012.

p. 7, 2.1.3 "A Sector Based on Hydrocarbon in Need of Diversification":

> The choice to favour gas can be explained by economic and geographical reasons. Neighbouring Algeria is a significant hydrocarbon producer and exports its gas to the European continent through a pipeline that crosses Tunisian territory. Tunisia benefits from a portion of the gas exported (5.625 per cent) by Algeria, called "fiscal gas", in exchange for the crossing of the gas pipeline on its territory. At first, the increased capacity of the Trans-Mediterranean Pipeline has allowed the growth of the Tunisian quota and has met the growing demand for energy (Table 5). But national gas production has declined since 2010 to reach the same volume as Algerian gas imports in 2015 (IEA 2017b).

English gloss: the authors explain the choice of gas by economics and geography. Tunisia
receives 5.625% of the Algerian gas crossing its territory as "fiscal gas". The pipeline's
capacity expansion first raised Tunisia's share and met rising demand. Domestic gas production
has declined since 2010, and by 2015 it equalled Algerian gas imports.

p. 8:

> At the same time, Algerian gas exports to Europe decreased as well as the gas received by Tunisia in royalties (Krimi 2018). [...] At the end of 2015, 74 per cent of natural gas consumption was used for electricity production

p. 8, Table 5 "Evolution of fiscal gas (1,000 toe), 1990–2015" (source given: El Haddad 2016: 26):

> 1990 592 | 2000 1,210 | 2005 1,164 | 2006 1,154 | 2007 1,043 | 2008 1,148 | 2009 972 | 2010 785 | 2011 741 | 2012 924 | 2013 559 | 2014 299 | 2015 317

English gloss: Algerian gas exports to Europe fell, and so did Tunisia's royalty gas. In 2015,
74% of gas consumption went to electricity production. Royalty ("fiscal") gas was 592 ktoe in
1990, peaked at 1,210 ktoe in 2000 and fell to 317 ktoe in 2015.

## Notes

EU-funded working paper; not peer-reviewed. Most figures come from secondary sources (IEA, El
Haddad 2016, Krimi 2018, Oxford Business Group). The 5.625% royalty rate is not traced to the
treaty text, and no data year is given. Press (SRC-TG-111) reports 5.25% under the convention
renewed from 1 September 2019. The 25% CPC share for 2012 is higher than the 18-19% that SRC-TG-104 gives for
about 2018-2019. Table 5 was checked against the rendered page image. The PDF was downloaded
and its text extracted locally (WebFetch could not read it).
