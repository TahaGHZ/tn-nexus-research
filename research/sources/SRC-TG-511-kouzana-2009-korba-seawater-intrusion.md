## Extracts

Abstract, page 21:

> the uncontrolled groundwater extraction causes a modification of natural flow systems and induces seawater intrusion from the coast and causes the groundwater quality deterioration. The Korba aquifer is located in the North-East of Tunisia, where a semi-arid Mediterranean climate prevails.

> The high groundwater salinity anomaly observed in Diar El Hajjej, Garaet Sassi and Takelsa-Korba zones was explained by the presence of seawater intrusion in these areas. This hypothesis is based on high chloride concentrations, the inverse cation exchange reactions, and the lower piezometric level compared to sea level.

Résumé, page 21:

> La nappe côtière du Plio-Quaternaire de la région de Korba a été très sollicitée par les agriculteurs, ce qui a engendré, probablement, une inversion du gradient hydraulique et, par conséquent, l'avancée du biseau salé.

English gloss: Heavy pumping by farmers from the Plio-Quaternary coastal aquifer of Korba probably reversed the hydraulic gradient and caused the saltwater wedge to advance.

Section 3, page 24:

> The recent 2006 piezometric map (Fig. 3) shows piezometric depressions related to overexploitation of the aquifer with negative piezometric levels on the order of –12 m.

## Notes

- Opened via: curl+pdftotext; saw: full text.
- Field data are from June 2006 (28 samples plus vertical electrical soundings). This is a historical snapshot of one aquifer of 430 km2 (governorate of Nabeul), not a projection.
- The paper shows that intrusion at Korba is already driven by pumping (water table up to about 12 m below sea level). Sea-level rise would add to an existing human-made inversion.
