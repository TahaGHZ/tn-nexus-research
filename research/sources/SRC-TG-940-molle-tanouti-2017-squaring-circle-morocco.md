## Extracts

Abstract, first sentence (RePEc/EconPapers record, opened):

> Drip irrigation is widely promoted as a means of saving water.

Abstract, remaining content as returned by a web-search summary of the abstract (the record page was opened, but its tool returned a paraphrase of the rest, so the wording below is the search summary, not a verified quote):

> ... drip tends to be associated with higher crop density, a shift to more water-intensive crops, and the reuse of 'saved water' to expand cultivated areas, resulting in higher water consumption.

English gloss: in Morocco, drip irrigation went together with denser planting, a shift to more water-hungry crops and use of the "saved" water to enlarge irrigated area, so total water use rose. The authors criticise the Green Morocco Plan for subsidising intensification as well as conversion.
